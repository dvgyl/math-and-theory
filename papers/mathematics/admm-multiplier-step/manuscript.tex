\begin{abstract}
We study classical two-block ADMM with a fixed penalty when only the multiplier update is multiplied by a constant step $\tau$. For finite-dimensional convex problems whose proximal subproblems attain their minima and which have a saddle point, the iteration converges for every $0<\tau\le \taunew=1.886795$: both primal blocks and the multiplier converge to a saddle point, and the objective values converge to the optimum. No smoothness, strong convexity, bounded iterates or summability is assumed. Conversely, one fixed pair of compact convex polytopes in $\R^{128}$, with zero objectives, has a nonconstant periodic ADMM orbit for every $\tau\ge\tauup\approx1.8868840$. The largest step with universal convergence therefore lies in an interval of width less than $0.00009$. In dimension one, by contrast, the iteration converges for every $0<\tau<2$, and this range is exact. The convergence proof combines quadratic energies of up to thirty-two consecutive states with exact rational certificates that are affine in $\tau$. Affine-constraint versions are also proved. Lean~4 formalizes the convergence theorems for $0<\tau\le\taulo$, their certificates, and an earlier periodic orbit valid for $\tau\ge1.8868954$; the extension to $\taunew$, the present orbit and the scalar theorem rest on exact rational checks and conventional proofs.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM) for
\[
\min\{f(x)+g(z): x=z\}
\]
alternates two proximal steps with a multiplier update. Fortin and Glowinski allowed the multiplier update to be scaled by a constant $\tau$ and proved convergence for $0<\tau<(1+\sqrt5)/2$ \cite{FortinGlowinski}. Whether this golden-ratio range can be enlarged for general convex problems has been an open question; Tao and Yuan call it Glowinski's open question and answer it for quadratic objectives \cite{TaoYuan}. Gu and Yang showed that the conventional energy used in convergence proofs can increase beyond the golden ratio, so that proof technique cannot cross it \cite{GuYang}. Larger steps are known under extra assumptions, such as strong convexity \cite{MaShuLiWu}, an added proximal term \cite{Ma2020}, or summability of multiplier increments \cite{ChenSunToh}. The earlier result of this collection is an exact five-cycle at $\tau=199/100$ for compact polytopes, valid from $\tau\ge140111/70637\approx1.98353554$, which rules out universal convergence on all of $(0,2)$ \cite{FiveCycle}.

This paper brackets the universal threshold. The convergence theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:main}) covers every $0<\tau\le\taunew=1.886795$. The periodic orbit (Theorem~\ref{thm:cycle}) exists for every $\tau\ge\tauup$, where
\[
\tauup=\frac{38847831300265103262189027}{20588352022933119368900773}\approx1.8868839651174105.
\]
Hence the supremum $\tau_*$ of steps with universal convergence satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bracket}
1.886795\le\tau_*\le\tauup,\qquad \tauup-1.886795<0.00009.
\end{equation}
The exact value of $\tau_*$ is not determined. Version~1.0 of this paper had the bracket $[1.886,\,1.8868954]$; the present one is about ten times narrower.

The threshold depends on the dimension. For scalar problems the universal range is exactly $(0,2)$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:scalar}): every scalar instance converges for $0<\tau<2$, and a trivial instance fails for every $\tau\ge2$. The proof uses a coercive Lyapunov function built from the order structure of the real line rather than a quadratic certificate.

The method is not new. Convergence comes from a quadratic Lyapunov energy of several consecutive states, certified by nonnegative combinations of convex interpolation inequalities and positive-semidefinite matrices; this is the approach of automated Lyapunov analysis \cite{UpadhyayaTight,AutoLyap}. The periodic orbit uses vertex normal-cone certificates for polytopes, as in the earlier five-cycle. What is new, as far as the literature we inspected shows, is the bracket~\eqref{eq:bracket} for the unmodified iteration in the general convex class, and the exact scalar range of Theorem~\ref{thm:scalar}.

Gao et~al.\ asserted convergence of this iteration for every $1<\tau\le2$ \cite{Gao2022}. Section~\ref{sec:gao} explains why that assertion cannot hold: its displayed inequality fails on a scalar example, the iteration has a two-cycle at $\tau=2$, and Theorem~\ref{thm:cycle} contradicts it for every $\tau\ge\tauup$.

\paragraph{Version history.} Version~1.0 (30 October 2026) proved convergence for $0<\tau\le\taulo$ and a periodic orbit for $\tau\ge897744153596994489/475778439151265168\approx1.8868954$. Version~1.1 (31 October 2026) extends convergence to $0<\tau\le\taunew$, lowers the orbit threshold to $\tauup$ and adds Theorem~\ref{thm:scalar}. The statements of version~1.0 remain valid, and their Lean formalization is unchanged.

\section{Setting and results}\label{sec:results}
Let $E$ be a finite-dimensional real inner-product space. Let $C,D\subseteq E$ be convex sets and let $f,g:E\to\R$ be convex on $C$ and $D$, respectively; values outside $C$ and $D$ play no role. Equivalently, one may add the convex indicators of $C$ and $D$.

\begin{definition}
A point $p$ is a \emph{proximal point} of $f$ on $C$ at center $c$ if $p\in C$ and
\[
f(p)+\tfrac12\norm{p-c}^2\le f(y)+\tfrac12\norm{y-c}^2\qquad(y\in C).
\]
We write $p=P_f(c)$. A \emph{saddle point} is a pair $(z_*,u_*)$ with $z_*\in C\cap D$ and
\[
f(y)\ge f(z_*)-\ip{u_*}{y-z_*}\ (y\in C),\qquad g(y)\ge g(z_*)+\ip{u_*}{y-z_*}\ (y\in D).
\]
\end{definition}
Adding the two inequalities of a saddle point shows that $z_*$ minimizes $f+g$ on $C\cap D$.

\paragraph{Standing hypotheses.}
For every $c\in E$, proximal points of $f$ on $C$ and of $g$ on $D$ exist, and a saddle point exists. Convexity makes proximal points unique (Lemma~\ref{lem:prox}).

The algorithm, with penalty one in consensus form, is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:admm}
x^{k+1}=P_f(z^k-u^k),\qquad z^{k+1}=P_g(x^{k+1}+u^k),\qquad u^{k+1}=u^k+\tau(x^{k+1}-z^{k+1}).
\end{equation}
Only the last update contains $\tau$. This is the multiplier-only relaxation of Fortin and Glowinski, not the over-relaxation that replaces $x^{k+1}$ by $\alpha x^{k+1}+(1-\alpha)z^k$ in the later steps.

\begin{theorem}[Convergence]\label{thm:main}
Assume the standing hypotheses and fix $0<\tau\le\taunew$. For every initial $(z^0,u^0)\in E\times E$ there is a saddle point $(\bar z,\bar u)$ such that
\[
x^{k+1}\to\bar z,\qquad z^k\to\bar z,\qquad u^k\to\bar u,
\]
and $f(x^{k+1})\to f(\bar z)$, $g(z^{k+1})\to g(\bar z)$. In particular $f(x^{k+1})+g(z^{k+1})$ converges to the minimum of $f+g$ on $C\cap D$.
\end{theorem}

For a penalty $\rho>0$ apply Theorem~\ref{thm:main} to $f/\rho$ and $g/\rho$; the variable $u$ is then the multiplier divided by $\rho$. When $f$ and $g$ are proper, closed and convex with effective domains $C$ and $D$, the proximal points exist for every center by the standard theory, so the standing hypotheses reduce to existence of a saddle point. The formal statement keeps attainment as an explicit hypothesis.

\begin{theorem}[Affine constraints]\label{thm:affine}
Let $U,V,E$ be finite-dimensional real inner-product spaces, let $A:U\to E$ and $B:V\to E$ be affine maps of arbitrary rank, let $C\subseteq U$ and $D\subseteq V$ be convex, and let $f$ and $g$ be convex on $C$ and $D$. Fix $\rho>0$ and $0<\tau\le\taunew$. Assume that for every center the subproblems
\[
\min_{x\in C} f(x)+\tfrac\rho2\norm{A(x)-c}^2,\qquad \min_{y\in D} g(y)+\tfrac\rho2\norm{B(y)-c}^2
\]
attain their minima, and that the problem $\min\{f(x)+g(y):x\in C,\ y\in D,\ A(x)=B(y)\}$ has a saddle point. Let $(x_n,y_n,u_n)$ be any sequence produced by
\[
x_{n+1}\in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{x\in C} f(x)+\tfrac\rho2\norm{A(x)-B(y_n)+u_n}^2,\quad
y_{n+1}\in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{y\in D} g(y)+\tfrac\rho2\norm{A(x_{n+1})-B(y)+u_n}^2,
\]
and $u_{n+1}=u_n+\tau(A(x_{n+1})-B(y_{n+1}))$, with arbitrary choices among minimizers. Then $A(x_n)$, $B(y_n)$, $\rho u_n$ and $f(x_n)+g(y_n)$ converge to the corresponding values at a saddle point of the original problem, and that limit value is the optimum. If $A$ and $B$ have continuous left inverses, then $x_n$ and $y_n$ also converge.
\end{theorem}
The usual form $A_0x+B_0y=b$ is the case $A=A_0$, $B(y)=b-B_0y$. Without injectivity the raw iterates need not converge: if $f$ does not depend on a coordinate of $x$ that $A$ ignores, an arbitrary choice of minimizers can move that coordinate at will.

\begin{theorem}[Periodic orbit]\label{thm:cycle}
There are compact convex polytopes $C,D\subset\R^{128}$, both containing $0$, such that for every $\tau\ge\tauup$ the iteration~\eqref{eq:admm} with $f=g=0$ on $C$ and $D$ has a nonconstant periodic orbit whose period divides $128$. Every proximal step on the orbit is the unique projection onto the respective polytope, $(0,0)$ is a saddle point, $\norm{x^k-z^k}^2=1638399999999268052811275665408$ for every $k$, and $z^k$ has no limit.
\end{theorem}
The polytopes do not depend on $\tau$; the initial multiplier is proportional to $\tau$.

\begin{theorem}[Scalar problems]\label{thm:scalar}
Let $E=\R$ and assume the standing hypotheses. For every $0<\tau<2$ and every initial $(z^0,u^0)\in\R^2$ the conclusions of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} hold. For every $\tau\ge2$ the instance $C=D=\R$, $f=g=0$, $z^0=0$, $u^0=1$ gives $z^k=0$, $u^k=(1-\tau)^k$ and $x^{k+1}=-(1-\tau)^k$, which do not converge. Hence the set of steps $\tau>0$ with universal convergence for scalar problems is exactly $(0,2)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}
The bracket~\eqref{eq:bracket} holds for the supremum $\tau_*$ of steps $t$ such that every $0<\tau\le t$ gives convergence in the class of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
\end{corollary}

The obstruction uses indicator objectives. Thresholds for smooth or strongly convex subclasses are not addressed; the earlier five-cycle has a strongly convex $C^2$ extension \cite{FiveCycle}, but only from $\tau\ge140111/70637$.

\section{Proximal steps and supporting inequalities}\label{sec:prox}
For $x\in C$ write $a\in\partial_Cf(x)$ when $f(y)\ge f(x)+\ip{a}{y-x}$ for every $y\in C$. This does not assume differentiability.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:prox}
$p=P_f(c)$ if and only if $p\in C$ and $c-p\in\partial_Cf(p)$. Proximal points are unique and $P_f$ is nonexpansive.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $c-p\in\partial_Cf(p)$, add the supporting inequality to $\tfrac12\norm{y-c}^2-\tfrac12\norm{p-c}^2=\tfrac12\norm{y-p}^2-\ip{c-p}{y-p}$. Conversely, compare $p$ with $p+t(y-p)$ for $0<t\le1$, use convexity, divide by $t$ and let $t\downarrow0$. For two centers, adding the supporting inequalities at $p=P_f(c)$ and $q=P_f(d)$ gives $\norm{p-q}^2\le\ip{c-d}{p-q}$, which proves uniqueness and nonexpansiveness.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:sandwich}
Let $y_k\to y$ in $C$, let $a_k\in\partial_Cf(y_k)$ converge, and let $b\in\partial_Cf(y)$. Then $f(y_k)\to f(y)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$f(y)+\ip{b}{y_k-y}\le f(y_k)\le f(y)-\ip{a_k}{y-y_k}$, and both outer terms tend to $f(y)$.
\end{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:sandwich} gives objective convergence without assuming continuity of $f$ at boundary points of $C$, where convex functions can jump.

\section{Finite-memory certificates}\label{sec:certificates}
\subsection{Centred variables and supporting gaps}
Fix a saddle point $(z_*,u_*)$ and replace $f$ and $g$ by
\[
\widehat f(x)=f(x+z_*)+\ip{u_*}{x}-f(z_*),\qquad \widehat g(z)=g(z+z_*)-\ip{u_*}{z}-g(z_*).
\]
Then $\widehat f,\widehat g\ge0$ vanish at the origin with supporting normal zero there. Put $\zeta^k=z^k-z_*$ and $v^k=(u^k-u_*)/\tau$. The multiplier update gives $x^k-z_*=\zeta^k+v^k-v^{k-1}$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:prox} identifies the normals
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normals}
a^k=\zeta^{k-1}-\tau v^{k-1}-(x^k-z_*)\in\partial\widehat f(x^k-z_*),\qquad
b^k=(x^k-z_*)+\tau v^{k-1}-\zeta^k\in\partial\widehat g(\zeta^k).
\end{equation}
The points are independent of $\tau$ and the normals are affine in $\tau$.

In a window of $m$ updates, label the saddle point as sample $0$ and the updates as samples $1,\dots,m$. For every ordered pair of samples the supporting gaps
\[
I_f(i,j)=\widehat f(X_j)-\widehat f(X_i)-\ip{a_i}{X_j-X_i}\ge0,\qquad
I_g(i,j)=\widehat g(Z_j)-\widehat g(Z_i)-\ip{b_i}{Z_j-Z_i}\ge0
\]
hold, where $X_i$, $Z_i$, $a_i$, $b_i$ are the centred points and normals. A certificate assigns nonnegative weights $\lambda^f_{ij},\lambda^g_{ij}$ whose row and column sums agree at every sample. Then all function values cancel in $\sum\lambda^f_{ij}I_f(i,j)+\sum\lambda^g_{ij}I_g(i,j)$, which is a quadratic form in the state window and is nonnegative on actual ADMM windows.

\subsection{The certificate identity}
Write a window of $m+1$ consecutive states as $w=(\zeta^0,v^0,\dots,\zeta^m,v^m)\in E^{2m+2}$ and, for a real symmetric matrix $M$, $[w]_M=\sum_{i,j}M_{ij}\ip{w_i}{w_j}$. Let $S_0$ and $S_1$ select the first and last $m$ states of the window, and let $H_m$ represent $\norm{\zeta^m-\zeta^{m-1}}^2+\norm{v^m-v^{m-1}}^2$. A certificate for the step interval $[\alpha,\beta]$ consists of rational symmetric matrices $P_\alpha,P_\beta$ and $T_\alpha,T_\beta$ and one set of weights such that, at both endpoints $\tau\in\{\alpha,\beta\}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:identity}
[S_0w]_{P(\tau)}-[S_1w]_{P(\tau)}-[w]_{H_m}-\sum_{i,j}\lambda^f_{ij}I_f(i,j)-\sum_{i,j}\lambda^g_{ij}I_g(i,j)=[Dw]_{T(\tau)}
\end{equation}
as an identity of quadratic forms, where $D$ maps the window to differences of its states, so that $[Dw]_{T}$ vanishes on constant windows, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:psd}
P(\tau)-C_m\succeq0,\qquad T(\tau)\succeq0,
\end{equation}
where $C_m$ represents $\norm{\zeta^{m-1}}^2+\norm{v^{m-1}}^2$ on the newest stored state. Because the normals~\eqref{eq:normals} are affine in $\tau$, so is the left side of~\eqref{eq:identity}. Defining $P(\tau)$ and $T(\tau)$ by linear interpolation between the endpoints, \eqref{eq:identity} holds for every $\tau\in[\alpha,\beta]$, and \eqref{eq:psd} holds by convexity of the positive-semidefinite cone. Scalar identities lift to vectors by bilinearity.

\subsection{The classical branch \texorpdfstring{$0<\tau<3/2$}{0 < tau < 3/2}}\label{sec:classical}
For $k\ge1$ let $r_k=v^k-v^{k-1}=x^k-z^k$, $q_{k+1}=\zeta^{k+1}-\zeta^k$ and $E_k=\norm{\zeta^k}^2+\tau\norm{v^k}^2$. The supporting inequalities between the new proximal points and the saddle point give
\[
E_k-E_{k+1}\ge(2-\tau)\norm{r_{k+1}}^2+\norm{q_{k+1}}^2+2\ip{r_{k+1}}{q_{k+1}},
\]
and monotonicity of the $g$-normals at consecutive points gives $\ip{q_{k+1}}{r_{k+1}+(\tau-1)r_k}\ge0$. With $W_k=E_k+|\tau-1|\norm{r_k}^2$ and $2|t\ip ab|\le|t|(\norm a^2+\norm b^2)$,
\[
W_k-W_{k+1}\ge(2-\tau-|\tau-1|)\norm{r_{k+1}}^2+(1-|\tau-1|)\norm{q_{k+1}}^2.
\]
Both coefficients are positive exactly for $0<\tau<3/2$, so $W_k$ is a coercive energy with strict decrease of the increments.

\section{From certificates to convergence}\label{sec:convergence}
Fix $\tau$ and a certificate of memory $m$ covering it; the classical branch is the case $m=1$ with energy $W_k$. Relative to a saddle point define
\[
\mathcal E_k=[(\zeta^k,v^k,\dots,\zeta^{k+m-1},v^{k+m-1})]_{P(\tau)}.
\]
By~\eqref{eq:psd}, $\mathcal E_k\ge\norm{\zeta^{k+m-1}}^2+\norm{v^{k+m-1}}^2$. By~\eqref{eq:identity} and nonnegativity of the gaps and of $[Dw]_{T(\tau)}$,
\[
\mathcal E_k-\mathcal E_{k+1}\ge\norm{\zeta^{k+m}-\zeta^{k+m-1}}^2+\norm{v^{k+m}-v^{k+m-1}}^2.
\]
Hence the states are bounded and their increments are square summable; in particular $x^{k+1}-z^{k+1}=v^{k+1}-v^k\to0$. These are conclusions, not hypotheses.

The state map $T_\tau:(z,u)\mapsto(z^+,u^+)$ is continuous by Lemma~\ref{lem:prox}. A bounded sequence in finite dimension has a convergent subsequence $s_{k_j}\to\bar s$. Vanishing increments give $s_{k_j+1}\to\bar s$, and continuity gives $s_{k_j+1}=T_\tau(s_{k_j})\to T_\tau(\bar s)$, so $\bar s$ is a fixed point. Because $\tau>0$, a fixed point has $x=z$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:prox} turns the two proximal equations into the saddle-point inequalities. Now centre the energy at this saddle point $\bar s$. Along the subsequence the finitely many states in the window converge to $\bar s$, so $\mathcal E_{k_j}\to0$; monotonicity gives $\mathcal E_k\to0$ and coercivity gives $s_k\to\bar s$. Continuity of $P_f$ gives $x^{k+1}\to P_f(\bar z-\bar u)=\bar z$.

Finally $a^{k+1}=z^k-u^k-x^{k+1}\to-\bar u$ and $b^{k+1}=x^{k+1}+u^k-z^{k+1}\to\bar u$ are supporting normals converging to the saddle normals, so Lemma~\ref{lem:sandwich} gives $f(x^{k+1})\to f(\bar z)$ and $g(z^{k+1})\to g(\bar z)$. The intervals $(0,3/2)$, $[3/2,7/4]$, $[7/4,47/25]$, $[47/25,\taulo]$ and the five intervals of version~1.1 cover $(0,\taunew]$, which proves Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

\section{Affine constraints}\label{sec:affine}
For Theorem~\ref{thm:affine} define the infimal images
\[
F(a)=\inf\{f(x):x\in C,\ A(x)=a\},\qquad G(b)=\inf\{g(y):y\in D,\ B(y)=b\}
\]
on the images $A(C)$ and $B(D)$. The original saddle point bounds every fibre from below, so $F$ and $G$ are finite and convex there. If $x$ attains the original affine subproblem, it also attains the fibre infimum at $A(x)$; otherwise a better point in the same fibre would improve the subproblem. Hence $A(x)$ is the proximal point of $F/\rho$ at the corresponding center, and every choice of original minimizers induces the same consensus iteration on the images, with multiplier $u$. Theorem~\ref{thm:main} gives a limiting image saddle point $(a,q)$. Attainment of the original subproblems at the centers $a-q$ and $a+q$, together with uniqueness of image proximal points, provides original points whose images both equal $a$; they attain their fibre infima and form an original saddle point with multiplier $\rho q$. Fibre optimality identifies the original objective values along the trajectory with the image values, which proves convergence of $f(x_n)+g(y_n)$ without closedness of $F$ or $G$. Continuous left inverses transfer image convergence to the raw iterates.

\section{The periodic obstruction}\label{sec:cycle}
The certificate file \lean{certificates/exact_cycle_1886884_K128.json} supplies integer vectors $z,v\in\mathbb Z^{128}$. Let $S$ be the cyclic left shift, so $S^{128}=I$, and set
\[
x=z+v-S^{-1}v,\qquad C=\conv\{S^ix:0\le i<128\},\qquad D=\conv\{S^iz:0\le i<128\}.
\]
The coordinates of $z$ and $v$ sum to zero, hence so do those of $x$, and averaging all shifts shows $0\in C\cap D$. With zero objectives, $(0,0)$ is a saddle point.

For each offset $j$ define the vertex margins
\[
M_f(j,\tau)=\ip{S^{-1}z-\tau S^{-1}v-x}{x-S^jx},\qquad M_g(j,\tau)=\ip{x+\tau S^{-1}v-z}{z-S^jz}.
\]
Each is an integer plus $\tau$ times an integer. The certificate verifies that every slope is nonnegative and that every margin is nonnegative at $\tau=\tauup$; the binding inequality is the $g$-margin at offset $j=1$. Hence all margins are nonnegative for every $\tau\ge\tauup$.

Nonnegative vertex margins extend by convexity to the whole polytope: if $p\in K$ and $\ip{c-p}{y-p}\le0$ for every $y\in K$, then $\norm{y-c}^2=\norm{p-c}^2+\norm{y-p}^2-2\ip{c-p}{y-p}\ge\norm{p-c}^2+\norm{y-p}^2$, so $p$ is the unique projection of $c$ onto $K$. Define $x^i=S^ix$, $z^i=S^iz$ and $u^i=\tau S^iv$. Because $S$ is orthogonal, the base inequalities give both proximal steps at every phase, and the multiplier update is the identity $u^i-u^{i-1}=\tau(S^iv-S^{i-1}v)=\tau(x^i-z^i)$. The sequences have period dividing $128$, the residual $\norm{x-z}^2=1638399999999268052811275665408$ is constant, and $Sz\ne z$ gives $\norm{z^{k+1}-z^k}=\norm{Sz-z}>0$ at every step, so $z^k$ does not converge. This proves Theorem~\ref{thm:cycle}.

The binding margin has positive slope and vanishes at $\tauup$, so the projection characterization fails just below $\tauup$ and the bound is tight for this orbit. A Fourier optimization located the integer vectors; it is not part of the proof. Version~1.0 used a different orbit of the same form, in \lean{certificates/exact_cycle_18869_10000_K128.json}, valid for $\tau\ge897744153596994489/475778439151265168\approx1.8868954$ with residual $16383999960516622876672$; that orbit is the one formalized in Lean (Section~\ref{sec:formal}).

\section{Scalar problems}\label{sec:scalar}
This section proves Theorem~\ref{thm:scalar}. The divergent instance is immediate: with $C=D=\R$ and $f=g=0$ both proximal maps are the identity, $(0,0)$ is a saddle point, and~\eqref{eq:admm} gives $x^{k+1}=z^k-u^k$, $z^{k+1}=x^{k+1}+u^k=z^k$ and $u^{k+1}=(1-\tau)u^k$. From $z^0=0$, $u^0=1$ this is the stated orbit; it alternates for $\tau=2$ and diverges for $\tau>2$.

Now fix $0<\tau<2$ and centre at a saddle point $(z_*,u_*)$ as in Section~\ref{sec:certificates}. Let $P$ and $Q$ be the proximal maps of $\widehat f$ and $\widehat g$. In the variables $x-z_*$, $z-z_*$, $u-u_*$, again written $x,z,u$, the iteration is $x^{k+1}=P(z^k-u^k)$, $z^{k+1}=Q(x^{k+1}+u^k)$, $u^{k+1}=u^k+\tau(x^{k+1}-z^{k+1})$, and $P(0)=Q(0)=0$. In dimension one the inequality $(p-q)^2\le(c-d)(p-q)$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:prox} says that $P$ and $Q$ are nondecreasing and $1$-Lipschitz, and so is $B=I-Q$. Thus $Q(t)$ and $B(t)$ have the weak sign of $t$, and $\lvert t\rvert=\lvert Q(t)\rvert+\lvert B(t)\rvert$.

\paragraph{A lifted recurrence.} Put $h=\tau-1$, $a=\lvert h\rvert<1$ and $\varepsilon=1-a>0$. For $k\ge1$ let $t_k=x^k+u^{k-1}$. Then $z^k=Q(t_k)$ and $u^k=u^{k-1}+\tau(t_k-u^{k-1}-Q(t_k))=\tau B(t_k)-hu^{k-1}$. Substituting $x^{k+1}=P(Q(t_k)-u^k)$ shows $(t_{k+1},u^{k+1})=\Phi(t_k,u^k)$ for the continuous map
\[
\Phi(t,u)=\bigl(s,\ \tau B(s)-hu\bigr),\qquad s=u+P(Q(t)-u).
\]
The state $(z^k,u^k)=(Q(t_k),u^k)$ is recovered from $(t_k,u^k)$; no proximal map is inverted. Since $P$ fixes $0$, is nondecreasing and is $1$-Lipschitz, $P(Q(t)-u)$ lies between $0$ and $Q(t)-u$, so $s$ lies in the closed interval with endpoints $q=Q(t)$ and $u$.

\paragraph{A Lyapunov function.} Define
\[
L(t,u)=\varepsilon\lvert Q(t)\rvert+\lvert B(t)\rvert+\lvert u-B(t)\rvert .
\]
It is continuous, $L(t,u)\ge\varepsilon\lvert t\rvert$ because $Q(t)$ and $B(t)$ have the same sign, and $L(t,u)\ge\lvert u\rvert$ by the triangle inequality. Write $b=B(t)$ and $J(c)=\lvert c\rvert+\lvert u-c\rvert$, so that $L(t,u)=\varepsilon\lvert q\rvert+J(b)$. Because the new multiplier satisfies $\tau B(s)-hu-B(s)=h(B(s)-u)$,
\[
L(\Phi(t,u))=\psi(s),\qquad \psi(r)=\varepsilon\lvert Q(r)\rvert+\lvert B(r)\rvert+a\lvert B(r)-u\rvert.
\]
For $0\le r<r'$, monotonicity of $Q$ and $B$ and the reverse triangle inequality give $\psi(r')-\psi(r)\ge\varepsilon(Q(r')-Q(r))+(1-a)(B(r')-B(r))=\varepsilon(r'-r)>0$; symmetrically $\psi$ is strictly decreasing on $(-\infty,0]$. Hence on a closed interval $\psi$ attains its maximum only at an endpoint, and every interior value is strictly smaller than the larger endpoint value. At the endpoint $u$, the common signs give $\psi(u)=\lvert Q(u)\rvert+\lvert B(u)\rvert=\lvert u\rvert=J(0)\le J(b)=L(t,u)-\varepsilon\lvert q\rvert$. At the endpoint $q$, monotonicity puts $B(q)$ between $0$ and $b$ (if $t\ge0$ then $0\le q\le t$, so $0\le B(q)\le B(t)$; the case $t<0$ is symmetric), and $\lvert Q(q)\rvert=\lvert q\rvert-\lvert B(q)\rvert$, so $\psi(q)=\varepsilon\lvert q\rvert+aJ(B(q))$. Convexity of $J$ and $J(0)\le J(b)$ give $J(B(q))\le J(b)$, hence $\psi(q)\le L(t,u)-\varepsilon J(b)$. Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scalar-lyap}
L(\Phi(t,u))\le L(t,u)\qquad\text{for all }(t,u)\in\R^2 .
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Equality.} Suppose $L(\Phi(w))=L(w)$ for $w=(t,u)$. Then $s\in\{q,u\}$ and the corresponding bound is an equality. If $s=q$ then $J(b)=0$, so $u=b=0$, $t=q$, $Q(q)=q$ and $P(q)=q$; thus $\Phi(w)=w$. Otherwise $s=u$, $q=0$ and $P(-u)=0$, and $\Phi(w)=(u,u')$ with $u'=B(u)-hQ(u)$. If moreover $L(\Phi^2(w))=L(\Phi(w))$, apply the same alternatives at $\Phi(w)$: either $\Phi(w)$ is fixed, or $Q(u)=0$. In the latter case $B(u)=u$ and $u'=u$, and $\Phi(u,u)=(u+P(-u),\tau u-hu)=(u,u)$. In both cases
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-drops}
L(w)=L(\Phi(w))=L(\Phi^2(w))\ \Longrightarrow\ \Phi(\Phi(w))=\Phi(w).
\end{equation}
One equality is not enough: for $P=0$, $Q$ the proximal map of $\max(0,z)$ on $\R$, $\tau=19/10$ and $w=(1,14/5)$ one finds $\Phi(w)=(14/5,-31/50)\ne w$ and $L(\Phi(w))=L(w)=14/5$.

\paragraph{Convergence.} Let $w_k=(t_k,u^k)$. By~\eqref{eq:scalar-lyap} and coercivity the sequence is bounded and $L(w_k)$ decreases to some $\ell$. Choose $w_{k_j}\to w'$. Continuity gives $L(w')=L(\Phi(w'))=L(\Phi^2(w'))=\ell$, so $\bar w=\Phi(w')$ is fixed by~\eqref{eq:two-drops} and is a cluster point, the limit of $w_{k_j+1}$. Write $\bar w=(\bar t,\bar u)$ and $\bar q=Q(\bar t)$. The second coordinate gives $\tau\bar u=\tau B(\bar t)$, so $\bar u=B(\bar t)$ and $\bar t=\bar q+\bar u$, and the first gives $P(\bar q-\bar u)=\bar q$; also $Q(\bar q+\bar u)=\bar q$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:prox}, $(\bar q,\bar u)$ is a saddle point of the centred problem, with $\bar q$ in both domains. Recentring at it replaces $P,Q$ by $c\mapsto P(\bar q-\bar u+c)-\bar q$ and $c\mapsto Q(\bar q+\bar u+c)-\bar q$, which have the same properties, and translates the lifted variables by $\bar w$; the recurrence keeps its form. The recentred function $L_{\bar w}(t,u)=\varepsilon\lvert Q(t)-\bar q\rvert+\lvert B(t)-\bar u\rvert+\lvert u-B(t)\rvert$ is therefore nonincreasing along $w_k$, and it tends to $0$ along $w_{k_j+1}$. Hence $L_{\bar w}(w_k)\to0$, and coercivity gives $t_k\to\bar t$ and $u^k\to\bar u$. Then $z^k=Q(t_k)\to\bar q$ and $x^{k+1}=P(z^k-u^k)\to P(\bar q-\bar u)=\bar q$. The normals $a^{k+1}$ and $b^{k+1}$ of Section~\ref{sec:convergence} converge to the saddle normals, so Lemma~\ref{lem:sandwich} gives the objective limits. Undoing the centring proves Theorem~\ref{thm:scalar}.

The argument uses the order of the real line through the common signs of $Q(t)$ and $B(t)$. It does not extend to higher dimension, where Theorem~\ref{thm:cycle} shows that the range is smaller.

\section{An earlier published claim}\label{sec:gao}
Gao, Li, Ma, Li, Sun, Maihemuti and Jin study relaxed ADMM for model-predictive control and, according to our reading of their Sections~2.2.3 and~4.3, assert convergence of the multiplier-only iteration~\eqref{eq:admm} for $1<\tau\le2$ \cite{Gao2022}. With penalty one in consensus form, a saddle point $(z_*,u_*)$, $r_k=x^k-z^k$ and $q_k=(u^k-u_*)^2/\tau+(z^k-z_*)^2$, their inequalities (45) and (47) specialize to
\[
q_k-q_{k+1}\ge\tau(z^{k+1}-z^k)^2+(\tau-1)r_k^2+(2-\tau)r_{k+1}^2.
\]
Take $E=\R$, $f(x)=x$, $g(z)=-z$ and $z^0=u^0=0$. The saddle point is $(0,-1)$, the updates are $x^{k+1}=z^k-u^k-1$, $z^{k+1}=z^k=0$ and $u^{k+1}=(1-\tau)u^k-\tau$. At $\tau=3/2$ one finds $q_1-q_2=1/8$ while the right side is $5/8$; at $\tau=7/4$ the values are $9/64$ and $57/64$. At $\tau=2$ the iteration is an actual two-cycle, $u^k$ alternating between $0$ and $-2$ and $x^k$ between $-1$ and $1$. This scalar example converges for $\tau<2$, as every scalar instance does by Theorem~\ref{thm:scalar}, so it refutes only the displayed inequality; Theorem~\ref{thm:cycle} refutes the asserted range for every $\tau\ge\tauup$. We could not re-open the full text of~\cite{Gao2022} while preparing this release, so the identification of their equations rests on an earlier reading recorded in the uploaded package.

No earlier theorem establishing universal convergence of~\eqref{eq:admm} beyond the golden ratio for general convex problems, and no convex obstruction below the five-cycle range of this collection, was found in the sources we inspected; Gao et~al.\ is an earlier assertion whose stated range is false. Version~1.1 refines the bracket of version~1.0 with the same methods. \section{Limitations and open questions}
The exact threshold $\tau_*$ in~\eqref{eq:bracket} is open. Theorems~\ref{thm:cycle} and~\ref{thm:scalar} show that the universal threshold depends on the dimension: it is $2$ for scalar problems and at most $\tauup$ in dimension $128$. Since an instance can be embedded in any larger dimension, the threshold does not increase with the dimension; its values in intermediate dimensions, and the smallest dimension in which it is below $2$, are open. The results concern finite dimension, exact subproblems, fixed $\tau$ and penalty, and two blocks; weak convergence in infinite dimension, inexact or varying steps, more blocks and rates are not addressed. The obstruction uses indicator objectives, so the thresholds for smooth or strongly convex subclasses may be larger. No practical recommendation for $\tau$ follows from the theorems.
