\begin{abstract}
We give an explicit five-variable conditional-independence model that admits a
structural adhesive extension at every overlap, yet is not induced by any
numerically selfadhesive real polymatroid. The positive statement is certified by
32 integer polymatroids, with all nontrivial coordinate tables printed below.
The obstruction is an exact identity involving 51 nonnegative conditional mutual
informations and two substantive copied extensions. It excludes every real
inducing profile, not merely the profiles used in the positive certificates.
This gives a negative answer to Question~4.18 (4.14 in the PDF version) of Boege's 2023 paper on
selfadhesivity~\cite{Boege}. The complete theorem, its powerset semantics, and
the exact certificates are formalized in Lean 4. Formal correctness and
historical novelty are separate claims, and the novelty assessment is
provisional.
\end{abstract}

\section{The exact question and theorem}
Let $N=\{1,2,3,4,5\}$. A polymatroid is a function $h:2^N\to\R$ satisfying
\[
h(\varnothing)=0,\qquad h(A)\le h(B)\ (A\subseteq B),\qquad
h(A)+h(B)\ge h(A\cap B)+h(A\cup B).
\]
For distinct $i,j$ and $K\subseteq N\setminus\{i,j\}$, put
\[
 I_h(i;j\mid K)=h(Ki)+h(Kj)-h(K)-h(Kij).
\]
Juxtaposition denotes union. Every such quantity is nonnegative. The induced
CI model $\M_h$ is its exact zero set. Thus specifying $\M_h=\M$ requires
strict positivity at every elementary CI outside $\M$.

For $L\subseteq N$, an $L$-copy is a set $N'_L$ together with a bijection
$b_L:N\to N'_L$ that fixes $L$, where $N\cap N'_L=L$. A polymatroid $g$ on
$U_L=N\cup N'_L$ is adhesive at this overlap when
\begin{equation}\label{eq:adhesion}
 A_L(g):=g(N)+g(N'_L)-g(U_L)-g(L)=0.
\end{equation}
This is the group conditional independence of the two complements given $L$.
The implication from adhesion to all required elementary crossing CIs is proved below.

A CI model is \emph{structurally selfadhesive} if, for every $L$, such a $g_L$
exists with both marginal CI models exactly equal to $\M$. Its inducing
numerical profile can depend on $L$. A single polymatroid $h$ is
\emph{numerically selfadhesive} if for every $L$ an adhesive $g_L$ exists whose
two numerical restrictions are both exactly $h$ under the copy map.

Boege asks whether every structurally selfadhesive structural CI model has a
numerically selfadhesive inducing polymatroid~\cite[Question~4.18 in the HTML;
Question~4.14 in the PDF]{Boege}. These are different quantifier orders:
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\forall L\ \exists g_L
   &&\bigl(\M_{g_L|_N}=\M_{g_L|_{N'_L}}=\M\bigr),\\
 &\exists h\ \forall L\ \exists g_L
   &&\bigl(\M_h=\M,\quad g_L|_N=g_L|_{N'_L}=h\bigr).
 \end{aligned}
\]
The copied restrictions are identified with profiles on $N$ via $b_L$;
polymatroid and adhesion conditions are understood in both lines.
The theorem disproves their equivalence.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let $\M$ consist exactly of the 26 CI statements in Table~\ref{tab:model}.
Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item For every $L\subseteq N$ and every bijective $L$-copy, there is a real
polymatroid $g_L$ on their union satisfying~\eqref{eq:adhesion}, whose two
restrictions induce exactly $\M$.
\item There is no real polymatroid $h:2^N\to\R$ inducing exactly $\M$ that is
numerically selfadhesive at every overlap.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently the known inclusion of CI models induced by numerically
selfadhesive polymatroids into $\st^{\diamond}(N)$ is strict when $|N|=5$.
\end{theorem}

\section{Positive proof: all overlaps have exact witnesses}\label{sec:positive}
We use canonical labels $U_L=\{1,\ldots,10-|L|\}$. The original labels are
$1,\ldots,5$. The copy fixes the labels of $L$ and sends the remaining labels,
in increasing order, to $6,7,\ldots,10-|L|$. Encode a subset $S$ by
$\sum_{i\in S}2^{i-1}$. Every array below is indexed in increasing mask order,
starting at the empty set.

We specify integer standardized supermodular tables $m_L$ and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:positiveh}
 g_L(S)=1155\,|S|-m_L(S).
\end{equation}
The modular term does not change any elementary CMI. The data are complete:
Appendix~\ref{app:positive} gives the 20 tables for $|L|=2,3$, and the following
formulas give the other 12 cases.

Define the five-variable base table $m_0$ by
\begin{Verbatim}[fontsize=\small]
0,0,0,0,0,1,0,5,0,1,0,5,2,3,5,10,
0,1,0,5,3,4,5,10,0,5,5,10,5,10,10,17.
\end{Verbatim}
For $|L|\le1$, set
\[
 m_L(S)=m_0(S\cap N)+m_0\bigl(b_L^{-1}(S\cap N'_L)\bigr).
\]
For $L=N$, set $m_L=m_0$. For $|L|=4$, write $N\setminus L=\{i\}$, let $i'$ be
its copied label, put $d_i=m_0(N)-m_0(L)$, and define
\[
 m_L(S)=m_0\bigl((S\cap N)\cup\{i:i'\in S\}\bigr)
       +d_i\,\mathbf1_{\{i,i'\}\subseteq S}.
\]
The 20 printed tables are independently scaled integer witnesses; there is no
requirement that their restrictions be equal across different overlaps.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:positive}
For all 32 overlaps, the functions in~\eqref{eq:positiveh} are polymatroids,
induce exactly Table~\ref{tab:model} on both copies, and satisfy
$A_L(g_L)=0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
This is a finite exact certificate proof. All coordinates are given above or in
Appendix~\ref{app:positive}. Substitution verifies $g_L(\varnothing)=0$ and the
following elementary inequalities for every admissible $S,i,j$:
\begin{align*}
 g_L(S)&\le g_L(Si),\qquad i\notin S,\\
 g_L(S)+g_L(Sij)&\le g_L(Si)+g_L(Sj),
       \qquad i,j\notin S,\ i\ne j.
\end{align*}
The first family implies monotonicity by adding missing elements one at a time.
The second family implies diminishing returns: successively adding the
members of $T\setminus S$ gives
\[
 g_L(Ti)-g_L(T)\le g_L(Si)-g_L(S)
 \quad(S\subseteq T,\ i\notin T).
\]
Inducting on $A\setminus B$ and adding these increment inequalities proves
full submodularity for every pair $A,B$. This argument is proved generically
in \texttt{LocalToGlobal.lean}; \texttt{LocalPM.lean} transports it through the
proved mask/finite-set encoding. Thus the formal conclusion is the full
polymatroid definition, not a replacement by unchecked local conditions.

Evaluating all 80 elementary CMIs on each copy gives zeros exactly at
Table~\ref{tab:model}, and positive integers everywhere else. The
four-coordinate expression $A_L(g_L)$ is zero in every case. The full
polymatroid property, both exact CI models, and the adhesion equality are
separately kernel-checked from the integer data. The empty
overlap is proved by an algebraic direct sum of two base polymatroids, while
the full overlap includes the identity copy.

There is an additional exact ordinary certificate observation: for each fixed
$L$ and every $S\subseteq N$, direct substitution gives
$g_L(S)=g_L(b_L(S))$. This numerical equality within each overlap is checked
in the supplied integer data; it is not part of the Lean endpoint or the
stated theorem.

The independent standard-library checker additionally enumerates every
unordered subset pair, without the local-to-global reduction: 1,603,888
submodularity checks and 248,832 comparable-pair monotonicity checks pass.
That checker is supplementary to the Lean proof, not an axiom used by it.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:crossing}
If $A,B$ form a modular pair in a polymatroid $g$, then for every
$A\cap B\subseteq S\subseteq A\cup B$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:split}
 g(S)=g(S\cap A)+g(S\cap B)-g(A\cap B).
\end{equation}
Consequently every crossing elementary CMI, with conditioning containing
$A\cap B$, vanishes.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Submodularity of $S\cap A,S\cap B$ gives one inequality
in~\eqref{eq:split}. Add submodularity for $A,S$ and for $B,A\cup S$.
Because $A\cap B\subseteq S\subseteq A\cup B$, the resulting inequality is
\[
 g(A)+g(B)+g(S)\ge g(S\cap A)+g(S\cap B)+g(A\cup B).
\]
Use $g(A)+g(B)=g(A\cup B)+g(A\cap B)$ for the reverse inequality.
For $P\subseteq A\setminus B$, $Q\subseteq B\setminus A$ and an admissible $K$,
apply~\eqref{eq:split} to $K,KP,KQ,KPQ$. The terms cancel, giving
$g(KP)+g(KQ)-g(K)-g(KPQ)=0$. Taking $P,Q$ to be singletons yields the required
elementary statements. The stronger arbitrary-subset statement is formalized.
\end{proof}
Any other bijective $L$-copy is obtained by relabeling the canonical union.
Pull back subsets under that ground-set bijection. This preserves every
coordinate, normalization, monotonicity, submodularity, CI value, and the
modular-pair equality. \texttt{Transport.lean} constructs actual powerset
encodings and proves this for every ground-set type and bijection. Lemmas
\ref{lem:positive} and~\ref{lem:crossing} therefore prove part 1 of
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} with its actual copy quantifiers.

\section{Negative proof: no common real profile exists}\label{sec:negative}
Assume that $h$ is a real polymatroid inducing exactly $\M$ and is numerically
selfadhesive. Choose numerical gluings $g_{34}$ and $g_{145}$ at $L=\{3,4\}$
and $L=\{1,4,5\}$. Their original and copied restrictions are $h$ itself.
The two copied ground sets use the canonical labeling from
Section~\ref{sec:positive}.

Let $Q(h,g_{34},g_{145})$ be the positive integer combination of CMIs in
Appendix~\ref{app:negative}. It contains five base CMIs and 46 CMIs in the two
copied polymatroids. Every summand is nonnegative. Direct expansion gives the
exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:certificate}
 I_h(2;4\mid135)+Q
 =R(h)+3A_{34}(g_{34})+3A_{145}(g_{145}),
\end{equation}
where
\begin{align*}
R(h)={}&3I_h(1;2\mid45)+2I_h(1;3\mid4)+I_h(1;3\mid5)
          +I_h(1;3\mid24)\\
 &+2I_h(1;4\mid3)+I_h(1;4\mid25)+I_h(1;5\mid3)\\
 &+I_h(2;4)+I_h(2;5)+2I_h(2;5\mid134)\\
 &+2I_h(3;4\mid12)+2I_h(4;5\mid23).
\end{align*}
To check~\eqref{eq:certificate}, replace each CMI by its four entropy
coordinates, substitute the equal numerical copy restrictions, and collect
coefficients. Every residual coefficient is zero. No normalization of $h(N)$,
no rationality hypothesis, and no chosen ray decomposition is used.

Each CMI in $R(h)$ is prescribed zero. Both adhesive gaps vanish. Thus the
right-hand side is zero, while every term on the left-hand side is
nonnegative. In particular
\[
 I_h(2;4\mid135)=0.
\]
That statement is absent from Table~\ref{tab:model}; exact induction of $\M$
requires it to be positive. This contradiction proves part 2 of
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. The Lean proof instantiates all 51 submodular
inequalities on actual real-valued functions and checks the coefficient
identity by ring normalization. It does not assume a solver's infeasibility
verdict or a Farkas certificate validator.

\section{Information-theoretic consequence and prior work}\label{sec:prior}
The foundational consequence is precise. Boolean CI-level selfadhesion tests
can all pass while no common numerical Shannon-polymatroid profile supports
the corresponding copy constructions. Hence structural selfadhesivity
forgets compatibility information needed by numerical copy tests. A solver
that separately realizes the CI model for each overlap cannot conclude that
there is one numerically selfadhesive realization.

This is an entropy-geometry and CI-inference result. It does not characterize
the five-variable entropy cone or supply a channel capacity or coding bound.
It also does not assert that the construction is a distribution: polymatroids
are the Shannon outer relaxation.

Boege's four-variable computation reports 1,224 structurally selfadhesive
isomorphism types, each induced by a numerically selfadhesive
polymatroid~\cite{Boege}. The five-variable result therefore lies beyond that
established positive case. Minimality relative to that published enumeration
is contextual; this project does not Lean-formalize that earlier enumeration.
The newer Boege--Bolt--Studen\'y paper proves numerical selfadhesivity implies
structural selfadhesivity and that almost-entropic polymatroids are numerically
selfadhesive~\cite[Lemma~8.2]{BBS}. Its five-variable extreme-ray computations
cover a restricted family, rather than all CI faces.

There is significant adjacent prior work. The ScaleMath repository committed
an exact five-variable counterexample on 23 August 2026 UTC~\cite{ScaleMath}.
Its stated theorem answers BBS Open Problem 3 by separating
\[
 \st^{\diamond}(5)\subsetneq\sg^{\diamond}(5)\cap\st(5).
\]
Its 20-CI model has no structural adhesion at one overlap. Our model has a
structural adhesion at every overlap and separates the different inclusion
\[
 \{\M_h:h\text{ is numerically selfadhesive}\}
 \subsetneq\st^{\diamond}(5).
\]
The distinction is in the theorem's hypotheses and quantifiers, not merely in
a different CI string. Searching higher-dimensional faces and proving exact
CMI identities were already used there and are not claimed as new methods.

The present model's non-entropicness is also not new information. Studen\'y's
older implication (I:1), under $(X,Y,Z,U)=(4,5,3,1)$, applies to the prescribed
zeros $45\mid\varnothing$, $45\mid3$, $13\mid4$, $13\mid5$ and forces
$13\mid\varnothing$ for actual distributions~\cite[Section~V.B]{Studeny}.
Our model omits that conclusion. This old observation does not settle whether
there is a numerically selfadhesive polymatroid inducing the model. The
contribution assessed here is the full structural/numerical separation.
