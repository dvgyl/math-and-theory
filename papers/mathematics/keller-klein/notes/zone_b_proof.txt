Keller--Klein upper concentration inequality: small-a Zone B
Conventional proof aligned with the compiled Lean sources, 2026-10-30.

This text alone is not formal verification or a priority claim. The actual
Zone B theorem and all analytic inputs compile in the companion project;
the independent clean-build and statement audits are recorded separately.
FINAL ROUTE: elementary cotangent bounds, Dsmall with ax/2, a direct
reflected-kernel alias estimate, a combined 6*e0 envelope remainder, and
the a*x^2 cutoff. The final master coefficient is cR=1/40.

STATEMENT

Let 0<a<=7/100, T=pi/a, and J be the actual piecewise characteristic
function envelope integral from Sections 3 and 10 of Ivanisvili's
October 21, 2026 manuscript, with its kernel sign written explicitly
below. Write phi0=1/sqrt(2*pi), phi(x)=phi0*exp(-x^2/2),
c(x)=integral_0^x phi(t)dt=Phi(x)-1/2, H(x)=2*c(x), and

  F_U(x)=J(-x;a,pi/a)-c(x)-c(a).

Then:
  (i) a/2<=x<=1 implies F_U(x)<=-phi0*a^3/20;
  (ii) 1<=x and a*x^2<=1 imply F_U(x)<=-a/40.
For x>=0 with a*x^2>=1, H(x)+H(a)>=1 by the Gaussian Mills bound
proved below, so the desired probability inequality is trivial.

Together with the actual Prawitz envelope inequality
  P(|X|<=x)<=2*J(-x;a,pi/a),
these statements prove the complete Zone B x>=a/2 for every normalized
finite Rademacher sum with all coefficient magnitudes at most a. The
closed event is retained throughout, including all atoms.

1. CONSTANTS AND ELEMENTARY ESTIMATES

Use Kg=1/20, cR=1/40, 3<pi<22/7, pi^2<=10, pi<=13/4, and
  39/100<=phi0<=2/5.
Let theta be the internally defined crossover satisfying
  pi/2<theta<pi, |cos theta|=exp(-theta^2/2).
Define eta=1/2+3/pi^2 and delta=4/(3*pi). Then
  4/5<=eta<=5/6, 0<=delta<=4/9, eta-delta>=16/45.
No numerical refinement of theta is needed for Zone B.

(A) For 0<=y<=1,
  0<=-log cos y-y^2/2<=y^4/12+y^6/20.
For the upper bound, use
  sin y<=y-y^3/6+y^5/120,
  cos y>=1-y^2/2+y^4/24-y^6/720>0.
For z=y^2 in [0,1], the difference
  (1-z/2+z^2/24-z^3/720)*(1+z/3+3*z^2/10)
    -(1-z/6+z^2/120)
is z^2*(1/6-11*z/80+13*z^2/1080-z^3/2400)>=0, because
1/6-11/80-1/2400>0. Hence tan y<=y+y^3/3+3*y^5/10.
Integrate tan y-y. The lower bound follows from tan y>=y.

(B) Define, for 0<u<1,
  rho(u)=(1-u)*cot(pi*u)+1/pi,
  h(u)=rho(u)-1/(pi*u),
  q(y)=cot y-1/y+y/3.
The elementary kernel inequalities are
  0<=rho(u)<=1/(pi*u), -pi*u/2<=h(u)<=0,
  q(y)<=0 for 0<y<pi.
To check positivity, put y=pi*u. The function sin y-y*cos y is
nonnegative on [0,pi], since its derivative is y*sin y. Applying it
at pi-y shows sin y+(pi-y)cos y>=0, exactly the numerator of rho(u)
after multiplication by pi*sin y. The derivative of
  (1-y^2/3)sin y-y*cos y
is (y/3)*(sin y-y*cos y)>=0. Hence cot y<=1/y-y/3, which implies
rho(u)<=1/y and q(y)<=0. For the lower bound, if y^2<=2 then
cos y>=1-y^2/2>=0 and sin y<=y give cot y>=1/y-y/2. Thus
rho(u)>=(1-u)*(1/y-y/2)+1/pi=1/y-y/2+u*y/2>=1/y-y/2.
If y^2>=2, the claimed lower bound is nonpositive, so rho>=0
already proves it. The stronger finite Taylor lower bound is
  (1-y^2/3-y^4/32)sin y<=y cos y, 0<=y<=pi/2.
If Q=1-y^2/3-y^4/32>=0, compare the sextic cosine lower bound
with Q times the quintic sine upper bound. Their difference is
  y^5*(13/1440-11*y^2/2880+y^4/3840).
For w=y^2 in [0,5/2], the parenthesis is at least 17/15360>0.
If Q<0, the sine and cosine are nonnegative and the inequality is
immediate. Thus q(y)>=-y^3/32 on 0<y<=pi/2, and in particular
  |q(y)|<=y^3/30 for 0<y<=1.
Also
  (1-u)q(pi*u)=h(u)+(1-u)*pi*u/3.
Its left side is nonpositive and its right side is at least -pi*u/2.
Consequently
  |(1-u)q(pi*u)|<=pi*u/2.
No cotangent partial-fraction expansion is used.

(C) The same finite Taylor cotangent bound gives, for 0<t<=1/2,
  rho(1-t)=[1-(pi*t)cot(pi*t)]/pi
          <=pi*t^2/3+(pi^3/32)*t^4
          <=pi*t^2/3+t^4<=(4/3)*t^2.
Here pi^3/32<=1, t^4<=t^2/4, and pi<=13/4. This direct reflected
coefficient bound replaces the earlier, longer alias estimate.

2. EXACT GAUSSIAN BASELINE AND ITS REMAINDERS

Put s=pi*u/a, xi=x/a, and g(s)=exp(-s^2/2). The upper-concentration
Prawitz kernel is explicitly
  k_-(u)=(1-u)cos(s*x)+rho(u)sin(s*x).
Thus the Lean function upperKernel(x/a,u) corresponds to J(-x), not
J(+x). For every xi and 0<u<1,
  |k_-(u)|<=1+1/(pi*u)=1+1/(a*s).
For clarity, on the frequency range 0<=a*s<=pi the actual envelopes
for the normalized finite cosine product are
  lower(s)=cos(a*s)^(1/a^2)                    if a*s<=pi/2;
           -|cos(a*s)|*exp(-(1-a^2)*s^2/2)    if pi/2<a*s<=theta;
           -|cos(a*s)|^(1/a^2)                if theta<a*s<=pi;
  upper(s)=g(s)                              if a*s<=theta;
           |cos(a*s)|^(1/a^2)                 if theta<a*s<=pi.
The envelope theorems prove lower(s)<=product_i cos(a_i*s)<=upper(s)
from sum_i a_i^2=1 and |a_i|<=a. The integral here is exactly
  J(-x;a,pi/a)=integral_0^1
      max(k_-(u)*lower(pi*u/a),k_-(u)*upper(pi*u/a))du.
Define E=integral_0^1 k_-(u)*g(pi*u/a)du.

Define actual half-line integrals
  D(x)=integral_0^infinity sin(s*x)g(s)ds,
  M(x)=integral_0^infinity s*cos(s*x)g(s)ds,
  N(x)=integral_0^infinity s^2*sin(s*x)g(s)ds.
Integration by parts gives M=1-xD and N=D+xM. Moreover
  |M|<=1, |N|<=pi*phi0, D(x)<=x for x>=0,
  xD(x)<=2, N(x)<=2x for 0<=x<=1.
The last bound uses N=x+(1-x^2)D.

The actual Gaussian Fourier identities are
  integral_0^infinity cos(s*x)g(s)ds=pi*phi(x),
  integral_0^infinity s*sin(s*x)g(s)ds=pi*x*phi(x),
  integral_0^infinity [sin(s*x)/s]g(s)ds=pi*c(x).
In Lean the last identity is proved by differentiation under the
integral, using an integrable Gaussian dominator, followed by FTC.

Since
  rho(u)=1/(a*s)-a*s/3+a^2*s^2/(3*pi)+(1-u)q(a*s),
these Fourier identities give
  E=c(x)+a*phi(x)-a^2*M(x)/pi^2-a^2*x*phi(x)/3
          +a^3*N(x)/(3*pi^2)+Rq+epsilon.
The x*phi term is NEGATIVE and the N term is POSITIVE. Here
  Rq=(a/pi)*integral_0^(pi/a) (1-a*s/pi)q(a*s)sin(s*x)g(s)ds
is the actual signed remainder. Split its absolute value at s=1/a.
On the first interval use |(1-u)q(a*s)|<=(a*s)^3/30 and moment m3=2;
on the second use |(1-u)q(a*s)|<=a*s/2 and the exact tail m1(R)=g(R).
This proves
  |Rq|<=a^4/(15*pi)+(a^2/(2*pi))*e0,
  e0=g(1/a)=exp(-1/(2*a^2)).

The Gaussian truncation epsilon is the negative tail beyond R=pi/a
of the explicitly integrated baseline. For R>0 the actual tail moments
satisfy I0(R)<=g(R)/R, I1(R)=g(R), I2(R)<=(R+1/R)g(R).
Also 1/(a*s)<=1/pi when s>=R. These bounds give
  |epsilon|<=a^2*[2/pi^2+1/pi^3+2/(3*pi)+a^2/(3*pi^3)]*g(pi/a).
The bracket is at most 4 already from pi>=1 and a<=1; because a<=1/2,
the full prefactor is at most 1. Gaussian monotonicity yields
  |epsilon|<=e0.
The actual baseline estimate used in the final assembly is therefore
  E<=c(x)+a*phi(x)-a^2*M(x)/pi^2-a^2*x*phi(x)/3
        +a^3*N(x)/(3*pi^2)+a^4/(15*pi)+(1+a^2/(2*pi))*e0.

3. ACTUAL PRIMARY LOSS: TWO BOUNDS

On 0<s<1/a define
  W(s)=g(s)-cos(a*s)^(1/a^2),
  L=(a/pi)*integral_0^(1/a) max(0,-k_-(a*s/pi))*W(s)ds.
Part(A) and exp(-v)>=1-v give
  0<=W(s)<=g(s)*(a^2*s^4/12+a^4*s^6/20).

For every x>=0 use the global inequalities
  sin(s*x)>=s*x-(s*x)^3/6, cos(s*x)>=1-(s*x)^2/2.
The term h(u)sin(s*x) is controlled without assuming that sine is
positive: since h<=0 and sin(s*x)<=s*x,
  h(u)sin(s*x)>=h(u)*s*x>=-a*x*s^2/2.
Consequently
  k_-(u)>=1-u+xi-s^2*B,
  B=x^2/2+x^3/(6*a)+a*x/2.
Since 1-u+xi>=0, max(0,-k_-)<=s^2*B.
The actual half-line Gaussian moments m6=15*pi*phi0 and m8=105*pi*phi0
then give, by extending a nonnegative majorant,
  L<=phi0*(5*a^3/4+21*a^5/4)*B.                         [Dsmall]

Alternatively max(0,-k_-)<=1+1/(a*s). Using moments
m3=2, m4=3*pi*phi0, m5=8, m6=15*pi*phi0 yields
  L<=phi0*a^3/4+a^2/(6*pi)+(3/4)*phi0*a^5
                           +2*a^4/(5*pi).               [Dlarge]
The rest of the first frequency region is included in the combined
middle interval below. No x-dependent prefactor enters that remainder.

4. DIRECT REFLECTED ALIAS ESTIMATE AND COMPENSATION

Let
  A=integral_(theta/pi)^1 |k_-(u)|*|cos(pi*u)|^(1/a^2)du.
For t=1-u, beta=pi*xi, the exact reflected kernel is
  k_-(1-t)=t*cos(beta*(1-t))+rho(1-t)*sin(beta*(1-t)).
Trigonometric addition and |sin y|<=|y| imply
  |cos(beta*(1-t))|<=|cos beta|+beta*t*|sin beta|,
  |sin(beta*(1-t))|<=|sin beta|+beta*t*|cos beta|.
Part(C), followed by |cos beta|<=1 in the cubic term, gives
  |k_-(1-t)|<=t*|cos beta|+pi*(xi+delta)*t^2*|sin beta|
                       +(4*pi/3)*xi*t^3.
Since t<=1-theta/pi<1/2,
  |cos(pi*(1-t))|^(1/a^2)<=exp(-pi^2*t^2/(2*a^2)).
Reflect the actual integral, scale s=pi*t/a, and extend the nonnegative
polynomial majorant to the positive half-line. The result is
  A<=Q*|cos(pi*xi)|+K*|sin(pi*xi)|+8*xi*a^4/(3*pi^3),
  Q=a^2/pi^2, K=(phi0/pi)*(xi+delta)*a^3.

For Q>0 and K>=0,
  Q*|cos z|+K*|sin z|-Q<=K^2/(2*Q).
This follows by squaring: (Q|cos z|+K|sin z|)^2<=Q^2+K^2 and
(Q+K^2/(2Q))^2>=Q^2+K^2, with both sides nonnegative. Thus
  A-Q<=phi0^2/2*(xi+delta)^2*a^4+8*xi*a^4/(3*pi^3).
For every xi>=0 the following scalar domination holds:
  phi0^2/2*(xi+delta)^2+8*xi/(3*pi^3)
    <=phi0^2/2*(xi+eta)^2+6*(xi+1)/pi^4.
Indeed eta>=delta>=0 handles the constant term, and the coefficient
of xi is controlled by
  phi0^2*(eta-delta)+6/pi^4
    >=(1521/10000)*(16/45)+3/50 >8/81>=8/(3*pi^3).
Finally use -a^2*M(x)/pi^2=a^2*x*D(x)/pi^2-Q. The Gaussian M term
and actual alias integral together are at most
  a^2*x*D(x)/pi^2+phi0^2/2*(xi+eta)^2*a^4
                         +6*(xi+1)*a^4/pi^4.

5. EXACT ENVELOPE SPLIT AND COMPLETE ERROR BUDGET

Write F(u) for the actual maximum of the two signed characteristic
function envelopes in JUpper, and G(u)=k_-(u)*g(pi*u/a).
On 0<u<1/pi the identity
  max(k*l,k*g)=k*g+max(0,-k)*(g-l), l<=g,
identifies the integral of F-G exactly with L after s=pi*u/a.

On 1/pi<=u<=theta/pi, |k_-|<=2 and both envelope magnitudes are at
most e0. When u<=1/2 this follows from the cosine/Gaussian profile
bound. When u>=1/2, the lower-envelope magnitude is bounded by
exp(-(1-a^2)s^2/2)<=e0, because a<=1/2 and a*s>=pi/2>3/2 imply
  (1-a^2)*s^2>=1/a^2.
Thus F<=2*e0 and |G|<=2*e0, whence F-G<=4*e0. The interval has
length at most 1. On theta/pi<u<1 the two envelopes are symmetric,
so F(u)=|k_-(u)|*|cos(pi*u)|^(1/a^2). Also -G<=|G|<=2*e0, and
this interval has length at most 1. Integrating the exact three-region
split gives the checked actual inequality
  J(-x)<=E+L+A+6*e0.

Together with section 2, the error apart from a^4/(15*pi) is at most
  (7+a^2/(2*pi))*e0<=8*e0<=9*e0.
The final assembly deliberately keeps the generous 9*e0 budget.
The degree-six exponential term gives
  e0<=6!*(2*a^2)^6=46080*a^12.
At a<=7/100, exact rational arithmetic gives
  9*46080*a^8<=1/1000.
Therefore
  a^4/(15*pi)+9*e0
    <=(1/45+1/1000)*a^4<(1/40)*a^4.

For either primary-loss bound Dsmall or Dlarge, all actual integrals
now yield the following master inequality, with xi=x/a:
  F_U(x)<=a*phi(x)-c(a)-a^2*x*phi(x)/3
      +a^2*x*D(x)/pi^2+a^3*N(x)/(3*pi^2)
      +D_bound+phi0^2/2*(xi+eta)^2*a^4
      +6*(xi+1)*a^4/pi^4+a^4/40.                         [MASTER]
No master inequality is assumed in the formal theorem.

6. SMALL x: a/2<=x<=1

Use D<=x, N<=2*x, and the actual Gaussian bounds
  phi(x)<=phi0*(1-x^2/2+x^4/8),
  phi(x)>=phi0*(1-x^2/2), c(a)>=phi0*(a-a^3/6).
Take Dsmall in MASTER and divide by phi0*a^3. Since
  5/4+21*a^2/4<=32/25=:K0,
the result is at most
  P=1/6-xi^2/2-xi/3
       +a^2*[xi^4/8+xi^3/6+K0*(xi^2/2+xi^3/6+xi/2)]
       +a*[C2*xi^2+C1*xi+C0],
where
  C2=100/351+1/5,
  C1=400/1053+1/3,
  C0=5/36+200/1053+5/78.
These dominate all analytic constants using pi>=3, phi0>=39/100,
phi0<=2/5 and eta<=5/6.

For 1/2<=xi<=1, use xi^4<=xi^2, xi^3<=xi^2 in the K0 term,
and xi^3<=xi in the standalone cubic term. At a=7/100,
  1/6+a*C0<=1/5,
  -1/2+a*C2+a^2*(1/8+2*K0/3)<=-9/20,
  -1/3+a*C1+a^2*(1/6+K0/2)<=-11/40.
Hence
  P<=1/5-9*xi^2/20-11*xi/40<=-1/20.
For xi>=1 and a*xi=x<=1, use
  a^2*xi^4<=xi^2, a^2*xi^3<=xi,
and in the K0 term a^2*xi^3<=a*xi^2. The exact checks give
  -3/8+a*C2+K0*(a^2/2+a/6)<=-3/10,
  -1/6+a*C1+K0*a^2/2<=-1/10.
Thus P<=1/5-3*xi^2/10-xi/10<=-1/5. This proves
  F_U(x)<=-phi0*a^3/20 throughout a/2<=x<=1.

7. LARGE x AND THE FAR TAIL

First suppose 1<=x and a*x^2<=1. Then a^2*x<=a. Use
  phi(x)<=phi(1), xD(x)<=2, |N(x)|<=pi*phi0,
and drop the favorable term -a^2*x*phi(x)/3. Take Dlarge in MASTER
and divide by a. Since exp(-1/2)<=5/8 and phi0>=39/100,
  phi(1)-phi0<=-117/800.
All positive terms are bounded by
  R(a)=a^2/15+2*a/9+2*a^2/45
       +[a^2/10+a/18+3*a^4/10+2*a^3/15]
       +(2/25)*[1+5*a/3+25*a^3/36]
       +(2/27)*(a+a^3)+a^3/40.
Every coefficient is nonnegative. At a=7/100,
  R(a)=1553895653/13500000000,
  -117/800+R(a)<-1/40.
This proves F_U(x)<=-a/40 in this range.

For x>=0 with a*x^2>=1 we have x>=1 and c(a)>=a/3. Since
0<a<=7/100<1/12, the cubic exponential term gives
  exp(1/(2*a))>=(1/(2*a))^3/6=1/(48*a^3)>=3/a.
Consequently exp(-1/(2*a))<=a/3. The actual Gaussian Mills estimate is
  1/2-c(x)<=phi(x)/x.
It follows by splitting the actual integral of phi on the positive
half-line and applying I0(x)<=g(x)/x. Therefore
  1/2-c(x)<=phi0*g(x)/x<=g(x)
           <=exp(-1/(2*a))<=a/3<=c(a),
which gives H(x)+H(a)>=1. The probability bound by one completes
this range without any assertion about JUpper in the far tail.

8. FORMAL CORRESPONDENCE AND AUDIT NOTES

The companion Lean project proves the actual finite-law Prawitz
reduction, characteristic envelopes, Gaussian integrals, signed kernel
bounds, piecewise integral decomposition, and both master closures.
None of those analytic facts is a hypothesis of the final theorem.
Key modules:
* GaussianMoments/GaussianFourier/GaussianTail: actual half-line moments,
  Fourier identities, sine-integral/cdf identity, D/M/N and tail bounds.
* ZoneBKernel/ZoneBProfile/ZoneBIntegral: global kernel negative-part
  estimate, controlled log-cosine remainder, and actual primary losses.
* ZoneBRemainder/GaussianBaseline: actual signed cotangent remainder,
  exact Gaussian decomposition, and actual truncation error.
* ZoneBAlias/ZoneBAliasCompensation: reflected alias integral and its
  compensation with the negative Q term.
* ZoneBRegions/ZoneBDecomposition: exact JUpper comparison with E,L,A
  and the 6*e0 remainder, including all affine substitutions.
* GaussianError: exponential budget, Mills bound, and actual far-tail
  inequality H(x)+H(a)>=1.
* ZoneBClosure: both master inequalities without analytic premises.
* ZoneBTheorem: actual finite-law concentration for every x>=0 and
  0<a<=.07, combining the actual Zone A and Zone B results.

All these modules compiled with Lean 4.19 and autoImplicit=false before
this text was aligned. The separate final audit records clean-build,
axiom, signed-coefficient, normal-cdf, and nonvacuity checks. This note
alone does not establish originality or formally verify the previously
published large-a and opposite-half results.

Potential sign and statement pitfalls checked:
* J is evaluated at -x, with the explicit plus-sine upper kernel.
* The x*phi term is negative and the N term is positive.
* The direct alias coefficient is xi+delta; the checked compensation
  yields the final xi+eta coefficient.
* The closed event |X|<=x follows from Prawitz for the strict event
  X<-x and symmetry. Atoms are retained.
* Kernel endpoint values arising from total division are handled using
  almost-everywhere integral identities, not false pointwise extensions.
* The combined middle error is independent of x because |k|<=2 once
  a*s>=1. There is no unquantified polynomial-in-1/a prefactor.
* The endpoint statement is nonvacuous, for example for 256 coefficients
  all equal to 1/16, whose squared sum is exactly 1 and maximum below .07.

The standard-library rational checker verifies the displayed rational
