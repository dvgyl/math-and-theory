Keller--Klein small-coefficient upper half: zone A
2026-10-30. The actual uniform Holder-profile inequality below is Lean verified
in KellerKlein.ZoneA.holderProfile_le_c_pair (Lean 4.19.0, pinned Mathlib).
The complete small-coefficient upper-probability theorem is assembled in
KellerKlein.Main. The final fresh-build status is in verification/certificate.json.
This auxiliary derivation covers Zone A only.

Goal. For 0<a<=7/100, 0<=x<=a/2 and 0<b<=a, prove

  G(b;x,pi/a) <= [H(x)+H(a)]/2,

where H(z)=2 Phi(z)-1 and

  G(b;x,T) = integral_0^1 k(u,-x,T) |cos(bTu)|^(1/b^2) du,
  k(u,-x,T) = (1-u)cos(Tux) + rho(u)sin(Tux),
  rho(u)=(1-u)cot(pi u)+1/pi.

The endpoint kernel values are interpreted by continuous limits. All integrands
are nonnegative in this zone. Ivanisvili, section 10, combines Prawitz and Holder
to give P(|X|<=x)<=2 sup_{0<b<=a} G(b;x,pi/a). Thus the stated goal suffices.

Notation. Set xi=x/a in [0,1/2], theta=b/a in (0,1], phi0=1/sqrt(2pi),
  A=xi^2/2+xi/3+xi^3/6,
  A_l=xi^2/2+xi/2+xi^3/6,
  B=(xi^2/2+xi/3)/pi,
  C=xi^4/24+xi^3/18+xi^5/120,
  D=xi^5/(360pi).
All these coefficients are nonnegative. A<=5/16, A_l<=19/48, and C<=113/11520.

1. Kernel polynomial bounds.

For y=pi u in [0,pi], the elementary cotangent estimates are

  1/y-y/2 <= rho(u) <= 1/y-y/3+y^2/(3pi) <=1/y.

The lower bound is elementary: for y<=sqrt(2), cos y>=1-y^2/2>=0 and
sin y<=y give cot y>=1/y-y/2, hence rho(u)>=1/y-y/2. For y>=sqrt(2),
the right side is nonpositive and rho(u)>=0 (Ivanisvili Lemma10.2, or differentiate
1/pi-t cot(pi t) after reflecting u when u>=1/2). The upper bound follows from
cot y<=1/y-y/3. To prove the latter without a series, differentiate
F(y)=(1-y^2/3)sin y-y cos y: F'(y)=y(sin y-y cos y)/3>=0, since
(sin y-y cos y)'=y sin y>=0 on [0,pi].
The usual global Taylor inequalities cos z<=1-z^2/2+z^4/24,
cos z>=1-z^2/2, sin z<=z-z^3/6+z^5/120, sin z>=z-z^3/6,
and sin z<=z imply

  L(y):=1+xi-y/pi-A_l y^2 <= k(y/pi,-x,pi/a)
       <= U(y):=1+xi-y/pi-Ay^2+B y^3+C y^4+D y^7.
  0<=k<=1+xi.

Proof of the upper bound: insert
  rho<=1/y-y/3+y^2/(3pi),
apply the upper cosine polynomial to the coefficient 1-y/pi, the upper sine
polynomial to 1/y+y^2/(3pi), and the lower sine polynomial to -y/3. The omitted
y^5 terms are -xi^4/(24pi)-xi^3/(18pi), hence nonpositive.

For the lower bound, use rho>=1/y-y/2. When y<=sqrt(2), this coefficient is
nonnegative, so insert sin(xi y)>=xi y-xi^3 y^3/6 and cos>=1-xi^2y^2/2.
This produces L plus nonnegative cubic and quartic terms. When y>=sqrt(2),
insert sin(xi y)<=xi y into the nonpositive coefficient instead; the resulting
lower bound is at least L. This justifies the lower bound even when L is negative.

2. Primary peak.

Use s=pi u/a and put S=min(pi/(2b),pi/a). Then S>=1/a.
On 0<=s<=S,

  cos(bs)^(1/b^2) <= exp(-s^2/2-b^2s^4/12).

This follows from -log cos z>=z^2/2+z^4/12 for 0<=z<pi/2; at the endpoint
both sides extend by inequality. For an elementary proof, tan z>=z follows
by differentiation; then (tan z)'=1+tan(z)^2>=1+z^2 yields
tan z>=z+z^3/3. Integrate once more to obtain the log-cosine bound. Since exp(-v)<=1-v+v^2/2 for v>=0,

  k cos(bs)^(1/b^2)
  <= exp(-s^2/2)[U(as) - (b^2 s^4/12)L(as)
                   + (b^4 s^8/288)(1+xi)].

Integrating and extending the polynomial Gaussian moments to infinity gives

  G_primary <= phi0 a(1+xi)-a^2/pi^2
      +phi0 a^3[-A-theta^2(1+xi)/4]
      +(a^4/pi^2)[xi^2+2xi/3+2theta^2/3]
      +phi0 a^5[3C+5A_l theta^2/4+35(1+xi)theta^4/96]
      +(2xi^5/(15pi^2))a^8 + E.

For the simpler Gaussian-only primary bound, omit every theta term in this
formula (use cos(bs)^(1/b^2)<=exp(-s^2/2) directly).

The extension error is at most 128 a^8. Indeed, only the negative monomials
  -(a/pi)s, -Aa^2s^2, -b^2(1+xi)s^4/12
can increase the upper estimate when extending. For s>=S>=1/a,
1<=(a s)^6. Hence, for each nonnegative integer k,
  integral_S^infinity s^k exp(-s^2/2) ds <= a^6 M_{k+6}(0).
The needed Gaussian moments are M_7(0)=48, M_8(0)=105 pi phi0,
and M_10(0)=945 pi phi0. Including the prefactor a/pi and using b<=a,
  E <= (48/pi^2)a^8 + [105 A phi0+(945/12)(1+xi)phi0]a^9.
Since a<=1, A<=5/16, xi<=1/2, phi0<=2/5, and pi^2>=9,
  E/a^8 <= 48/9+105(5/16)(2/5)+(945/12)(3/2)(2/5)
         =1577/24 <66<128.
This polynomial-moment tail comparison replaces an earlier exponential-tail
argument and retains the same deliberately generous 128 a^8 error budget.

3. Alias peak.

There is no alias portion when theta<=1/2. Otherwise set
  c=pi/b-pi/a=pi(1-theta)/(theta a)>=0,
  w=pi/b-s, t=1-u=a(w-c)/pi.
On the alias range c<=w<=pi/(2b), we have 0<=t<=1/2 and
  |cos(bs)|^(1/b^2)=cos(bw)^(1/b^2)<=exp(-w^2/2).
Put beta=pi xi. Since 0<=beta<=pi/2,
  cos(beta(1-t))<=cos beta+beta t sin beta,
  sin(beta(1-t))<=sin beta.
For 0<=t<=1/2,
  rho(1-t)=1/pi-t cot(pi t)<=pi t^2/3+t^4.
A finite-Taylor proof avoids cotangent partial fractions. For z in [0,pi/2],
put w=z^2<=5/2 and Q=1-w/3-w^2/32. If Q<=0, Q sin z<=z cos z is
automatic. If Q>=0, insert cos z>=1-w/2+w^2/24-w^3/720 and
sin z<=z(1-w/6+w^2/120). The difference, divided by z, is
w^2[13/1440-11w/2880+w^2/3840], whose bracket is decreasing on [0,5/2]
and at 5/2 is 17/15360>0. Thus 1-z cot z<=z^2/3+z^4/32. Taking
z=pi t and using pi^3<32 proves the displayed rho bound.
Thus
  k(1-t,-x,pi/a)<=t cos beta+(beta+pi/3)t^2 sin beta+t^4 sin beta.

Writing M_j(c)=integral_c^infinity (w-c)^j exp(-w^2/2)dw gives
  G_alias <= (a^2/pi^2)cos beta M_1(c)
        +(a^3/pi^2)(xi+1/3)sin beta M_2(c)
        +(a^5/pi^5)sin beta M_4(c).
Since M_1(0)=1, M_2(0)=pi phi0, M_4(0)=3pi phi0,
  G_alias <= a^2 cos beta/pi^2
        +phi0 a^3(xi+1/3)sin beta/pi
        +3phi0 a^5 sin beta/pi^4.

4. theta>=19/20.

The target is at least
  phi0 a(1+xi)-phi0 a^3(1+xi^3)/6.
Combining the primary and alias bounds, using sin(pi xi)<=pi xi and discarding
the nonpositive term -a^2(1-cos(pi xi))/pi^2, the cubic coefficient is at most
  phi0 [1/6+xi^2/2-(361/400)(1+xi)/4].
This convex quadratic has maximum -449/9600 on [0,1/2] (the maximum is at 1/2).
Bounding every other coefficient at xi=1/2 and theta=1 yields

  G-[H(x)+H(a)]/2
  <= -(449/9600)phi0 a^3 + (5/(4pi^2))a^4
       +phi0(1371/1280+3/pi^4)a^5+(128+1/(240pi^2))a^8.

Using 39/100<phi0<2/5, pi^2>=9, and a<=7/100, the right side is <=-a^3/200.
This last assertion is an elementary rational inequality, recorded in the
companion certificate script.

5. theta<=19/20.

If an alias portion exists, c>=pi/(19a)>2. Write w=c+t and use
  exp(-(c+t)^2/2) <= exp(-c^2/2) exp(-t^2/2), t>=0.
Because exp(c^2/2)>=1+c^2/2>=3, this gives
  M_1(c)<=1/3, M_2(c)<=pi phi0/3, M_4(c)<=pi phi0.
These deliberately loose bounds are sufficient and avoid any additional
exponential-moment evaluation. Use the Gaussian-only primary estimate and
the target lower estimate to get

  G-[H(x)+H(a)]/2
   <= -2a^2/(3pi^2)
      +phi0 a^3[1/6+5/(18pi)]
      +7a^4/(12pi^2)
      +phi0 a^5[113/3840+1/pi^4]
      +(128+1/(240pi^2))a^8.

Using 9<=pi^2<=10, pi>=3, phi0<=2/5 and a<=7/100 makes this <=-a^2/20.
If theta<=1/2 there is no alias, so the same upper estimate remains valid.

Conclusion. Both theta branches prove the desired uniform Holder-profile bound.
Every error term above is explicit. The actual functional theorem is now proved
in Lean, with the integral changes of variables, kernel estimates, Gaussian
moments, tail comparisons, and final inequalities included in its dependency
chain. The probability reduction and Zone B (x>=a/2) are separate formal
components assembled in KellerKlein.Main. The all-coefficient two-sided
conjecture additionally uses external results and is not Lean verified here.
