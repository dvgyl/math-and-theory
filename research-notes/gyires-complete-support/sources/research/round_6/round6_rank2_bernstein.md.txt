# An exact finite certificate for rank-two matrices in orders 2–15

## Status and scope

This note proves a finite-dimensional result using exact integer coefficient certificates and an analytic reduction:

> For every doubly stochastic matrix A of rank at most two and order 2 <= n <= 15,
> per(AA^T) <= per(A).

Applying the same result to A^T gives both Gram contractions. Consequently the original Gyires inequality holds in these orders for every matrix of rank at most two, with equality only at the uniform matrix.

This is **not a proof for every order**, and it does not address unrestricted higher-rank matrices. The analytic reduction is valid in all orders; the coefficient verification has a finite upper order. No novelty claim, human peer review, or proof-assistant verification is asserted.

The finite verification uses exact integer arithmetic and checks a polynomial on its entire feasible interval through coefficient nonnegativity. It is not a finite sample of real parameter values. The program and mathematical reduction must both be correct for the computational theorem to hold.

## 1. Exact rank-two formula

Put c_n=n!/n^n. Every nonuniform rank-two doubly stochastic matrix can be written

\[
A_{ij}=\frac{x_i y_j}{t}+\frac{(1-x_i)(1-y_j)}{n-t},
\quad x,y\in[0,1]^n,\quad \sum x_i=\sum y_j=t\in(0,n).
\tag{1}
\]

Indeed its rows lie on a line in the probability simplex. Express them as convex combinations of the endpoint rows, with coefficients x_i, and use the column sums to obtain y_j. Row and column permutations may be made independently: per(A) and per(AA^T) are invariant under those operations.

Set theta=t/n, sigma=theta(1-theta), D=t(n-t), and

\[
u_k=e_k(x-\theta\mathbf1),\quad v_k=e_k(y-\theta\mathbf1),\quad
\kappa_y=\frac{\sum_j(y_j-\theta)^2}{n\sigma}.
\]

Expanding the permanent of A=J_n+n(x-theta)(y-theta)^T/D by the selected rank-one factors gives

\[
\frac{\operatorname{per}(A)}{c_n}
=\sum_{k=0}^n\frac{n^{2k}u_kv_k}{\binom nk D^k}.
\tag{2}
\]

There are k! ways to match the k selected rows and columns and (n-k)! ways to match the rest, which supplies the coefficient. Since

\[
AA^T=J_n+\frac{n\kappa_y}{D}
(x-\theta\mathbf1)(x-\theta\mathbf1)^T,
\]

the same expansion yields

\[
\frac{\operatorname{per}(AA^T)}{c_n}
=\sum_{k=0}^n\frac{n^{2k}u_k^2\kappa_y^k}{\binom nk D^k}.
\tag{3}
\]

The k=0 terms are one and k=1 terms are zero.

## 2. Why it suffices to check shifted-binomial profiles

The preceding round gave this reduction using the binomial noise operator. An elementary derivation using only (2)–(3) makes the present note independent of that operator.

Let P_x,P_y be the Poisson-binomial laws from x,y. If F_P(z)=sum_j P(j)z^j is the generating polynomial of P, then

\[
\prod_i(1+(x_i-\theta)z)
=(1-\theta z)^n F_{P_x}\!\left(1+\frac{z}{1-\theta z}\right)
=\sum_{j=0}^nP_x(j)(1-\theta z)^{n-j}(1+(1-\theta)z)^j.
\]

Thus u_k and v_k are linear functions of their probability laws when n,t are fixed. Also

\[
\kappa_y=1-\frac{\operatorname{Var}(P_y)}{n\sigma}
=-\frac{2v_2}{n\sigma}
\]

is affine in P_y and lies in [0,1]. Write w_k=1/(binomial(n,k) sigma^k)>0. The contraction deficit from (2)–(3) is

\[
E(P_x,P_y)=\sum_{k=0}^n w_k u_k^2\kappa_y^k
-\sum_{k=0}^n w_k u_kv_k.
\tag{4}
\]

It is separately convex. For fixed P_y, the first sum is a positive semidefinite quadratic form in the linear coordinates u_k. For fixed P_x, it is a sum of nonnegative multiples of convex functions kappa_y^k on [0,1]; k=0 is constant and the k=1 term vanishes. The second sum is linear in either law separately. This proves the needed convexity directly.

Fix n and t. A continuous convex functional of a Poisson-binomial law at mean t has a maximizing parameter vector with all interior parameters equal. To see this, choose a maximizer with maximal sum of squared parameters. For two distinct parameters p,q in (0,1), fix p+q and vary z=pq. The generating polynomial for this pair is affine in z, so the functional is convex in z. The current point is strictly inside the feasible z-interval; since it is a global maximum, both endpoints have the same maximum value. Moving to the smaller z endpoint puts one parameter at zero or one and raises p^2+q^2, a contradiction.

Thus each law can be replaced at a maximum by a shifted binomial. Apply the argument successively to the two laws in (4), preserving a global maximum at both steps. It suffices to consider

\[
x=(1^a,q^m,0^{n-a-m}),\quad q=(t-a)/m,
\]
\[
y=(1^c,r^\ell,0^{n-c-\ell}),\quad r=(t-c)/\ell,
\tag{5}
\]

where a,c>=0, m,ell>=1, a+m<=n, c+ell<=n, and

\[
L:=\max(a,c)\le t\le R:=\min(a+m,c+\ell).
\tag{6}
\]

This is the same classical fixed-mean extremal structure used for linear functionals by Hoeffding; the argument above establishes the convex version needed here. No assertion that E is jointly convex is needed.

### Coverage of degenerate cases and orientations

- Every nondegenerate shifted-binomial law has its mean strictly inside its support interval. A pair with a common mean therefore has L<R unless both laws are deterministic or a deterministic representation has been chosen unnecessarily narrowly.
- If L=R, both displayed profile laws are deterministic at their common mean. In that case kappa_y=1 and the two laws coincide, so the contraction is equality directly. Discarding intervals of zero length therefore omits no unproved case.
- A deterministic law delta_h with 0<h<n is the endpoint t=h of the valid profile (a,m)=(h,1). If the other law is nondegenerate at mean h, its upper support endpoint exceeds h, so this choice gives L<R. Two deterministic laws at the common mean can use the same profile and are covered by its endpoint.
- The cases t=0,n give the uniform matrix in the limiting parametrization and are immediate separately.
- The full-binomial profile (a,m)=(0,n) yields x_i=theta and hence A=J_n, regardless of y. These profiles are omitted from the finite enumeration because the contraction is equality.
- Every ordered pair of remaining profiles with L<R is checked. No transposition or orientation is discarded. Permuting entries within the parameter vectors corresponds to independent row and column permutations and preserves both quantities in the contraction.

Endpoint values follow by continuity from the polynomial certificate on the interval interior. Thus the enumeration covers every rank-two matrix at the stated order.

## 3. Homogeneous integer polynomials

Let U,V>=0 and homogenize t=(LU+RV)/(U+V). All expressions below are homogeneous polynomials in U,V. Define

\[
\mathscr D=(LU+RV)((n-L)U+(n-R)V),
\]

\[
\mathscr K=\ell\mathscr D
-n((L-c)U+(R-c)V)((\ell+c-L)U+(\ell+c-R)V).
\tag{7}
\]

Then kappa_y=mathscrK/(ell mathscrD) when U+V>0 and 0<t<n.

Let X_k be the kth elementary symmetric polynomial of these n linear forms:

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
m((n-L)U+(n-R)V),& a\text{ copies},\\
(n(L-a)-mL)U+(n(R-a)-mR)V,&m\text{ copies},\\
-m(LU+RV),& n-a-m\text{ copies}.
\end{array}
\tag{8}
\]

These are n m (U+V)(x_i-theta). Define Y_k by replacing a,m with c,ell. Put

\[
\mathscr C=\sum_{k=0}^n
k!(n-k)!(m\ell)^{n-k}X_kY_k\mathscr D^{n-k},
\tag{9}
\]

\[
\mathscr G=\sum_{k=0}^n
k!(n-k)!m^{2(n-k)}\ell^{n-k}
X_k^2\mathscr K^k\mathscr D^{2(n-k)}.
\tag{10}
\]

Their degrees are 2n and 4n. Equations (2)–(3) give exactly

\[
\frac{\operatorname{per}(A)}{c_n}
=\frac{\mathscr C}{n!m^n\ell^n\mathscr D^n},
\qquad
\frac{\operatorname{per}(AA^T)}{c_n}
=\frac{\mathscr G}{n!m^{2n}\ell^n\mathscr D^{2n}}.
\tag{11}
\]

Thus contraction is equivalent, in the interval interior, to nonnegativity of the homogeneous integer polynomial

\[
\boxed{\mathscr H=m^n\mathscr C\mathscr D^n-\mathscr G.}
\tag{12}
\]

The strong squared inequality is equivalent to nonnegativity of

\[
\boxed{\mathscr S=\mathscr C^2-n!\ell^n\mathscr G.}
\tag{13}
\]

Every denominator cleared in (11) is strictly positive for 0<t<n. The arrays in the code are the ordinary monomial coefficients of U^(4n-j)V^j. These have the same signs as the Bernstein coefficients on [L,R], since the conversion only divides by positive binomial factors.

## 4. Exact finite coefficient verification

The program `round6_rank2_bernstein.py` constructs (7)–(13) with Python integers. It checks every coefficient of both mathscrH and mathscrS for every ordered profile pair in (5)–(6), in orders 2 through 15. All checked coefficients are nonnegative.

The numbers of oriented profile intervals checked at orders 2 through 15 are

\[
2,15,51,126,260,477,805,1276,1926,2795,3927,5370,7176,9401.
\]

In total, 33,607 ordered profile intervals and 3,524,206 integer coefficients were checked, split equally between the two deficits. The complete report is `round6_rank2_bernstein_certificate.json`; the recorded run took approximately 105 seconds in the working environment. Coefficient digests are recorded per order for reproducibility.

The independent `round6_rank2_bernstein_fraction_check.py` instead builds (2)–(3) in the ordinary power basis in t over rational numbers, substitutes t=L+(R-L)z, and converts to Bernstein degree 4n. The saved comparison checks every resulting coefficient against the homogeneous integer implementation, including its exact normalization, for all 454 profile intervals in orders 2–6: 20,452 coefficients agree exactly. The report is `round6_rank2_fraction_comparison.json`. This implementation is substantially slower at larger orders.

To reproduce the saved checks, run from the directory containing the scripts:

```bash
python3 round6_rank2_bernstein.py --first 2 --last 15
python3 round6_rank2_bernstein_fraction_check.py --first 2 --last 6
```

The primary script's optional sampling mode is for exploration only; the saved certificate uses exhaustive enumeration, with no `--samples` argument. The scripts use only the Python standard library.

The finite arithmetic verification proves nonnegativity on each entire real interval, not only at finitely many t-values. Combined with Section 2, it proves the contraction for all rank-two doubly stochastic matrices in the stated finite order range. It does not prove coefficient positivity when n>15.

## 5. Original Gyires and equality in these orders

Apply contraction to A and A^T, and permanent Cauchy–Schwarz to A^2. If d=per(A),

\[
\operatorname{per}(AA^T),\operatorname{per}(A^TA),\operatorname{per}(A^2)\le d.
\]

The original Gyires deficit is therefore at least 4d(d-c_n).

For nonuniform rank-two A, both centered parameter vectors in (1) are nonzero, so kappa_x,kappa_y>0. In (3) the k=2 term is strictly positive because

\[
u_2=-\tfrac12\sum_i(x_i-\theta)^2\ne0.
\]

All terms in (3) are nonnegative. Hence per(AA^T)>c_n; contraction gives d>c_n. This proves strict original Gyires away from J_n without needing to invoke a separate equality theorem for the van der Waerden bound. A rank-one doubly stochastic matrix is J_n, and J_n attains equality.

## 6. The unproved all-order statement

The following would complete the strong rank-two case in every dimension:

> For every admissible n,a,m,c,ell, every monomial coefficient of mathscrH in (12) is nonnegative.

This coefficient assertion is stronger than the needed pointwise inequality. Its observed validity through order 15 does not prove it in arbitrary order. No uniform coefficient formula with a proved sign, induction in the multiplicities, or analytic proof of the corresponding one-parameter inequality has been obtained here.

The all-order rank-two conjecture and the unrestricted Gyires conjecture therefore remain unresolved by this note.
