# Round 4: an exact obstruction to boundary-directed deletion

## Status

The unrestricted Gyires inequality and the adaptive **maximum over all coordinates** deletion lemma remain unresolved. This round establishes an exact counterexample to a new proposed shortcut: a coordinate with zero pure-power coefficient need not satisfy the prescribed deletion bound. The counterexample can have **exactly one** zero pure-power coefficient, with every other coefficient positive. Consequently, taking the maximum only over zero-pure coordinates also fails.

The example is an ordinary doubly stochastic product polynomial, so the obstruction lies inside the original matrix class and inside the actual induction closure. It is not an artifact of passing to a larger Lorentzian class. The original Gyires inequality and both strong Gram inequalities hold for the example.

## 1. Definitions and the proposed shortcut

For a degree-n homogeneous polynomial p in n variables, write

\[
N(p)=\sum_{|\alpha|=n}\alpha!p_\alpha^2,
\qquad q_j=\left.\partial_jp\right|_{x_j=0},
\qquad g_n=\left(\frac{n-1}{n}\right)^{n-1}.
\]

If positive D capacity-balances q_j, define

\[
H_j(p)=\frac{N(q_j(Dx))}{(\prod_iD_i)^2}.
\]

The unresolved adaptive lemma asks whether max_j H_j(p) is at least g_n N(p) for every balanced stable p. The shortcut tested here was the stronger prescribed-coordinate statement

\[
p_{ne_j}=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad H_j(p)\ge g_nN(p).
\tag{1}
\]

The examples below disprove (1), and also disprove the variant that takes the maximum only over coordinates with p_(ne_j)=0.

## 2. A small rational matrix and a quartic capacity parameter

Set

\[
M=\begin{pmatrix}
0&0&5&3\\
3&0&0&5\\
2&3&3&0\\
3&5&0&0
\end{pmatrix},\qquad A=M/8.
\]

Every row and column of M sums to 8, so A is doubly stochastic. Let

\[
p_M(x)=\prod_{i=1}^4\sum_{j=1}^4M_{ij}x_j.
\]

Using p_M rather than p_A multiplies both H and N by the same factor 8^8 and therefore leaves their ratio unchanged. Since M_11=0, the pure coefficient [x_1^4]p_M is zero. Direct integer coefficient expansion gives

\[
N(p_M)=2,276,500.
\]

For the first-coordinate deletion, write the remaining variables as (y,z,w). Then

\[
q_1(y,z,w)
=(5z+3w)(45y^2+45yz+95yw+45zw).
\tag{2}
\]

Let t be the unique positive root of

\[
f(t)=19t^4+9t^3-15t-15=0.
\tag{3}
\]

There is exactly one positive root by Descartes' rule of signs, and exact rational evaluation gives

\[
\frac{1,021,945}{1,000,000}<t<\frac{1,021,946}{1,000,000}.
\tag{4}
\]

### Verification of the capacity scaling

The scaling D=(t,1,t^2) balances q_1. For a polynomial q with nonnegative coefficients, the capacity objective

\[
(z_1,z_2,z_3)\longmapsto\log q(e^{z_1},e^{z_2},e^{z_3})-z_1-z_2-z_3
\]

is convex. Thus any positive stationary point is a global minimizer.

Writing the quadratic factor of (2) as Q, the y-balance equation is yQ_y=Q. At D it reduces to 45t^2=45t^2. For the w-balance equation, the numerator of wq_w-q at D is

\[
15t(19t^4+9t^3-15t-15),
\]

which vanishes by (3). Homogeneity then supplies the z-balance equation. The affine span of the support is the full degree-three hyperplane, so the minimizer is unique up to common scaling.

### Exact evaluation of the deletion norm

Expanding (2), scaling by D, and dividing by (t\cdot1\cdot t^2)^2 gives

\[
H_1(p_M)=
\frac{101250}{t^4}+
\frac{202500}{t^2}+372100+72900t^2+162450t^4.
\tag{5}
\]

Every coefficient in this Laurent polynomial is positive. Substitution of the rational interval (4), with the endpoint appropriate to the sign of each exponent, proves

\[
0.9497577785<
\frac{H_1(p_M)}{(27/64)N(p_M)}
<0.9497594293<1.
\tag{6}
\]

The decimals in (6) describe exact rational interval endpoints saved in the certificate. The decisive comparison is between fractions, not floating-point values. Numerically, t is about 1.0219453613 and H_1 is about 912146.6654, whereas

\[
g_4N(p_M)=\frac{27}{64}\,2,276,500=960398.4375.
\]

This disproves (1) in order four.

It also rules out the stronger degree-sensitive proposal with g_(deg x_1 p)=g_3=4/9, because deg x_1 p=3 and g_3>g_4.

## 3. A matrix with exactly one zero entry

To show that choosing some other zero-pure coordinate cannot repair the shortcut, set

\[
B=\begin{pmatrix}
0&1/3&1/3&1/3\\
1/3&2/9&2/9&2/9\\
1/3&2/9&2/9&2/9\\
1/3&2/9&2/9&2/9
\end{pmatrix}
\]

and

\[
A'=\frac{99}{100}A+\frac1{100}B
=\frac1{7200}
\begin{pmatrix}
0&24&4479&2697\\
2697&16&16&4471\\
1806&2689&2689&16\\
2697&4471&16&16
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{7}
\]

This matrix is doubly stochastic and its only zero entry is a'_11. Its product polynomial has exactly one zero coefficient among all degree-four monomials: [x_1^4]p_(A')=0. Indeed, any other monomial has an occurrence of a variable other than x_1 that can be assigned to row one; all entries in the remaining rows are positive.

The exact rational certificate below proves

\[
0.9555806993<
\frac{H_1(p_{A'})}{(27/64)N(p_{A'})}
<0.9558746601<1.
\tag{8}
\]

Thus the maximum over zero-pure coordinates is also below the proposed bound, because coordinate one is the only such coordinate.

### Exact existence certificate for the capacity minimizer

Let M'=7200A', expand p_(M') with integer coefficients, and let q be its first-coordinate deletion. Fix the gauge D=(y,z,1). The capacity equations are

\[
F_1(y,z)=yq_y(y,z,1)-q(y,z,1)=0,
\]

\[
F_2(y,z)=zq_z(y,z,1)-q(y,z,1)=0.
\]

The certificate uses the rational box

\[
X=[0.9789,0.9791]\times[0.9584,0.9586],
\]

center c=(979/1000,1917/2000), and radius 1/10000 in each coordinate. It computes the exact rational inverse R=F'(c)^(-1) and an interval enclosure J(X) for the Jacobian over X. Exact interval arithmetic gives

\[
c-RF(c)+(I-RJ(X))(X-c)\subset\operatorname{int}X.
\tag{9}
\]

For orientation, the two coordinate intervals of this enclosed image are approximately

\[
[0.9789782450,0.9789783502],\qquad
[0.9584771819,0.9584772722].
\]

Equation (9) proves that the continuous map x↦x−RF(x) sends X into itself. Brouwer's fixed-point theorem gives a zero of F in X; R is nonsingular by an exact determinant check. The convex log-capacity objective makes this root a global capacity minimizer. The deletion q has all ten ternary cubic coefficients positive, so the minimizer is unique modulo common scaling.

Finally, the certificate bounds

\[
\frac{N(q(yY,zZ,W))}{(yz)^2}
=\sum_{a+b+c=3}a!b!c!q_{abc}^{\,2}y^{2a-2}z^{2b-2}
\]

throughout X by exact interval arithmetic. Its upper endpoint is strictly below (27/64)N(p_(M')), proving (8).

The same script computes all four permanents in the original Gyires inequality by exact integer arithmetic. Both strong Gram gaps and the original Gyires gap are strictly positive for A'. This example disproves an auxiliary deletion rule, **not** the original conjecture or the maximum-over-all-coordinates lemma.

## 4. Capacity balancing can increase an individual deletion norm

The first example also invalidates a possible shortcut from raw deletion norms to capacity-scaled norms. Direct expansion gives

\[
N(q_1)=911200.
\]

The exact lower interval bound in (5) is strictly greater than 911200. Therefore

\[
H_1(p_M)>N(q_1).
\]

Capacity balancing does not always decrease a deletion's gauge-normalized Fock norm, even for a deletion of a doubly stochastic product polynomial. This says nothing by itself about the sum of all deletion norms.

## 5. Targeted searches in the actual induction closure

The exploratory optimizer starts with a positive product polynomial of degree d+r in d+r variables, extracts exponent one from r designated variables, and capacity-balances the remaining degree-d polynomial in d variables. This is the actual closure needed by the induction argument. Although the initial row factors need not already be doubly stochastic, matrix scaling can make them so; initial positive column scalings only rescale the extracted polynomial and its remaining variables, and these effects are absorbed by the subsequent balancing.

`round4_nested_product.cpp` performs the coefficient extraction by dynamic programming and computes its reverse derivatives. `round4_nested_deletion_search.py` differentiates through both the outer and the deletion capacity minimizers using their covariance Hessians. Independent finite-direction checks gave gradient errors below 1.5e-9. Three small integer input checks matched naive exact coefficient expansion with zero error; these are in `round4_nested_kernel_validation.json`.

The initial average-deletion searches used dense asymmetric inputs, sparse supports, almost-decomposed cycles, and conditioning variables competing for a small set of rows. They covered residual degrees 3,4,6,8 with up to eight singleton deletions. No average- or maximum-over-all-coordinates failure was found. Dense runs tended to the multinomial equality polynomial; sparse runs also found local minima above one. This is numerical optimization evidence only.

The prescribed zero-pure search enforced r+1 factors omitting the selected variable, so the residual pure coefficient vanished exactly. It immediately exposed the order-four product counterexample above. Additional numerical prescribed-coordinate failures in residual degrees five and six are saved, but the small exact certificate already settles that proposed rule and no further such search is needed.

## 6. Large-degree concentrated families

A separate compressed calculation uses

\[
p(x)=e_n(\underbrace{x_1,\ldots,x_1}_{m_1},\ldots,
\underbrace{x_n,\ldots,x_n}_{m_n}),
\]

followed by capacity balancing. These are also in the product-deletion closure: with M=Σm_i, start with M factors x_i+(y_1+⋯+y_(M−n)) and extract every y_j once. The result is (M−n)! times the displayed elementary-symmetric polynomial.

For two multiplicity classes, balance reduces to one scalar noncentral-hypergeometric mean equation. The Fock norm and both types of deletions reduce to univariate coefficient products. The case m=0 in the saved data denotes the infinite-multiplicity Poisson limit; finite m=1 gives a deterministic occupancy after balancing and is handled as a boundary limit.

The structured sweep in `round4_conditional_binomial.py` has 320 parameter cases at orders 3,4,8,16,32,64,128,256. It varies a small-capacity class against a large-capacity or Poisson reservoir, from one exceptional coordinate through a macroscopic fraction of the variables. No average, maximum, or final Fock violation was found. At order256 the smallest average ratio was approximately 1.00003919. These are numerical values, not an all-order theorem. Four small finite examples were independently expanded into their full multivariate coefficients; their ratios agreed within 6e-14, recorded in `round4_conditional_binomial_validation.json`.

## 6A. Feasible Lorentzian boundary optimization

A direct coefficient search in degree four used all 35 coefficients, fixed the pure coefficient of x_1^4 to zero, required every other coefficient to be positive, imposed the four linear balance equations, and constrained all 10 quadratic derivative Hessians to have at most one positive eigenvalue. All eight initial SLSQP outputs were infeasible and were discarded; their low objective values are not counterexamples.

The replacement algorithm uses the equivalent centered Schur matrix condition. For each symmetric quadratic Hessian H with 1^T H1>0, the one positive eigenvalue condition is

\[
\frac{(H\mathbf1)(H\mathbf1)^T}{\mathbf1^TH\mathbf1}-H\succeq0
\quad\hbox{on }\mathbf1^\perp.
\]

A log-determinant barrier on these restricted matrices keeps every accepted iterate strictly Lorentzian, while an affine nullspace enforces balance and the vanished coefficient numerically. Strictly Lorentzian starting arrays use

\[
p_\alpha\propto\frac{\lambda^{\sum_i\binom{\alpha_i}{2}}}{\alpha!},
\quad0<\lambda<1,
\]

with the selected pure monomial omitted, then are capacity-balanced. Before the omission, each quadratic derivative Hessian is diagonally congruent to J−(1−λ)I. The omitted corner subtracts a positive diagonal rank-one matrix from one such Hessian and preserves its single positive eigenvalue. Small asymmetric perturbations were accepted only after checking strict feasibility.

Four degree-four starts reached minimum deletion-average ratio approximately 1.00392342. Two degree-five starts, using 126 coefficients and 35 Hessian constraints, reached approximately 1.00032812 and1.00033093. No average or unrestricted maximum violation was found. The reported best points have positive restricted Schur eigenvalues and balance residuals below 3e-13; these remain numerical observations, not global certificates. See `round4_lorentzian_barrier.py`, `round4_lorentzian_boundary.py`, and their JSON outputs.

The limiting shape resembles p=(s+t x)^n−(t x)^n with s the sum of the other variables. For this polynomial every singleton deletion is a pure power of a positive linear form. Consequently every capacity-scaled deletion norm satisfies H_j=p_center²/c_(n−1), so every deletion ratio equals the final Fock target ratio. This family cannot separate the auxiliary deletion lemma from the final norm inequality.

## 6B. Independent audit of an all-order scalar theorem

The separate note `round4_scalar_ulc.md` proves an all-order likelihood-ratio inequality for mean-one ultra-log-concave distributions relative to Bin(n,1/n), and thereby proves the Fock inequality for every one-special-variable Lorentzian polynomial. I independently audited its compactness, localization perturbation, truncated-tail bounds, equality cases, and polynomial identities and found no gap. The full independent review is `round4_scalar_ulc_independent_audit.md`. This is a valid all-order family theorem; it does not establish the unrestricted multivariate or matrix assertion.

## 6C. Multivariate localization obstruction and the repeated-column theorem

The follow-up note `round4_multivariate_localization.md` gives an exact obstruction to porting the scalar perturbation proof directly to all Lorentzian polynomials. The balanced product p_0=(x+y)(y+z)(z+x) has no nonzero two-sided supported linear perturbation preserving the central coefficient, balance, and all Lorentzian Hessian conditions. The first-order kernel constraints leave only the alternating direction V=(x−y)(x−z)(y−z); along it every derivative Hessian has determinant −64t² and gains a second positive eigenvalue. The one-coordinate marginals and every binary coefficient-line log-concavity condition nevertheless remain unchanged or valid. At t=1/2, the normalized polynomial violates the desired Fock inequality by exactly 1/288. This is a counterexample to the weaker scalar/marginal conditions, not a Lorentzian or Gyires counterexample. The standard-library checker is `round4_localization_obstruction.py`, with exact records in its JSON output.

The separate theorem `round4_one_special_matrix.md` proves both Gram inequalities and original Gyires in every order for doubly stochastic matrices with n−1 identical columns or rows. I independently checked its counting formulas, quantitative one-success lower bounds, endpoint estimates, equality cases, and final Cauchy–Schwarz argument. The review is `round4_one_special_matrix_audit.md`.

## 7. Reproduction and limitations

The decisive certificate requires only the Python standard library:

```text
python round4_zero_pure_certificate.py
```

Its complete exact rational records are in `round4_zero_pure_certificate.json`. The certificate's mathematical use of the interval self-map and convex capacity objective is explained in Section3; there is no reliance on a numerical optimizer in the decisive comparisons.

To rebuild and run the numerical closure optimizer:

```text
g++ -O3 -std=c++17 -shared -fPIC round4_nested_product.cpp -o round4_nested_product.so
OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python round4_nested_deletion_search.py --d 4 --r 0 --starts 1 --iterations 250 --which prescribed --zero-pure --parameterization linear
```

The exploratory Python scripts require NumPy and SciPy. The `.so` file is a local compiled artifact; the C++ source is the portable deliverable. The large-degree script can be run independently:

```text
OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python round4_conditional_binomial.py
```

The **maximum over all coordinates** deletion lemma, its average variant, the proposed uniform-power boundary reduction as a route to a full theorem, and unrestricted Gyires remain unproved. The zero-pure counterexample means that any successful boundary induction must allow a different coordinate choice or a different argument; pure-coefficient vanishing alone does not supply the needed deletion estimate.
