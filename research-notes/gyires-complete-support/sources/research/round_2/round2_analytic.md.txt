# Round 2: analytic routes and independent review of an all-order theorem

## Status

The conjecture for arbitrary doubly stochastic matrices is **not proved** here. The useful positive result in this round is an all-order theorem for matrices

\[
 aI+bQ+cQ^2,\qquad a,b,c\geq 0,
\]

where \(Q\) is any permutation matrix. The companion computation supplies the finite-order certificates. This note independently checks the analytic tail, the normalization, the transfer matrices, the small-cycle issues, and the passage to an arbitrary permutation. It also records why several proposed routes to the full conjecture do not yet close.

**Subsequent improvement:** Section 7 independently reviews a sharp, entirely analytic spectral bound \(1+\sqrt2\). It supersedes the \(5/2\) certificate and lowers the tail threshold from 70 to 48. Sections 3–4 are retained as an independent backup argument.

Write \(c_n=n!/n^n\). The stronger inequality under consideration is

\[
 \operatorname{per}(A)^2\geq c_n\operatorname{per}(AA^T). \tag{S}
\]

The statement is homogeneous, so row sum one is unnecessary for the special family. After division by \(a+b+c\), it is a doubly stochastic family. Applying (S) also to the transpose and using permanent Cauchy–Schwarz gives the original Gyires inequality.

## 1. The cyclic transfer identity

Let \(P\) be the permutation matrix of a cycle of length \(n\), and put

\[
 C=aP^{-1}+bI+cP.
\]

Multiplication on the left by \(P\) changes neither its permanent nor the permanent of its Gram matrix, and gives \(aI+bP+cP^2\).

For a band with offsets \(-r,\ldots,r\), use a state consisting of the occupied/unoccupied status of \(2r\) consecutive column slots. Before row \(i\) is processed, these are columns \(i-r,\ldots,i+r-1\). Choosing offset \(j-r\), where \(0\leq j\leq 2r\), is legal exactly when its slot is unoccupied. The oldest slot must be occupied after the choice, since no later row can reach it. The transition then discards that slot and shifts the window one step.

This is exactly the transition rule in `round2_circ_transfer.py`: for a mask \(m\), choose an unset bit \(j\), set it, require the resulting least significant bit to be one, and shift right. The transition weight is the band coefficient at offset \(j-r\).

A periodic closed walk of length \(n\) specifies one column choice for each row. The occupation rule says each column is selected exactly once. Conversely, a cyclic permutation assignment determines its occupation states uniquely. One way to make the periodic boundary precise is to lift the assignment to a map \(f:\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z\) with \(f(i+n)=f(i)+n\). A bijection of residues with a chosen allowed offset for each row gives a bijection of \(\mathbb Z\); its occupied slots give the closed walk. Thus

\[
 \operatorname{per}(C)=\operatorname{tr}(T_C^n),\qquad
 \operatorname{per}(CC^T)=\operatorname{tr}(T_G^n). \tag{1}
\]

For \(n\geq5\), all five Gram offsets are distinct modulo \(n\), so there are no aliasing conventions to discuss. Direct symbolic checks also verified (1) for \(n=1,2,3,4\) when coincident offsets are added in the matrix entries.

The number of occupied slots is preserved by a transition. Hence the Gram transfer matrix decomposes into blocks of dimensions

\[
1,4,6,4,1.
\]

Set

\[
d=a^2+b^2+c^2,\qquad e=b(a+c),\qquad f=ac.
\]

The Gram band is \((f,e,d,e,f)\). In increasing-mask order, the nontrivial blocks are

\[
T_1=\begin{pmatrix}
 e&d&e&f\\ f&0&0&0\\0&f&0&0\\0&0&f&0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
T_2=\begin{pmatrix}
d&e&0&f&0&0\\
e&0&e&0&f&0\\
f&0&0&0&0&0\\
0&e&d&0&0&f\\
0&f&0&0&0&0\\
0&0&f&0&0&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The remaining four-dimensional block is \(T_1^T\); the singleton blocks equal \(f\). I checked these block entries directly against the mask rule, independently of the spectral certificate.

## 2. A lower exponential bound, including odd orders

The four-state transfer matrix for \(C\) has singleton blocks \(a,c\) and a two-dimensional block with characteristic polynomial

\[
 z^2-bz-ac.
\]

Consequently, with

\[
 \lambda_\pm=\frac{b\pm\sqrt{b^2+4ac}}2,
\]

we have the exact formula

\[
 \operatorname{per}(C)=a^n+c^n+\lambda_+^n+\lambda_-^n. \tag{2}
\]

Define

\[
 L=\max\{a,c,\lambda_+\}.
\]

Then

\[
 \operatorname{per}(C)\geq L^n. \tag{3}
\]

The only delicate point is the negative \(\lambda_-\) term when \(n\) is odd. Since

\[
 |\lambda_-|\leq\sqrt{ac},\qquad
 a^n+c^n\geq2(ac)^{n/2},
\]

we have \(a^n+c^n+\lambda_-^n\geq0\), which proves the \(\lambda_+^n\) lower bound. The \(a^n\) and \(c^n\) lower bounds follow directly from the corresponding permutation terms in the nonnegative permanent. For even \(n\), all four terms of (2) are nonnegative. These arguments also cover zero coefficients.

If \(L=0\), the matrix is zero and the desired inequality is immediate. Suppose \(L>0\) and scale all three coefficients by \(L\). Dropping primes from the normalized coefficients, we obtain

\[
 0\leq a,c\leq1,
 \qquad b+ac\leq1. \tag{4}
\]

Indeed, the positive root of \(z^2-bz-ac\) is at most one precisely when \(1-b-ac\geq0\).

For fixed \(a,c\), increase \(b\) to \(1-ac\). This only increases \(d,e\) and leaves \(f\) unchanged. Thus it increases the Gram transfer matrix entrywise. Perron monotonicity reduces the spectral upper bound to the square

\[
 0\leq a,c\leq1,\qquad b=1-ac. \tag{5}
\]

It would be incorrect to impose equality in (5) directly from normalization; the intervening monotonicity step is essential.

## 3. Independent exact audit of the spectral bound

After substitution (5),

\[
 d=1+a^2+c^2-2ac+a^2c^2,
\quad e=a+c-a^2c-ac^2,
\quad f=ac.
\]

The certificate in `round2_spectral_certificate.json` proves that every leading principal minor of \(5I-2T_1\) and \(5I-2T_2\) is strictly positive on \([0,1]^2\).

For a polynomial \(p(a,c)=\sum p_{kl}a^kc^l\) of bidegree \((m,r)\), its coefficient in the tensor-product Bernstein basis is

\[
 \beta_{ij}=\sum_{k\leq i,\ l\leq j}
 p_{kl}\frac{\binom{i}{k}}{\binom{m}{k}}
       \frac{\binom{j}{l}}{\binom{r}{l}}.
\]

All Bernstein basis functions are nonnegative and sum to one on the unit square. Hence \(p\geq\min\beta_{ij}\). In this certificate every minor already has strictly positive Bernstein coefficients on the entire square: **subdivision is not needed**.

I wrote `round2_analytic_review.py` to recompute the determinants using a different algorithm, subset dynamic programming rather than permutation expansion, and independently convert them to Bernstein coefficients. It obtains the following exact minima:

| Block | Leading-minor sizes | Minimum Bernstein coefficients |
|---|---|---|
| \(T_1\) | 1, 2, 3, 4 | \(3,\ 37/3,\ 65,\ 325\) |
| \(T_2\) | 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 | \(1,\ 1,\ 5,\ 25,\ 125,\ 609\) |

All recomputed polynomials and minima agree exactly with the certificate. The audit uses integer and rational arithmetic only.

### Why positive leading minors suffice here

For a general real matrix, positive leading principal minors do not imply positive definiteness or positive stability. Here the matrices are **Z-matrices**: their off-diagonal entries are nonpositive.

Gaussian elimination without pivoting has positive pivots \(\Delta_k/\Delta_{k-1}\), because all leading minors \(\Delta_k\) are positive. A Schur complement of a positive diagonal pivot preserves the nonpositive off-diagonal signs. Thus the resulting triangular factors have positive diagonal entries and nonpositive off-diagonal entries; their inverses are nonnegative. Consequently

\[
 (5I-2T)^{-1}\geq0.
\]

In fact \(x=(5I-2T)^{-1}\mathbf1\) is strictly positive, since the inverse has positive diagonal. It satisfies

\[
 Tx=\tfrac52x-\tfrac12\mathbf1<\tfrac52x.
\]

The Perron comparison bound yields \(\rho(T)<5/2\). This applies to both blocks. The transpose block has the same spectrum, and the singleton blocks have eigenvalue \(f\leq1\). Hence the full normalized Gram transfer matrix satisfies

\[
 \rho(T_G)<\frac52. \tag{6}
\]

Restoring the scale gives \(\rho(T_G)<(5/2)L^2\).

## 4. The analytic tail starts at order 70

A matrix of dimension 16 has

\[
 |\operatorname{tr}(T_G^n)|
 \leq16\rho(T_G)^n.
\]

No diagonalizability is needed: the trace of a matrix power is the sum of the powers of its eigenvalues with algebraic multiplicity. Combining (1), (3), and (6),

\[
 c_n\operatorname{per}(CC^T)
 \leq16c_n\left(\frac52\right)^nL^{2n}
 \leq16c_n\left(\frac52\right)^n\operatorname{per}(C)^2.
\]

Put \(r_n=16(n!/n^n)(5/2)^n\). Exact arithmetic gives

\[
 r_{70}=
 \frac{41050352053065672562043322738282433776620375867720479437459869384765625}
 {42840453709319069452619499814981916643125523157090608114850969803554816}
 <1.
\]

Numerically this is approximately \(0.9582146896\), but the rational comparison is what is used. Moreover,

\[
 \frac{r_{n+1}}{r_n}
 =\frac52\left(\frac n{n+1}\right)^n
 \leq\frac52\left(\frac67\right)^6
 =\frac{116640}{117649}<1
 \qquad(n\geq6).
\]

The displayed monotonicity follows from the standard elementary monotonicity of \((1+1/n)^n\). Therefore (S) holds for every \(n\geq70\), uniformly for all nonnegative \(a,b,c\). It is strict for a nonzero matrix in this range.

The companion exact coefficient computation covers \(5\leq n\leq100\), so it more than overlaps the tail. My additional direct symbolic checks cover \(n=3,4\): all six ordering chambers have nonnegative coefficients, with minimum nonzero coefficient 12 for \(n=3\), and 160 or 232 for \(n=4\).

The parent's independent JavaScript implementation, `round2_independent_finite.mjs`, has now checked \(3\leq n\leq69\) using native exact `BigInt` arithmetic. It constructs polynomials directly in ordered-gap coordinates and obtains Gram traces from Newton identities, so it neither takes powers of the original transfer matrices nor uses the original gap-conversion routine. It checks 693,249 coefficient positions, finds no negative coefficient, and agrees with every primary minimum for the overlapping orders. Its three chambers cover all six by the symmetry interchanging \(a,c\).

I also independently expanded the characteristic polynomials used in that implementation by a subset-determinant algorithm. They are exactly

\[
 \det(zI-T_1)=z^4-ez^3-dfz^2-ef^2z-f^4,
\]

\[
 \det(zI-T_2)=z^6-dz^5-(e^2+f^2)z^4-2e^2fz^3
 -(e^2f^2+f^4)z^2+df^4z+f^6.
\]

The independent square-permanent recurrence is also correct: the cross term \(2(ac)^n+2(-ac)^n\) vanishes for odd \(n\), and is \(4(ac)^n\) for even \(n\). This accounts for the parity condition in the JavaScript checker.

For \(n=2\), put \(d=a+c\). Then

\[
 \operatorname{per}(C)=b^2+d^2,
\qquad
 \operatorname{per}(CC^T)=(b^2+d^2)^2+4b^2d^2,
\]

and the unnormalized gap is

\[
 2^2\operatorname{per}(C)^2-2!\operatorname{per}(CC^T)
 =2(b^2-d^2)^2\geq0.
\]

For \(n=1\), equality is immediate. This avoids relying on any historical low-order theorem.

## 5. Arbitrary permutations, direct sums, and scope

If \(Q\) has cycle lengths \(m_1,\ldots,m_s\), the matrix \(aI+bQ+cQ^2\) becomes block diagonal after a simultaneous row and column permutation. The permanent and Gram permanent factor over these blocks. Applying the cyclic inequality gives

\[
 \operatorname{per}(A)^2
 \geq\left(\prod_{j=1}^s c_{m_j}\right)\operatorname{per}(AA^T).
\]

For every partition \(n=\sum_jm_j\),

\[
 c_n\leq\prod_jc_{m_j}.
\]

To see this, the probability of observing counts \((m_1,\ldots,m_s)\) in \(n\) independent trials with category probabilities \(m_j/n\) is at most one. Written out, that inequality is precisely the displayed comparison of constants. Thus the theorem extends to every permutation \(Q\), with arbitrary cycle structure.

It also extends to direct sums whose individual blocks have different triples \((a,b,c)\), after any independent row and column permutations. This follows from the same product-of-constants argument; the triples do not need to agree between blocks.

There is **no justified closure under matrix multiplication**. In particular, knowing the theorem for two trinomials in \(Q\) does not prove it for their product. That would require additional control relating the permanent of a product and the permanents of its factors; normalized submultiplicativity is itself equivalent to the stronger full conjecture. Positive-coefficient factorizations therefore do not currently extend this proof to arbitrary circulants.

## 6. Attempts at the unrestricted conjecture

### 6.1 Capacity and productization

For a doubly stochastic matrix, let

\[
 P_A(x)=\prod_{i=1}^n\left(\sum_j a_{ij}x_j\right)
       =\sum_{|\alpha|=n}p_\alpha x^\alpha.
\]

Then \(P_A(\mathbf1)=1\), \(\nabla P_A(\mathbf1)=\mathbf1\), and its capacity is one. Its central coefficient is \(p_{\mathbf1}=\operatorname{per}(A)\), while its Fischer–Fock norm is

\[
 \|P_A\|_F^2=\sum_\alpha\alpha!p_\alpha^2
 =\operatorname{per}(AA^T).
\]

Thus (S) asks for a central coefficient bound in terms of a global norm, not merely capacity. The van der Waerden capacity bound only gives \(p_{\mathbf1}\geq c_n\).

Gurvits–Leake's productization theorem preserves the value and gradient at one and the value at a selected positive evaluation point. Crucially, its product polynomial may depend on that point. It does not provide one product polynomial preserving the central coefficient and the full Fischer–Fock norm. Therefore it does not supply the missing norm comparison.

Primary source: Leonid Gurvits and Jonathan Leake, *Capacity Lower Bounds via Productization*, arXiv:2007.08390, especially Theorems 2.4 and 7.2: <https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.08390>.

The combinatorial investigation additionally found exact examples where the squared-coefficient polynomial \(\sum\alpha!p_\alpha^2x^\alpha\) is neither balanced nor Lorentzian. Consequently it cannot simply be substituted into the standard balanced stable-polynomial theorem. Those examples are recorded in the separate combinatorial round-2 report.

### 6.2 A duplication bridge that remains unproved

Define

\[
 C_A=[x_1^2\cdots x_n^2]P_A(x)^2.
\]

Then

\[
 \operatorname{per}(A\otimes\mathbf1_{2\times2})=2^nC_A.
\]

A candidate bridge is

\[
 C_A\stackrel{?}{\geq}
 \frac{(2n)!}{2^n n!n^n}\operatorname{per}(AA^T). \tag{B}
\]

Equality holds at the uniform matrix. Targeted floating-point searches did not find a counterexample, including strongly skew rank-two examples through order 25. This is exploratory evidence only, not a certificate or a proof of (B).

Kunisky–Spielman–Yu prove, for every real matrix of rank at most two,

\[
 \operatorname{per}(A)^2\geq
 \binom{2n}{n}^{-1}
 \operatorname{per}(A\otimes\mathbf1_{2\times2}).
\]

If (B) were proved for rank-two doubly stochastic matrices, these two inequalities would give (S) for that entire family. The bridge is the unresolved step. Their theorem does not apply in arbitrary rank.

Primary source: Dmitriy Kunisky, Daniel A. Spielman, and Xiao-Nan Yu, *Inequalities for rank-two permanents and finite free convolutions*, arXiv:2608.28520, Theorems 1.2 and 3.3: <https://arxiv.org/html/2608.28520v1>.

### 6.3 Two tempting stronger statements are exactly false

Let \(P_4\) be a four-cycle and \(A=(I+P_4)/2\). Exact computation gives

\[
 \operatorname{per}(A)=\frac18,
 \qquad\operatorname{per}(AA^T)=\frac9{64},
 \qquad\operatorname{per}(A\otimes\mathbf1_{2\times2})=\frac98.
\]

Thus \(\operatorname{per}(AA^T)\leq\operatorname{per}(A)\) is false. Also

\[
 \operatorname{per}(A)^2-
 \frac{\operatorname{per}(A\otimes\mathbf1_{2\times2})}{\binom84}
 =-\frac1{2240}<0,
\]

so the sharp rank-two duplication inequality cannot be generalized to all nonnegative doubly stochastic matrices. The latter example is also identified in Remark 1.3 of the cited paper. I independently recomputed all three permanent values exactly.

This is not a counterexample to (S): its actual strong Gyires gap is \(5/2048>0\).

### 6.4 Operator symmetrization and the original conjecture

Let \(v\) be the unit symmetric tensor corresponding to one occurrence of each coordinate, let \(T=A^{\otimes n}\), and put \(H=(T+T^*)/2\). Then

\[
 \langle v,Tv\rangle=\operatorname{per}(A),
\]

and

\[
 \operatorname{per}(AA^T)+\operatorname{per}(A^TA)
 +2\operatorname{per}(A^2)
 =4\|Hv\|^2.
\]

Thus the original conjecture is the angle inequality

\[
 \langle v,Hv\rangle^2\geq c_n\|Hv\|^2.
\]

This retains cancellations that the stronger one-sided Gram inequality discards. However, a self-adjoint contraction fixing the uniform tensor need not satisfy the required angle inequality; the tensor-power and entrywise nonnegativity constraints still have to be used. No missing comparison has been proved in this route.

## What is established and what remains open in this work

The three-band transfer argument has a genuine order-independent analytic tail, verified by exact rational spectral certificates. It is therefore structurally different from extrapolating a list of small orders. With the finite certificates, it gives an all-order family, including every cycle decomposition of an arbitrary permutation.

The unrestricted doubly stochastic problem, unrestricted circulants, the duplication bridge (B), and closure under products remain unproved. No counterexample to either the original conjecture or its stronger Gram version was found in these investigations. No novelty or formal-proof-assistant claim is made for the all-order family until the literature and the final proof package have been checked separately.

## 7. Independent review of the sharp analytic improvement

The later lemma in `round2_sharp_spectral_lemma.md` replaces the Bernstein calculation by an explicit positive-vector comparison and gives the optimal uniform spectral constant

\[
 \kappa=1+\sqrt2,\qquad \kappa^2=2\kappa+1.
\]

I reviewed the proof and ran its exact arithmetic checker. No gap was found. The following details give the independent audit.

### 7.1 The reduction to a single parameter is valid

After the normalization and monotonic increase of \(b\) already justified in Section 2, put \(u=ac\in[0,1]\). Because \((1-a)(1-c)\geq0\), we have \(a+c\leq1+u\). Consequently

\[
 d\leq D(u)=2-2u+2u^2,\qquad
 e\leq E(u)=1-u^2,\qquad f=u.
\]

Both bounds hold simultaneously, and the transfer matrices increase entrywise in all three parameters. Hence it suffices to bound \(T_j(D(u),E(u),u)\). Equality in the parameter bounds is possible with \(a=1,c=u,b=1-u\), so this reduction does not introduce incompatible extremal values.

### 7.2 The side block

For \(w=(1,\kappa^{-1},\kappa^{-2},\kappa^{-3})^T\), the last three coordinates of \(T_1w\leq\kappa w\) follow directly from \(u\leq1\). The first is bounded by

\[
 1+\frac2\kappa+\frac1{\kappa^2}+\frac1{\kappa^3}
 =\frac5\kappa<\kappa.
\]

The identity and inequality are exact consequences of \(\kappa^2=2\kappa+1>5\). Thus the side-block argument is valid, with a strictly positive comparison vector on the entire closed interval.

### 7.3 The central block

For \(0<u<1\), the proposed vector has coordinates

\[
 v_1=1,\quad v_2=\frac{E}{\kappa-u},\quad v_3=\frac u\kappa,
\]

\[
 v_4=\frac{E^2}{\kappa(\kappa-u)}+\frac{Du}{\kappa^2}+\frac{u^3}{\kappa^3},
 \quad v_5=\frac{uE}{\kappa(\kappa-u)},\quad v_6=\frac{u^2}{\kappa^2}.
\]

Every coordinate is strictly positive. I substituted these coordinates into all six rows. Rows three, five, and six follow immediately. Row four is exactly the defining formula for \(v_4\). Row two reduces to

\[
 E\left(1+\frac u\kappa\right)
 +\frac{u^2E}{\kappa(\kappa-u)}
 =\frac{\kappa E}{\kappa-u},
\]

which holds after combining the fractions. Thus rows two through six satisfy equality.

The residual in row one is

\[
 R=\kappa-D\left(1+\frac{u^2}{\kappa^2}\right)
 -\frac{E^2(\kappa+u)}{\kappa(\kappa-u)}-\frac{u^4}{\kappa^3}.
\]

Multiplying by the positive denominator \(\kappa^3(\kappa-u)\) gives exactly the displayed polynomial \(P\) in the lemma. Its factorization is

\[
 P=u(1-u)\left[(20\kappa+8)-(8\kappa+4)u+(12\kappa+4)u^2\right].
\]

The bracket equals

\[
 (12\kappa+4)+(8\kappa+4)(1-u)+(12\kappa+4)u^2>0.
\]

This gives \(R>0\) for \(0<u<1\), so the positive-vector Perron comparison applies. Continuity of the spectral radius handles the endpoints correctly; the proof does not incorrectly invoke a strictly positive vector at points where some displayed coordinates vanish.

There is also an independent consistency check using the characteristic polynomial already verified in Section 4: after substitution \(d=D,e=E,f=u,z=\kappa\), it equals

\[
 \det(\kappa I-T_2)=(\kappa+u)P(u).
\]

This follows by multiplying out the residual denominator or by direct substitution into the six-degree characteristic polynomial.

### 7.4 Sharpness and scaling

At \(u=0\), the matrix has a two-dimensional diagonal block

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}
\]

with positive eigenvalue \(\kappa\); the four remaining eigenvalues are zero. This endpoint is realized by \((a,b,c)=(1,1,0)\), for which the normalizing maximum is one. Hence the constant is indeed optimal for this uniform transfer bound. Restoring the original scale multiplies every entry of the transfer matrix by \(L^2\), so the bound becomes \(\rho(T_G)\leq\kappa L^2\).

### 7.5 A separate rational verification of the order-48 threshold

The supplied checker verifies \(16c_{48}\kappa^{48}<1\) directly in \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)\), and I confirmed that it passes. An independent proof uses only rational arithmetic:

\[
 \sqrt2<\frac{99}{70},\qquad
 \left(\frac{99}{70}\right)^2-2=\frac1{4900}>0.
\]

Therefore \(\kappa<169/70\). Exact integer comparison gives

\[
 16\,48!\,169^{48}<48^{48}70^{48},
\]

or

\[
 16c_{48}\kappa^{48}
 <16c_{48}\left(\frac{169}{70}\right)^{48}
 <1.
\]

The rational upper bound is approximately \(0.9382212767\); the actual factor is approximately \(0.9368763434\). The integer comparison is the proof. For subsequent orders,

\[
 \frac{16c_{n+1}\kappa^{n+1}}{16c_n\kappa^n}
 =\kappa\left(\frac n{n+1}\right)^n
 <\frac{116640}{117649}<1\qquad(n\geq6).
\]

Hence the analytic tail now covers every \(n\geq48\), and only the already-verified finite range \(3\leq n\leq47\) is required. The all-order theorem no longer depends on the Bernstein spectral certificate. The earlier exact certificate remains a separate check of the same transfer setup.
