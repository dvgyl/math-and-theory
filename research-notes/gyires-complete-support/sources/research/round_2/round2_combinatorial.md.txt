# Second attempt: occupancy, stability, and closure under permutation cycles

## Status

This note does **not** prove Gyires's conjecture for an arbitrary doubly stochastic matrix. It records exact obstructions to several tempting general arguments, and proves a useful closure statement for any established one-cycle family. All algebraic counterexamples below were checked with integer arithmetic. They are counterexamples to proposed intermediate lemmas, not to Gyires's inequality.

Write \(J_n=(1/n)_{i,j}\), \(c_n=n!/n^n\), and let \(\Omega_n\) be the doubly stochastic matrices.

The strong assertion under investigation is

\[
\operatorname{per}(A)^2\ge c_n\operatorname{per}(AA^T),\qquad A\in\Omega_n.\tag{S}
\]

If (S) holds for both \(A\) and \(A^T\), permanent Cauchy--Schwarz gives Gyires's original inequality. Universal validity of (S) is also equivalent to

\[
c_n\operatorname{per}(AB)\le\operatorname{per}(A)\operatorname{per}(B)
\quad(A,B\in\Omega_n),\tag{M}
\]

because
\(\operatorname{per}(AB)^2\le\operatorname{per}(AA^T)\operatorname{per}(B^TB)\), and the reverse implication follows by taking \(B=A^T\).

## 1. The exact occupancy target

Let

\[
f_A(x)=\prod_{i=1}^n\sum_{j=1}^n a_{ij}x_j
      =\sum_{|\alpha|=n}p_\alpha x^\alpha.
\]

The coefficients \(p_\alpha\) form the occupancy law of independent categorical choices with row distributions \(a_i\). Double stochasticity says \(E_p\alpha_j=1\) for every \(j\). Let

\[
q_\alpha=\frac{n!}{n^n\alpha!}=\frac{c_n}{\alpha!},
\qquad r_\alpha=\frac{p_\alpha}{q_\alpha}.
\]

The multinomial theorem makes \(q\) a probability law. The permanent Cauchy--Binet identity gives

\[
\operatorname{per}(AA^T)=\sum_\alpha\alpha!p_\alpha^2
 =c_n\sum_\alpha\frac{p_\alpha^2}{q_\alpha},
\qquad \operatorname{per}(A)=p_{\mathbf1}.
\]

Consequently (S) is exactly

\[
E_q r^2=1+\chi^2(p\Vert q)\le r_{\mathbf1}^2.\tag{1}
\]

This is a useful identification of the missing inequality, but it is not a proof. In particular, balance of the first moments alone is insufficient, and the pointwise assertion \(r_\alpha\le r_{\mathbf1}\) is false.

One possible entropy decomposition would be to prove both

\[
D(p\Vert q)\le\log r_{\mathbf1},
\qquad D_2(p\Vert q)\le2D(p\Vert q).
\]

Neither statement is proved here. The next example rules out a direct Jensen argument for the first statement using multivariate concavity of normalized coefficients.

## 2. Exact obstruction: normalized derivative coefficients are not Jensen-concave

Take

\[
A=\frac15
\begin{pmatrix}
2&0&3\\
0&3&2\\
3&2&0
\end{pmatrix}\in\Omega_3.
\]

Ignoring the common scale \(5^{-3}\), its product polynomial is

\[
(2x+3z)(3y+2z)(3x+2y).
\]

Its central coefficient is \(35\). The coefficients of \(x^2y\), \(y^2z\), and \(xz^2\) are all \(18\). Therefore the factorial-normalized coefficients \(D_\alpha=\alpha!p_\alpha\) satisfy

\[
D_{111}=\frac{35}{125},\qquad
D_{210}=D_{021}=D_{102}=\frac{36}{125}.
\]

But

\[
\frac{(2,1,0)+(0,2,1)+(1,0,2)}3=(1,1,1).
\]

Hence

\[
\log D_{111}
<\frac13\bigl(\log D_{210}+\log D_{021}+\log D_{102}\bigr).
\]

The polynomial is a product of nonnegative linear forms and is real stable, and its occupancy mean is exactly the center. Thus neither of these facts supplies the full multivariate Jensen concavity needed by that shortcut. Pairwise Alexandrov--Fenchel/Newton inequalities do not establish the missing multivariate Jensen claim.

This example does not refute the entropy inequality with the actual weights \(p_\alpha\): its three averaging weights above are \(1/3\), not those occupancy probabilities.

## 3. Exact obstruction: the collision polynomial loses stability and balance

For a general \(A\), consider

\[
h_A(x)=\sum_\alpha\alpha!p_\alpha^2x^\alpha
       =\operatorname{per}(A\operatorname{diag}(x)A^T).
\]

It is tempting to apply the stable-polynomial capacity theorem to \(h_A\), treating coefficient squaring as preserving both stability and the balance condition. Both premises fail.

Take

\[
A=\frac15
\begin{pmatrix}
2&3&0\\
2&0&3\\
1&2&2
\end{pmatrix}\in\Omega_3.
\]

Removing the common positive factor \(5^{-6}\), its collision polynomial is

\[
\begin{aligned}
h(x,y,z)={}&96x^3+392x^2y+392x^2z+288xy^2\\
&+1089xyz+288xz^2+648y^2z+648yz^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

Its total mass is \(h(1,1,1)=3841\). The three occupancy first-moment numerators are \(3521,4001,4001\), so the normalized collision law has mean

\[
\left(\frac{3521}{3841},\frac{4001}{3841},\frac{4001}{3841}\right),
\]

which is not \((1,1,1)\).

To see the failure of stability exactly, the Hessian of \(\partial_y h\) is

\[
H=\begin{pmatrix}
784&576&1089\\
576&0&1296\\
1089&1296&1296
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Its determinant is \(-120932352\), and its trace is \(2080\). Since it is real symmetric, it has exactly two positive eigenvalues and one negative eigenvalue: the determinant gives an odd number of negative eigenvalues, and positive trace excludes three negative eigenvalues.

A nonnegative homogeneous real-stable quadratic has at most one positive Hessian eigenvalue. One way to see this is to restrict the associated quadratic form to lines in a positive direction and use real-rootedness: on the hyperplane orthogonal to that direction with respect to the form, the discriminant condition forces the form to be nonpositive. Differentiation preserves real stability. Therefore \(h\) is not real stable. Its derivative Hessian also violates the Lorentzian quadratic condition.

The same example obstructs a naive Fock-norm induction. If

\[
N(f)=\sum_\alpha\alpha!p_\alpha^2,
\]

then

\[
\frac{N(\partial_xf\vert_{x=0})}{N(f)}
=\frac{1665}{3841}<\frac49
=\left(\frac{3-1}{3}\right)^{3-1}.
\]

Indeed, the terms with \(x\)-exponent one have total weight \(288+1089+288=1665\), and \(14985<15364\). Thus one cannot eliminate an arbitrarily chosen variable while retaining the sharp Gurvits recursion factor at the level of the squared coefficient norm. An adaptive elimination/scaling argument would need an additional theorem.

## 4. A tempting global trace lower bound also fails

The PSD trace bound proved in the first note controls \(\operatorname{per}(J+D)\) for centered PSD \(D\) when \(\operatorname{tr}D<1/e\). To turn it into a global theorem, one might hope for a lower bound such as

\[
\frac{\operatorname{per}(A)}{c_n}
\mathrel{\stackrel{?}{\ge}}
\exp\left(\frac12\|A-J_n\|_F^2\right).
\]

This is false. Already for

\[
A=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}0&1&1\\1&0&1\\1&1&0\end{pmatrix},
\]

one has \(\operatorname{per}(A)/c_3=9/8\) and \(\|A-J_3\|_F^2=1/2\), whereas \(e^{1/4}>5/4>9/8\).

The obstruction is not confined to order three: for \(A=(nJ_n-I)/(n-1)\), the exact ratio is \(D_n n^n/[n!(n-1)^n]\), where \(D_n\) is the derangement number. At \(n=5\) it is \(6875/6144\), while the squared Frobenius distance is \(1/4\) and \(e^{1/8}>1+1/8=9/8>6875/6144\).

This does not rule out a weaker quantitative lower bound, but it prevents the above Gaussian-looking estimate from closing the full problem.

## 5. Rigorous closure under direct sums and independent permutations

The following reduction is useful independently of the particular method used to prove a one-cycle theorem.

**Closure lemma.** Suppose \(B_k\in\Omega_{m_k}\), \(m_1+\cdots+m_r=n\), and each \(B_k\) satisfies both

\[
\operatorname{per}(B_k)^2\ge c_{m_k}\operatorname{per}(B_kB_k^T),
\qquad
\operatorname{per}(B_k)^2\ge c_{m_k}\operatorname{per}(B_k^TB_k).
\]

Then \(B=B_1\oplus\cdots\oplus B_r\) satisfies both strong inequalities of order \(n\). So does \(A=UBV\) for arbitrary permutation matrices \(U,V\). In consequence, every such \(A\) satisfies Gyires's original inequality.

**Proof.** Permanents multiply over direct sums, as do their Gram permanents. Hence

\[
\operatorname{per}(B)^2\ge
\left(\prod_{k=1}^r c_{m_k}\right)\operatorname{per}(BB^T).
\]

Moreover,

\[
\frac{c_n}{\prod_kc_{m_k}}
=\frac{n!}{\prod_km_k!}\prod_k\left(\frac{m_k}{n}\right)^{m_k}
\le1.
\]

The expression is the probability of the count vector \((m_1,\ldots,m_r)\) in \(n\) multinomial trials with probabilities \((m_1/n,\ldots,m_r/n)\). This proves the first strong inequality; the second follows in the same way. If \(r\ge2\), the probability is strictly less than one.

For \(A=UBV\),

\[
AA^T=U(BB^T)U^T,\qquad A^TA=V^T(B^TB)V.
\]

Thus \(\operatorname{per}(A)=\operatorname{per}(B)\), and both Gram permanents are unchanged. Finally,

\[
\operatorname{per}(A^2)
\le\sqrt{\operatorname{per}(AA^T)\operatorname{per}(A^TA)}
\le\frac{\operatorname{per}(A)^2}{c_n}.
\]

Summing the four bounds yields Gyires. This last step is needed because independent row and column permutations need not preserve \(\operatorname{per}(A^2)\). \(\square\)

**Permutation-cycle consequence.** Suppose the strong assertion has been established, for every \(m\ge1\), for

\[
C_m=aI_m+bP_m+cP_m^2,
\quad a,b,c\ge0,\quad a+b+c=1,
\]

where \(P_m\) is a single cyclic permutation. Then it holds for

\[
A=aI_n+bQ+cQ^2
\]

for every permutation matrix \(Q\), and Gyires holds for every independent row/column permutation of this \(A\).

Indeed, conjugating \(Q\) to its cycle decomposition gives a direct sum of the indicated \(C_m\). Each \(C_m\) is normal, so the two Gram matrices coincide and one verified strong inequality supplies both. The closure lemma applies. Multiplication by \(P_m^{-1}\) changes the displayed form to \(aP_m^{-1}+bI_m+cP_m\), so the consecutive-three-shift and cyclic tridiagonal formulations are equivalent.

The closure lemma actually permits different triples \((a_k,b_k,c_k)\) in different cycle blocks. The base cases \(m=1,2,3,4\) must be checked for the strong assertion itself when the transfer argument starts at \(m=5\). An existing proof only of original Gyires at a small order is not enough for this reduction.

## 6. Literature check and limits

The current author-maintained [Conjecture 20 page](https://yair-lavi.com/mincs-list/conjecture-20/index.html) lists all-order convex combinations of two permutation matrices, and combinations of at most three permutation matrices of order five. It does not list the all-order consecutive-three-shift family. A targeted search did not locate a primary publication proving Gyires for that whole family; this is not a claim of novelty.

Permanents of tridiagonal circulants themselves have classical exact formulas. Cummings and Seberry Wallis's [1977 paper](https://documents.uow.edu.au/~jennie/WEBPDF/059_1977.pdf) cites Minc's earlier tridiagonal-circulant work. The [2021 recurrence paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8624399/) treats finite bands inside uniform circulants. These are relevant calculation methods, not sources of the missing universal Gyires inequality.

Gurvits's [stable-polynomial capacity theorem](https://arxiv.org/html/0711.3496v2) gives the sharp central-coefficient lower bound from capacity. It does not by itself give the squared-coefficient norm estimate (1); Sections 2 and 3 identify specific incorrect ways of trying to supply that extra step.

The unrestricted occupancy inequality (1) remains the unresolved step in this attempt.
