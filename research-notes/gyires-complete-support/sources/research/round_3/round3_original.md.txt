# Round 3: the original Gyires inequality and global analytic mechanisms

## Status

This attempt targets the **original** conjecture for arbitrary doubly stochastic matrices, retaining the term involving \(A^2\) and the cancellation between the two orientations. It does not prove or disprove that conjecture. It establishes a useful global radial decomposition and gives exact counterexamples to several substantially stronger intermediate claims. Every counterexample below is explicitly identified by the assertion it refutes; none is presented as a nonnegative doubly stochastic counterexample to Gyires.

The exact rational checks are in `round3_original_check.py`. They all pass.

Let

\[
 c_n=\frac{n!}{n^n},\qquad
 Q(A)=\frac{\operatorname{per}(AA^T)+\operatorname{per}(A^TA)
                  +2\operatorname{per}(A^2)}4.
\]

The target is

\[
 \operatorname{per}(A)^2\geq c_nQ(A). \tag{G}
\]

## 1. The exact symmetrized polynomial formulation

Define the row-product polynomial

\[
 P_A(x)=\prod_{i=1}^n\left(\sum_j a_{ij}x_j\right).
\]

Equip homogeneous polynomials with the Fischer–Fock inner product

\[
 \langle x^\alpha,x^\beta\rangle_F
 =\mathbf1_{\alpha=\beta}\,\alpha!.
\]

The permanent Cauchy–Binet identity gives

\[
 \langle P_A,P_B\rangle_F=\operatorname{per}(AB^T).
\]

Therefore, for

\[
 R_A=\frac{P_A+P_{A^T}}2,
\]

we have

\[
 \|R_A\|_F^2=Q(A),\qquad
 [x_1\cdots x_n]R_A=\operatorname{per}(A).
\]

Also \(R_A(\mathbf1)=1\) and \(\nabla R_A(\mathbf1)=\mathbf1\). The original conjecture is precisely

\[
 [x_1\cdots x_n]R_A\geq\sqrt{c_n}\,\|R_A\|_F. \tag{1}
\]

This formulation retains the original cancellation. In particular,

\[
 Q(A)=\frac{\|P_A\|_F^2+\|P_{A^T}\|_F^2}{2}
       -\frac{\|P_A-P_{A^T}\|_F^2}{4}.
\]

Replacing \(Q(A)\) by either individual Gram permanent or their average discards this favorable final term.

## 2. A global radial decomposition with nonnegative coefficients

Let \(A_t=J_n+tB\), where \(B=A-J_n\). Thus \(B\mathbf1=B^T\mathbf1=0\). Use orthonormal coordinates

\[
 x=z_0u+y,\qquad u=\frac{\mathbf1}{\sqrt n},\quad y\in u^\perp.
\]

Each row linear form becomes

\[
 (A_tx)_i=\frac{z_0}{\sqrt n}+t(By)_i.
\]

For \(0\leq k\leq n\), put

\[
 r_k(y)=\frac{e_k(By)+e_k(B^Ty)}2,
\]

where \(e_k\) is the elementary symmetric polynomial in the \(n\) displayed coordinates. Then

\[
 R_{A_t}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^n
 t^k\frac{z_0^{n-k}}{n^{(n-k)/2}}r_k(y). \tag{2}
\]

Here \(r_0=1\) and \(r_1=0\). The latter follows from the zero row and column sums of \(B\).

Different powers of \(z_0\) are orthogonal in the Fischer–Fock inner product, which is invariant under the orthogonal coordinate change. Consequently

\[
 Q(A_t)=c_n+\sum_{k=2}^n q_k(A)t^{2k},\qquad
 q_k(A)=\frac{(n-k)!}{n^{n-k}}\|r_k\|_F^2\geq0. \tag{3}
\]

This identity holds for every real matrix with row and column sums one, regardless of entry signs. In particular, the original symmetrized denominator has nonnegative even-power coefficients, even though the three individual terms in its definition look different.

### The first coefficient is explicitly positive

Set

\[
 M=B^TB+BB^T.
\]

Since the entries of \(By\) sum to zero,

\[
 r_2(y)=-\frac14y^TMy.
\]

For a real symmetric matrix \(M\),

\[
 \|y^TMy\|_F^2=2\operatorname{tr}(M^2).
\]

Thus

\[
 q_2(A)=\frac{(n-2)!}{n^{n-2}}
         \frac{\operatorname{tr}[(B^TB+BB^T)^2]}8. \tag{4}
\]

The matrix \(M\) is positive semidefinite and is nonzero exactly when \(B\ne0\). Therefore

\[
 A\ne J_n\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 Q(A_t)\text{ is strictly increasing for }t>0. \tag{5}
\]

This is a global proved structural fact, not a local expansion or a computational observation.

## 3. Radial monotonicity of the desired ratio is nevertheless false

A natural attempt would prove (G) by showing that

\[
 \mathcal R_A(t)=\frac{\operatorname{per}(A_t)^2}{Q(A_t)}
\]

is nondecreasing from its value \(c_n\) at \(t=0\). This intermediate claim fails already in order three.

Let \(P\) be a three-cycle and \(A=(I+P)/2\). The circulant weights of \(A_t\) are \((u,u,v)\), where

\[
 u=\frac13+\frac t6,\qquad v=\frac13-\frac t3.
\]

Transposition merely permutes the row linear forms. Hence

\[
 P_{A_t}=P_{A_t^T},
\]

so there is no orientation cancellation to discard: the original denominator equals the individual Gram permanent. Direct exact expansion gives

\[
 p(t)=\operatorname{per}(A_t)
      =\frac29+\frac{t^2}{12}-\frac{t^3}{18},
\]

\[
 q(t)=Q(A_t)
      =\frac29+\frac{t^4}{48}+\frac{t^6}{144}.
\]

At \(t=1\),

\[
 p(1)=q(1)=\frac14,
 \qquad p'(1)=0,\qquad q'(1)=\frac18.
\]

It follows that

\[
 \mathcal R_A(1)=\frac14,\qquad
 \mathcal R_A'(1)=-\frac18,
 \qquad (\log\mathcal R_A)'(1)=-\frac12.
\]

This is an exact failure for the original ratio, not only for the stronger Gram ratio. The endpoint remains safely above \(c_3=2/9\), so it does not refute Gyires. The example was identified by the circulant investigation and independently checked here, including the equality of all three denominator terms as polynomials in \(t\).

## 4. Transpose averaging does not restore stability or balanced Fock moments

The polynomials \(P_A\) and \(P_{A^T}\) are real stable. Their average \(R_A\) need not be stable, or even Lorentzian. Thus the original symmetrization does not permit a direct application of a stable-polynomial theorem to \(R_A\).

An exact example is

\[
 A=\frac16
 \begin{pmatrix}
 3&2&1\\1&0&5\\2&4&0
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]

Let \(A_0=6A\) and ignore the positive scaling between \(P_{A_0}+P_{A_0^T}\) and \(R_A\). The Hessian of its derivative with respect to the second variable is

\[
 H=\begin{pmatrix}
 96&36&168\\36&0&120\\168&120&120
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]

We have

\[
 \det H=-86400,\qquad \operatorname{tr}H=216.
\]

Hence \(H\) has two positive eigenvalues and one negative eigenvalue. This is incompatible with the quadratic derivatives of a homogeneous real stable polynomial with nonnegative coefficients.

There is an explicit upper-half-plane witness for the derivative quadratic. Put \(x=(-12,10,5)\) and \(e=(1,1,1)\). Exact arithmetic gives

\[
 x^THx=24,\quad x^THe=0,\quad e^THe=864.
\]

Thus the derivative, evaluated at \(x+te\), is

\[
 \frac12(x+te)^TH(x+te)=12+432t^2,
\]

which vanishes at \(t=i/6\). All three coordinates then lie in the upper half-plane. Stability is preserved by differentiation, so the original averaged polynomial cannot be stable.

The squared-coefficient distribution is also not balanced. If

\[
 P_{A_0}+P_{A_0^T}=\sum_\alpha r_\alpha x^\alpha,
 \qquad h_\alpha=\alpha!r_\alpha^2,
\]

then

\[
 \sum_\alpha h_\alpha=54000,
 \qquad
 \frac{\sum_\alpha\alpha h_\alpha}{\sum_\alpha h_\alpha}
 =\left(\frac{117}{125},\frac{129}{125},\frac{129}{125}\right).
\]

So averaging the transpose does not remove the mean drift that obstructed the earlier capacity argument either.

For clarity, this matrix satisfies the actual conjecture with the positive unnormalized gap

\[
 4\operatorname{per}(A)^2
 -c_3\left(\operatorname{per}(AA^T)+\operatorname{per}(A^TA)
                         +2\operatorname{per}(A^2)\right)
 =\frac{169}{486}>0.
\]

## 5. The obvious operator-compression strengthening is false

Let \(T=A^{\otimes n}\), and let \(E\) be the orthogonal projection onto the span of the \(n!\) tensor basis vectors with all coordinates distinct. The unit uniform vector on this subspace is the symmetrized all-distinct vector \(v\).

The compression \(S=ETE\) has entries

\[
 S_{\sigma,\tau}=\prod_{i=1}^n a_{\sigma(i),\tau(i)}.
\]

Every row and column sum is \(\operatorname{per}(A)\), so \(Sv=S^Tv=\operatorname{per}(A)v\). In the symmetric case the desired scalar inequality is

\[
 \langle v,S^2v\rangle\geq c_n\langle v,ET^2Ev\rangle.
\]

It would follow from the stronger matrix inequality

\[
 S^2\succeq c_nET^2E. \tag{6}
\]

However, (6) fails even on nonnegative test vectors.

Take

\[
 A=\frac12
 \begin{pmatrix}0&1&1\\1&0&1\\1&1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

In the lexicographic permutation basis

\[
 (123),(132),(213),(231),(312),(321),
\]

let \(w=e_{123}+e_{132}\). This vector is nonnegative. With \(G=E(A^2)^{\otimes3}E\), direct exact computation gives

\[
 \langle w,S^2w\rangle=\frac1{16},
 \qquad\langle w,Gw\rangle=\frac5{16}.
\]

Consequently

\[
 \left\langle w,\left(S^2-\frac29G\right)w\right\rangle
 =-\frac1{144}<0.
\]

The failure is therefore stronger than failure of positive semidefiniteness: the difference is not copositive on the all-distinct coordinate cone. On the required uniform vector, the scalar strong Gyires gap is instead \(+1/144\). A successful proof must exploit that specific vector; it cannot prove this natural operator inequality for every vector.

For nonsymmetric \(A\), replacing \(T\) by \((T+T^*)/2\) does not rescue the proposed operator statement, since the symmetric example is already included.

## 6. A valid PSD completion does not justify coefficient extraction

Another genuinely global route starts with

\[
 S_A=\begin{pmatrix}0&A\\A^T&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 B_t=\frac{I_{2n}+tS_A}{1+t},\qquad0\leq t\leq1.
\]

For doubly stochastic \(A\), \(\|A\|_2\leq1\). Therefore \(B_t\) is symmetric positive semidefinite and doubly stochastic. Its permanent has the exact matching-minor expansion

\[
 \operatorname{per}(I_{2n}+tS_A)
 =\sum_{k=0}^n t^{2k}
     \sum_{\substack{I,J\subseteq[n]\\|I|=|J|=k}}
       \operatorname{per}(A[I,J])^2. \tag{7}
\]

The explanation is direct: the top rows sent across the blocks form a set \(I\), the bottom rows sent across form a set \(J\), and the identity entries force the complements to stay fixed. The two cross-block matchings contribute the same minor permanent twice.

Since \(0\preceq B_t^2\preceq B_t\), permanent monotonicity for positive semidefinite matrices gives the valid inequality

\[
 \operatorname{per}((I_{2n}+tS_A)^2)
 \leq(1+t)^{2n}\operatorname{per}(I_{2n}+tS_A). \tag{8}
\]

This construction contains all orders of squared minors and therefore looked like a way to extract the desired top-order comparison. But positivity of (8) on \([0,1]\) is not coefficientwise positivity. In fact the coefficient of \(t^{4n}\) in the right side minus the left side is

\[
 \operatorname{per}(A)^2
 -\operatorname{per}(AA^T)\operatorname{per}(A^TA),
\]

which can be negative. For \(A=(I+P_4)/2\), its value is

\[
 \frac1{64}-\left(\frac9{64}\right)^2
 =-\frac{17}{4096}.
\]

Thus the PSD completion and its inequality are valid, but the proposed coefficient extraction is false. Applying the stronger PSD Gyires inequality to \(B_t\) also introduces many orders of minors and the constant \(c_{2n}\); no valid elimination of those extra quantities has been obtained.

## 7. Plain matrix symmetrization does not give a pointwise reduction

One could hope to reduce the original conjecture to symmetric matrices by proving

\[
 \frac{\operatorname{per}(A)^2}{Q(A)}
 \geq\frac{\operatorname{per}((A+A^T)/2)^2}{Q((A+A^T)/2)}.
\]

This specific monotonicity assertion is false. For

\[
 A=\frac15\begin{pmatrix}0&0&5\\1&4&0\\4&1&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad B=\frac{A+A^T}{2},
\]

the exact ratios are

\[
 \frac{\operatorname{per}(A)^2}{Q(A)}=\frac{289}{321},
 \qquad
 \frac{\operatorname{per}(B)^2}{Q(B)}=\frac{110889}{122956}.
\]

Their difference is

\[
 -\frac{61085}{39468876}<0.
\]

This does not exclude a more subtle reduction to symmetric matrices, but it rules out the direct pointwise symmetrization argument.

## 8. Why the remaining entrywise constraint matters

The tensor and Fock formulas are valid for real matrices with row and column sums one. The nonnegative-entry hypothesis cannot be dropped and replaced merely by symmetry, contraction, and the van der Waerden lower bound.

For example,

\[
 A=\frac16\begin{pmatrix}-2&4&4\\4&1&1\\4&1&1\end{pmatrix}
\]

is symmetric, has all row and column sums one, and has eigenvalues \(1,-1,0\). It satisfies

\[
 \operatorname{per}(A)=\frac5{18}>\frac29,
 \qquad\operatorname{per}(A^2)=\frac12.
\]

Nevertheless,

\[
 \operatorname{per}(A)^2-c_3\operatorname{per}(A^2)
 =-\frac{11}{324}<0.
\]

This signed example was found in the earlier analysis and is included here to identify the exact logical limitation of the operator route. Any full proof must use the nonnegative entries in a way stronger than their implication that \(A\) is a contraction and its permanent is at least \(c_n\).

## 9. An exact order-three step for a conditional induction

The most promising remaining route is an induction on polynomial degree using capacity-balanced coordinate deletions. The exact result established in this round is one step for matrix-product polynomials of degree three. It does **not** establish the required lemma for arbitrary stable polynomials or arbitrary degree.

For a polynomial with nonnegative coefficients, write

\[
 N(p)=\sum_\alpha\alpha!p_\alpha^2.
\]

For a doubly stochastic matrix \(A\) of order three, let \(p=P_A\), let \(b=p_{111}=\operatorname{per}(A)\), and write each coordinate deletion as

\[
 q_j=[x_j]p=a_jx_k^2+b x_kx_\ell+c_jx_\ell^2,
 \qquad\{j,k,\ell\}=\{1,2,3\}.
\]

After a capacity-minimizing diagonal scaling, divided by the product of its scale factors, the squared Fischer norm of this binary quadratic is

\[
 H_j=b^2+4a_jc_j.
\]

If an outer coefficient is zero, this is the limiting expression; a finite minimizing diagonal need not exist. With \(u_i=p_{3e_i}\), direct expansion gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
 G(p)&=3\sum_jH_j-4N(p)\\
 &=5b^2+12\sum_ja_jc_j-8\sum_j(a_j^2+c_j^2)-24\sum_iu_i^2\\
 &=5b^2-\sum_j\bigl((a_j+c_j)^2+7(a_j-c_j)^2\bigr)
      -24\sum_iu_i^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

**Exact theorem.** For every nonnegative doubly stochastic \(3\times3\) matrix \(A\),

\[
 \frac13\sum_{j=1}^3H_j\ge\frac49N(P_A),
\]

with equality if and only if \(A=J_3\).

The proof is an exact computer-assisted certificate. Write \(A\) as a nonnegative combination of the six permutation matrices. In each chamber ordering the six Birkhoff weights, replace the ordered weights by successive sums of nonnegative gaps. Every coefficient of the resulting degree-six polynomial \(G\) is nonnegative. Row-permutation symmetry reduces the primary calculation to 120 chambers; the independent audit checks all 720 directly and computes the norm as a Gram permanent, using a different polynomial construction. It verifies 324,360 positive coefficient positions, with smallest positive coefficient 5. All pure sixth-power coefficients vanish precisely on rays whose cumulative permutation tails are uniform matrices. Nonnegativity of all coefficients then forces equality only at a uniform matrix.

The primary programs are `round3_deletion_n3_certificate.py` and `round3_deletion_n3_review.py`. The full independent audit is `round3_deletion_n3_independent_audit.py`. Their JSON reports contain exact integer results. The derivation, chamber coverage, independent verification, and equality argument are detailed in `round3_deletion_audit.md`.

Here is the conditional all-order mechanism. Suppose \(p\) is a balanced homogeneous real-stable polynomial of degree \(n\) in \(n\) variables. For \(q_j=[x_j]p\), let \(D_j\) be a capacity-minimizing positive diagonal matrix and put

\[
 H_j=\frac{N(q_j(D_jx))}{(\det D_j)^2}.
\]

The missing lemma is

\[
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \max_j H_j\ge
 \left(\frac{n-1}{n}\right)^{n-1}N(p).}
\]

Indeed,

\[
 r_j(x)=\frac{q_j(D_jx)}{q_j(D_j\mathbf1)}
\]

is again real stable and is normalized balanced: \(r_j(\mathbf1)=1\) and \(\nabla r_j(\mathbf1)=\mathbf1\). Its central coefficient is
\(p_{\mathbf1}\det D_j/q_j(D_j\mathbf1)\).
Multiplying the inductive norm inequality for \(r_j\) by
\((q_j(D_j\mathbf1)/\det D_j)^2\) would therefore give

\[
 p_{\mathbf1}^2\ge c_{n-1}H_j\ge c_nN(p).
\]

For \(p=P_A\), this is the strong Gram inequality and implies the original conjecture. Strictly positive coefficients ensure the capacity minimizers exist. General boundary inputs can be handled by the stability-preserving approximation
\(p(((1-\varepsilon)I+\varepsilon J_n)x)\), followed by continuity. These normalization and boundary details are proved in the audit note.

The closure gap is essential: deleting a variable from a product of linear forms generally gives a sum of products, not another product. Consequently, the exact order-three matrix-product certificate does not prove the missing lemma for all balanced stable cubics, and cannot be used as the degree-three step in a general degree-four induction. Together with the valid binary-quadratic base case, it does rederive the strong order-three permanent inequality.

For comparison, the recent primary paper [Gurvits and Leake, *Unique Minimizers for Permanents, Mixed Discriminants, and Log-concave Polynomials*](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.21083v2) proves central-coefficient uniqueness and deletion-capacity bounds for its stated classes. Those conclusions do not include the relative Fischer-norm bound displayed above. Neither ordinary capacity bounds nor pointwise productization have been shown here to supply the missing norm comparison.

As an exploratory boundary check, all nine distinct coefficient patterns obtained by identifying the seven variables of the rank-three Fano matroid basis polynomial into three groups of at least two variables were capacity-balanced numerically. They satisfied the proposed average-deletion bound. This is evidence about those finitely many inputs only; it is not a certificate for the stable or Lorentzian classes and is not used in any theorem above.

## Remaining gap

The retained cancellation is exactly described by \(R_A\), and its radial norm has the sum-of-squares expansion (3). What remains unproved is a dimension-sharp comparison of the central coefficient with that norm for the special transpose-paired product polynomials arising from nonnegative doubly stochastic matrices.

Capacity and balancing alone do not provide this comparison. Transpose averaging does not restore stability or balanced squared-coefficient moments. A universal PSD or copositive compression inequality is false. Neither radial ratio monotonicity nor plain matrix symmetrization is available. The PSD block construction is valid but does not permit the needed coefficient extraction.

These obstructions narrow the search without establishing the full conjecture. The exact order-three deletion theorem provides a checked auxiliary step, while its extension to a deletion-closed polynomial class remains unproved. The all-order three-band theorem from the previous round remains valid, but it is not being used here as a substitute for an unrestricted solution.
