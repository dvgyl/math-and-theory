# Third attempt: exact entropy obstruction and regular-graph expectations

## Status

The unrestricted Gyires inequality is not proved or disproved in this note. The work below uses structure specific to products of nonnegative linear forms. It provides (i) an exact counterexample to a proposed entropy bridge, including the actual occupancy weights, and (ii) exact expectation formulas that can certify the existence of a counterexample if their ratio ever crosses a stated threshold. The finite expectation checks performed here did not cross that threshold.

Put \(c_n=n!/n^n\). For \(A\in\Omega_n\), write

\[
\prod_i\sum_j a_{ij}x_j=\sum_{|\alpha|=n}p_\alpha x^\alpha,
\qquad q_\alpha=\frac{c_n}{\alpha!},\qquad r_\alpha=p_\alpha/q_\alpha.
\]

The strong assertion is

\[
E_qr^2\le r_{\mathbf1}^2,
\]

equivalently

\[
\operatorname{per}(A)^2\ge c_n\operatorname{per}(AA^T).
\]

## 1. A proposed KL-divergence bridge is false

A possible route was to try to prove

\[
D(p\Vert q)\le\log r_{\mathbf1},\qquad
D_2(p\Vert q)\le2D(p\Vert q).
\]

The first inequality is false, even for the very structured two-permutation family \(A=(I+P_n)/2\), where \(P_n\) is one cyclic permutation and \(n\ge3\).

Let \(X_1,\ldots,X_n\) be independent fair bits. Choosing the diagonal or shifted entry in row \(j\) gives the occupancy vector

\[
\alpha_j=1-X_j+X_{j-1},
\]

with cyclic indices. If the word \(X\) is nonconstant, the differences \(X_{j-1}-X_j\) determine the entire binary word uniquely: any two solutions differ by a constant, and a nonconstant binary word contains both 0 and 1. The two constant words both produce \(\alpha=\mathbf1\).

Consequently,

\[
p_{\mathbf1}=2^{1-n},
\qquad p_\alpha=2^{-n}
\quad\text{for every other occupancy in the support.}
\]

Every coordinate of \(\alpha\) is 0, 1, or 2. If \(K\) is the number of 0-to-1 transitions around the binary cycle, then

\[
\alpha!=2^K,
\qquad E K=\frac n4.
\]

The entropy of the occupancy vector is

\[
H(p)=n\log2-2^{1-n}\log2,
\]

because only the two equally likely constant words are merged. It follows that

\[
\begin{aligned}
D(p\Vert q)
&=-H(p)+E\log\alpha!-\log c_n\\
&=-\frac{3n}{4}\log2+2^{1-n}\log2-\log c_n.
\end{aligned}
\]

Meanwhile,

\[
\log r_{\mathbf1}=(1-n)\log2-\log c_n.
\]

Thus the exact gap is

\[
\boxed{
D(p\Vert q)-\log r_{\mathbf1}
=\left(\frac n4-1+2^{1-n}\right)\log2.
}
\]

It is positive for every \(n\ge4\), and grows linearly with \(n\). This is stronger than merely finding coefficients that violate Jensen concavity: the actual occupancy distribution itself violates the proposed weighted entropy inequality.

For example, at \(n=4\),

\[
\operatorname{per}(A)=\frac18,
\quad c_4=\frac3{32},
\quad r_{\mathbf1}=\frac43,
\quad D(p\Vert q)=\log\frac43+\frac18\log2.
\]

The strong Gyires inequality itself still holds. Indeed,

\[
\operatorname{per}(AA^T)=\frac9{64},
\qquad E_qr^2=\frac32<\frac{16}{9}=r_{\mathbf1}^2.
\]

So this obstruction invalidates the entropy decomposition, not the target inequality. No claim about universal validity or failure of the second proposed KL/Renyi comparison is made here.

## 2. A product-specific conditional-probability target

The collision law

\[
\nu_A(\alpha)
=\frac{\alpha!p_\alpha^2}{\operatorname{per}(AA^T)}
\]

is a probability law, by permanent Cauchy--Binet. The strong assertion is precisely

\[
\nu_A(\mathbf1)\ge c_n.
\]

This law can be generated by choosing a permutation \(\sigma\) with weight
\(\prod_i(AA^T)_{i,\sigma(i)}\), then independently selecting the intermediate label for each paired row with weights proportional to \(a_{ij}a_{\sigma(i),j}\). It is therefore a mixture of occupancy laws of independent categorical variables. The constituent row-stochastic matrices generally are not doubly stochastic; neither is the collision law generally balanced. This identifies a genuine conditioning problem, but does not furnish a proof by applying van der Waerden to each constituent.

### Negative association and moment bounds are still insufficient

The following is an abstract probability distribution, **not** an occupancy law obtained from a doubly stochastic product polynomial. It rules out replacing that product-specific structure by balance, negative association, covariance contraction, and a van der Waerden central-mass lower bound.

For \(0\le\theta\le1\), let \(p^{(\theta)}\) place probability \(\theta\) at \((1,1,1)\), and probability \((1-\theta)/6\) at each of the six permutations of \((2,1,0)\). It has mean \((1,1,1)\) and covariance

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(p^{(\theta)})=(1-\theta)(I_3-J_3)
\preceq I_3-J_3=\operatorname{Cov}(q).
\]

Every mixed falling-factorial moment is at most 1. This can be checked exhaustively by its total degree: first moments equal 1; the second moments are \(2(1-\theta)/3\) for one repeated coordinate and \(2/3+\theta/3\) for two distinct coordinates; third moments are \((1-\theta)/3\) for an exponent pattern \((2,1,0)\), \(\theta\) for \((1,1,1)\), and zero for \((3,0,0)\). Higher falling-factorial moments vanish.

The coordinates are negatively associated. To verify this directly, condition on the first coordinate. The remaining ordered pair has the following laws:

- Given \(\alpha_1=0\): uniform on \((1,2),(2,1)\).
- Given \(\alpha_1=1\): a mixture of the uniform law on \((0,2),(2,0)\) and the point mass at \((1,1)\).
- Given \(\alpha_1=2\): uniform on \((0,1),(1,0)\).

The first law stochastically dominates the second, and the second dominates the third, in the coordinatewise order. For example, couple \((0,2)\) with \((1,2)\), \((2,0)\) with \((2,1)\), and couple \((1,1)\) with either of the two upper points; analogous couplings work for the lower pair. Thus for every increasing function of the other two coordinates, its conditional expectation decreases with \(\alpha_1\). Its covariance with any increasing function of \(\alpha_1\) is nonpositive. Symmetry gives the same statement for each coordinate. In three coordinates, every pair of disjoint nonempty coordinate sets has one side of size one, proving negative association.

Now take \(\theta=3/13\). Then

\[
p_{111}=\frac3{13}>\frac29=c_3,
\qquad
\sum_\alpha\alpha!p_\alpha^2
=\theta^2+\frac{(1-\theta)^2}{3}
=\frac{127}{507},
\]

but

\[
c_3\sum_\alpha\alpha!p_\alpha^2-p_{111}^2
=\frac{11}{4563}>0.
\]

Thus the desired norm inequality fails despite all the stated weaker properties. The associated polynomial is not real stable or Lorentzian: in the Hessian of its \(x\)-derivative, the antisymmetric \((y,z)\) direction has positive eigenvalue \((1-4\theta)/3=1/39\), and the complementary two-dimensional part has another positive eigenvalue. A proof must retain more than these negative-dependence and moment consequences.

## 3. Exact expectation formula for regular bipartite configuration graphs

Let \(d\ge2\) and \(n\ge1\). Give each of \(n\) row vertices and \(n\) column vertices exactly \(d\) labeled stubs, and choose a uniformly random bijection between the \(dn\) row stubs and the \(dn\) column stubs. Let \(G\) be the resulting \(n\times n\) nonnegative integer adjacency matrix, with parallel edges counted. Then \(G/d\) is doubly stochastic in every realization.

Define

\[
F_d(x,y)=
\sum_{\substack{k,r\ge0\\k+2r\le d}}
\frac{d!(k+r)!}{k!(r!)^2(d-k-2r)!}\,x^ky^r.
\]

Then the following finite identity is exact:

\[
\boxed{
E\operatorname{per}(GG^T)
=\frac{n!d^n}{(dn)!}
\sum_{t=0}^{n}
(d-1)^{n-t}\bigl((d-2)n+t\bigr)!(n-t)!
[x^ty^{n-t}]F_d(x,y)^n.
}\tag{2}
\]

### Derivation

Expand the Gram permanent as a pair of edge choices, colored red and blue, with one red and one blue edge at every row. At a column, the red edges and blue edges are paired; if there are \(\ell\) of each color, this contributes \(\ell!\).

Let \(t\) be the number of row vertices that use the same edge for both colors. These contribute one stub of type RB each; every other row contributes distinct R-only and B-only stubs. The number of ways to color the row stubs is

\[
\binom nt d^t\bigl(d(d-1)\bigr)^{n-t}.
\]

At a column, suppose \(k\) stubs are RB, \(r\) are R-only, and \(r\) are B-only. The remaining \(d-k-2r\) stubs are unused. There are

\[
\frac{d!}{k!(r!)^2(d-k-2r)!}
\]

such stub colorings and \((k+r)!\) pairings of the colored edges, producing the corresponding term of \(F_d\). Across all columns, the prescribed color totals are \(t,n-t,n-t,(d-2)n+t\).

For any fixed row and column colorings with these totals, the probability that a uniform stub bijection respects all four colors is

\[
\frac{t!\,(n-t)!^2\,((d-2)n+t)!}{(dn)!}.
\]

Multiplying and cancelling factorials gives (2).

For illustration,

\[
F_2(x,y)=1+2x+2x^2+2y,
\]

\[
F_3(x,y)=1+3x+6x^2+6x^3+6y+12xy.
\]

### The exact second moment uses the same formula

For two perfect matchings, each column must receive exactly one red and one blue edge. Thus its local possibilities are RB or R-only plus B-only. Replace \(F_d\) in (2) by

\[
L_d(x,y)=dx+d(d-1)y.
\]

The result is exactly \(E\operatorname{per}(G)^2\). In particular, if

\[
w_t=(d-1)^{n-t}((d-2)n+t)!(n-t)!,
\]

then

\[
\frac{c_nE\operatorname{per}(GG^T)}{E\operatorname{per}(G)^2}
=\frac{n!}{n^n}
\frac{\sum_t w_t[x^ty^{n-t}]F_d(x,y)^n}
     {\sum_t w_t\binom nt d^n(d-1)^{n-t}}.
\tag{3}
\]

Every quantity in (3) is an explicitly computable integer before the final division.

## 4. What an expectation violation would prove

If (3) exceeded 1 for any \((d,n)\), some realization \(G\) would be a counterexample to the strong assertion. A theorem about typical graphs would not be needed: the pointwise inequality, if universally valid, would also hold after taking expectations.

For the original Gyires inequality, the configuration model is invariant under transposition, so

\[
E\operatorname{per}(GG^T)=E\operatorname{per}(G^TG).
\]

Since \(\operatorname{per}(G^2)\ge0\), a ratio in (3) greater than 2 would already contradict the averaged original inequality and therefore imply the existence of an original Gyires counterexample. This sufficient threshold does not use the nonnegative square term.

An expectation ratio below these thresholds gives no pointwise theorem: exceptional matrices could still violate the inequality.

## 6. A precise remaining induction lemma

The following is a **conjectural sufficient lemma**, not a proof of the general Gyires inequality. It is stated for the larger class required to make the induction legitimate.

Let \(p\) be a nonnegative homogeneous real-stable polynomial of degree \(n\) in \(n\) variables, normalized by

\[
p(\mathbf1)=1,\qquad \partial_jp(\mathbf1)=1\quad(1\le j\le n).
\]

Write

\[
N(p)=\sum_{|\alpha|=n}\alpha!p_\alpha^2.
\]

For each coordinate let

\[
q_j=\left.\partial_jp\right|_{x_j=0}.
\]

This is a degree-\(n-1\) stable polynomial in \(n-1\) variables. Choose positive scaling parameters \(D_j=(d_{j,i})_{i\ne j}\) that attain its capacity,

\[
\inf_{x_i>0}\frac{q_j(x)}{\prod_{i\ne j}x_i}.
\]

The normalized polynomial \(q_j(D_jx)/q_j(D_j\mathbf1)\) is then balanced. Define the scale-invariant quantity

\[
H_j=\frac{N(q_j(D_jx))}{\prod_{i\ne j}d_{j,i}^2}.
\tag{4}
\]

One can fix the gauge \(\prod_i d_{j,i}=1\), as in the numerical code.

**Unproved adaptive deletion lemma.** With \(g_n=((n-1)/n)^{n-1}\),

\[
\max_{1\le j\le n}H_j\ \ge\ g_nN(p).
\tag{5}
\]

All numerical experiments below actually test the stronger average assertion

\[
\frac1n\sum_jH_j\ \ge\ g_nN(p).
\tag{6}
\]

### Why this would solve the problem

Assume inductively that every balanced stable polynomial of degree \(n-1\) in \(n-1\) variables satisfies

\[
[x_1\cdots x_{n-1}]q\,^2\ge c_{n-1}N(q).
\]

Apply this to the normalized scaled deletion. Its central coefficient before scalar normalization is \(p_{\mathbf1}\prod_i d_{j,i}\). The scalar normalizations cancel, yielding

\[
p_{\mathbf1}^2\ge c_{n-1}H_j\quad\hbox{for every }j.
\]

Combining this with (5) gives

\[
p_{\mathbf1}^2\ge c_{n-1}g_nN(p)=c_nN(p).
\]

The order-one base case is equality. The order-two case follows directly: balance gives \(p=ax^2+bxy+ay^2\), and stability gives \(b\ge2a\), exactly equivalent to \(b^2\ge\tfrac12N(p)\).

For \(p=p_A\), the conclusion is the strong Gram permanent inequality and hence implies the original Gyires inequality. **An induction proved only for products of linear forms would be invalid:** the deletion \(q_j\) is generally a sum of such products. This is why the broader stable class is essential to the proposed argument.

### Capacity attainment and boundary cases

For a polynomial with all degree-\(n\) monomial coefficients positive, each deletion has full support, and its capacity minimizer exists and is unique up to common scaling. To reduce the desired final Fock inequality to this case, replace a balanced stable polynomial by

\[
p(T_\varepsilon x),\qquad
T_\varepsilon=(1-\varepsilon)I+\varepsilon J_n,
\quad0<\varepsilon<1.
\]

This substitution preserves stability and balance, gives positive coefficients, and tends coefficientwise to \(p\). The final Fock inequality then passes to the limit. This avoids asserting attainment for arbitrary boundary deletions. Adding an arbitrary stable polynomial to \(p\) would not be a valid regularization because stability is not generally preserved under addition.

## 7. Raw deletion and the exact cubic reduction

Before capacity scaling, coefficient extraction gives the exact identity

\[
\sum_jN(q_j)=\sum_\alpha
\#\{j:\alpha_j=1\}\,\alpha!p_\alpha^2.
\tag{7}
\]

Thus the raw average assertion is precisely

\[
E_{\nu_p}\#\{j:\alpha_j=1\}\ge ng_n,
\qquad
\nu_p(\alpha)=\frac{\alpha!p_\alpha^2}{N(p)}.
\]

It also passed all stable-polynomial and Lorentzian tests below. The capacity-scaled sum was no larger than the raw sum in those tests, but **no general monotonicity theorem is claimed**. Even a proof of the raw assertion would not by itself justify the induction, because unscaled deletions need not be balanced.

For \(n=3\), write each deletion as

\[
q_j=a_jx^2+bxy+c_jy^2,
\qquad b=p_{111}.
\]

Balancing a binary quadratic gives exactly

\[
H_j=b^2+4a_jc_j,
\]

including boundary cases by continuity. Consequently the average lemma is the homogeneous quadratic coefficient inequality

\[
G(p):=3\sum_jH_j-4N(p)\ge0.
\tag{8}
\]

Writing the three pure-cube coefficients as \(U_i\), this is equivalently

\[
G(p)=5b^2-\sum_j\bigl[(a_j+c_j)^2+7(a_j-c_j)^2\bigr]
-24\sum_iU_i^2.
\tag{9}
\]

The subset described by \(G\ge0\), \(b\ge0\) is a convex Lorentz cone in coefficient space. This observation does not imply that all balanced stable cubics belong to it.

### Exact result for cubic DS products

The analytic agent established (8) for every \(3\times3\) doubly stochastic product polynomial by an exact sorted-Birkhoff coefficient certificate. Expand the polynomial \(G(p_A)\) in the six permutation weights of a Birkhoff decomposition, then substitute ordered-gap variables. It is enough to check 120 orderings after fixing the smallest-weight permutation by row-permutation invariance; this covers all 720 orderings. Every resulting coefficient is nonnegative. This establishes the cubic product case, **not the general stable cubic case and not an induction in arbitrary order**.

Reproducible certificate and independently implemented reviews:

- `round3_deletion_n3_certificate.py` and `round3_deletion_n3_birkhoff.json`.
- `round3_deletion_n3_review.py` and `round3_deletion_n3_review.json`.
- `round3_deletion_n3_independent_audit.py` and `round3_deletion_n3_independent_audit.json`.

An additional exact integer-grid check used \(3\times3\) integer matrices with every row and column summing to \(k\). For \(k=3,5,8,12\), all 55, 231, 1,035, and 4,186 matrices respectively passed (8). This finite grid was exploratory; the Birkhoff certificate is the general proof for DS products.

## 8. Reproducible numerical evidence for the induction lemma

The following are **numerical tests of (6), not proofs**. Raw coefficient construction is exact integer arithmetic; capacity balancing and Fock ratios are floating-point calculations. Each saved record reports gradient residuals so that the numerical balance quality can be assessed.

### Stable-polynomial suite with exact initial balance

`round3_deletion_probe.py` and `round3_deletion_probe.json` contain 208 examples: 52 at each order \(n=3,4,5,6\). At each order there are 40 products of elementary-symmetric polynomials after the same doubly stochastic substitution, and 12 determinant polynomials of exactly balanced PSD tuples constructed from Householder matrices.

| Order | Examples | Minimum scaled average ratio | Minimum raw singleton average ratio |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 3 | 52 | 1.0000000 | 1.0000000 |
| 4 | 52 | 1.0373282 | 1.0375230 |
| 5 | 52 | 1.0242451 | 1.0242792 |
| 6 | 52 | 1.0218714 | 1.0218926 |

The ratio in the third column is \(\sum_jH_j/(ng_nN(p))\); the fourth uses unscaled deletions. The largest reported balancing gradient residual is less than \(4.5\times10^{-8}\). Every tested final Fock inequality also passed.

### Stable-polynomial suite with adversarial substitutions

`round3_deletion_adversarial.py` and `round3_deletion_adversarial.json` contain 360 examples: 90 at each order \(n=3,4,5,6\). Each order includes 60 products of elementary-symmetric polynomials on different positive linear forms, with different factors favoring different overlapping subsets of variables, and 30 generic PSD determinant pencils. The complete input parameters are in the JSON. Initial balancing and all deletion balancing use convex log-sum-exp minimization.

| Order | Examples | Minimum scaled average ratio | Minimum raw singleton average ratio |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|

The largest balancing gradient residual is less than \(2.4\times10^{-10}\). The scaled/raw sum ratio ranged from approximately 0.95592 to 0.999997. Every tested final Fock inequality also passed.

For determinant polynomials, coefficients were recovered exactly by multivariate finite differences of integer determinants. For \(|\alpha|=n\),

\[
p_\alpha=\frac1{\alpha!}
\sum_{\beta\le\alpha}(-1)^{n-|\beta|}
\binom\alpha\beta\det\!\left(\sum_i\beta_iM_i\right).
\]

The code uses integer Bareiss elimination for determinant evaluation and verifies integrality and nonnegative coefficients.

### Lorentzian stress tests from nonstable source matroids

`round3_matroid_deletion_probe.py` and `round3_matroid_deletion_probe.json` contain 750 examples, 150 in each of the following five families: Fano rank-three basis polynomials; dual-Fano rank-four basis polynomials; and direct sums of Fano with one, two or three coloops, giving degrees four, five and six. Every ground-set variable is replaced by a positive linear form in as many variables as the polynomial's degree, then the resulting polynomial is capacity-balanced.

These inputs are certified Lorentzian by Brändén–Huh, *Lorentzian polynomials*, Theorems 2.10 and 3.10: nonnegative linear substitutions preserve Lorentzianity, and matroid basis generating polynomials are Lorentzian. The Fano source is not stable; a particular substitution may nevertheless become stable. These tests probe a possible Lorentzian extension of the lemma; they do not establish one.

## 9. Reproduction and final status

The reproducible stable and matroid test suites contain **1,318 examples** in total: 208 exactly initially balanced stable examples, 360 capacity-balanced stable examples, and 750 Lorentzian matroid examples. No scaled-average, raw-average or final-Fock violation was found. The main scripts can be rerun from this directory using Python 3 with NumPy and SciPy:

```text
python round3_deletion_probe.py
python round3_deletion_adversarial.py
python round3_matroid_deletion_probe.py
```

The adversarial script imports exact polynomial/determinant helpers from `round3_deletion_probe.py`. The matroid script imports helpers from both earlier scripts. The exact regular-graph script `round3_annealed.py` is independent of these numerical capacity routines. The cubic proof and its independent reviews are separate exact-arithmetic scripts listed in Section 7.

**Proved in this note:** the explicit failures of the KL and weaker-dependence approaches; the exact configuration-model expectation formulas and finite comparisons; the validity of the conditional reduction (5) to the strong inequality; and the exact algebraic cubic deletion reduction. The cubic DS-product average inequality additionally has the separate exact Birkhoff certificate.

**Unproved:** the adaptive deletion lemma in arbitrary order, its stable cubic generalization, a universal raw singleton bound, any universal Lorentzian extension, and the unrestricted Gyires inequality itself. Numerical passes do not close these gaps. The induction lemma identifies a concrete global bottleneck, but the full general solution has not been obtained.
