## 1. Precise defect and exact computation

For the nonnegative circulant `C(w)_{ij}=w_{j-i mod n}`, write

\[
D_n(w)=n^n\operatorname{per}(C(w))^2-n!\operatorname{per}(C(w)C(w)^T).
\]

`circ_gap_test.py` computes both permanent polynomials from their defining subset dynamic program. The Gram entries are the exact autocorrelations `g_j=sum_i w_i w_(i+j)`. Exponent vectors are encoded in base `2n+1`; every exponent throughout is at most `2n`, so multiplication by addition of encoded exponents cannot produce carries. No floating-point arithmetic is involved.

For a sorting permutation pi, it substitutes `w_pi(k)=x_0+...+x_k` by a variable permutation followed by elementary substitutions from the largest index downward. Affine relabellings `j -> a*j+b (mod n)`, with a a unit, preserve both permanents. Setting pi(0)=0 and taking the lexicographically least unit multiple gives exactly `n!/(n phi(n))` chamber representatives.

All coefficients in every representative are nonnegative. The zero coefficients are precisely the constant-weight ray monomial `x_0^(2n)` and the `n-1` monomials `x_0^(2n-1)*x_j`. Indeed D_n and all its first partial derivatives vanish at constant weights: at w_i=t, `per(C)=n!t^n`, each derivative of `per(C)` is `n!t^(n-1)`, `per(CC^T)=n! n^n t^(2n)`, and each derivative of this Gram permanent is `2n! n^n t^(2n-1)`. These supply n guaranteed zero coefficients; the computed positive-term count leaves no other zero coefficients. In particular all pure gap powers have coefficient at least the stated constant.

### Mathematical consequences of the completed exact coefficient checks

For every nonnegative order-six circulant and its five consecutive sorted gaps z_j,

\[
324\operatorname{per}(C)^2-5\operatorname{per}(CC^T)
\ge296\sum_{j=1}^{5}z_j^{12}.
\]

Here `gcd(6^6,6!)=144`. The coefficient 296 is sharp: at `w=(1,1,0,0,0,0)`, `per(C)=2`, `per(CC^T)=200`, and the sum of gap powers is 1. Thus the left side is `324*4-5*200=296`.

For every nonnegative order-seven circulant and its six consecutive sorted gaps,

\[
117649\operatorname{per}(C)^2-720\operatorname{per}(CC^T)
\ge116929\sum_{j=1}^{6}z_j^{14}.
\]

Here `gcd(7^7,7!)=7`. The constant 116929 is sharp at `w=(1,0,0,0,0,0,0)`, for which both permanents and the sum of gap powers are 1.

In both sizes the un-margined strong inequality is equality exactly when every weight is equal, by the positive gap margin and direct evaluation of a constant matrix. These strong inequalities imply the original Gyires inequality, using normality of circulants and the permanent Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, as in the existing order-five manuscript.

These conclusions are finite exact algebraic certificates. They have not been formalized in Lean in this subtask. A full all-order circulant or arbitrary doubly stochastic theorem has not been obtained.

## 3. A concrete obstruction to a tempting fully general proof

One might try replacing the n disjoint cyclic permutation matrices by an arbitrary Birkhoff decomposition and asserting that sorting all permutation weights always makes the defect coefficientwise nonnegative. That assertion is false, already at n=4.

Let E be the 4 by 4 all-ones matrix and I the identity. Among the 24 permutation matrices:

- their sum is `6E`;
- the identity plus the nine derangements sum to `3E-2I` (each off-diagonal position occurs in three derangements).

These are nested subsets of permutation matrices. Accordingly,

\[
A(t,u,v)=6tE+u(3E-2I)+vI,\qquad t,u,v\ge0,
\]

is a restriction to three gap variables of an actual sorting chamber for the 24 permutation coefficients: baseline all 24, a tail of ten consisting of I and all derangements, and the singleton tail I.

For

\[
D_4(A)=4^4\operatorname{per}(A)^2-4!\operatorname{per}(AA^T),
\]

the exact coefficient is

\[
[t^6uv]D_4(A)=-6,879,707,136.
\]

For the reduced defect `32 per(A)^2-3 per(AA^T)`, it is `-859,963,392`. There are twelve negative coefficients in this restricted polynomial. This is **not** a counterexample to Gyires's inequality or to the strong inequality; it disproves the proposed coefficientwise proof strategy for arbitrary permutation lists.

### Independent analytical derivation of the negative coefficient

Put `H=I-E/4`, so `||H||_F^2=3`. The centered parts of the u and v directions are `-2H` and `H`, respectively. For a matrix with zero row and column sums X,

\[
\operatorname{per}(aE+X)
=4!a^4+\tfrac12(4-2)!a^2\|X\|_F^2+O(X^3).
\]

The Gram permanent has no quadratic term in X: `(aE+X)(aE+X)^T=4a^2E+XX^T`, and the sum of the entries of `XX^T` is zero. The quadratic part of D_4 is therefore

\[
4^4\,4!\,(4-2)!\,a^6\|X\|_F^2.
\]

At `a=6t` and `X=(-2u+v)H`, the coefficient is

\[
256\cdot24\cdot2\cdot6^6\cdot(-12)
=-6,879,707,136.
\]

The changing constant component of the u and v directions cannot affect this mixed term of total degree two in u,v.

For *disjoint* Latin-square permutation layers, this quadratic obstruction disappears: if nested supports contain a and b disjoint layers with a<=b, the Frobenius inner product of their centered indicator matrices equals `a(n-b)>=0`. This explains why the Birkhoff counterexample does not refute the narrower circulant or Latin-square possibility. It does not control higher coefficients.

## 4. Dimension-unbounded subgroup-support consequence

Suppose n=km and the support of the circulant weights lies in a coset of a subgroup of the cyclic group of size m. A shift of weights removes the coset translation; after row and column permutations C is a direct sum of k identical m by m circulant blocks B. Thus

\[
\frac{\operatorname{per}(C)^2}{\operatorname{per}(CC^T)}
=\left(\frac{\operatorname{per}(B)^2}{\operatorname{per}(BB^T)}\right)^k
\ge c_m^k\ge c_{km},\quad c_r=r!/r^r.
\]

The last inequality follows from the elementary multinomial bound

\[
\frac{(km)!}{(m!)^k}\le k^{km}.
\]

Consequently any validated order-m strong theorem gives a family in arbitrarily large dimensions. In particular the existing order-five result, and the independently checked finite computations in orders six and seven, cover circulants with support in such subgroup cosets. This is a closure consequence, not a solution for unrestricted circulants. For a nonzero matrix and k>1, the multinomial inequality is strict.
