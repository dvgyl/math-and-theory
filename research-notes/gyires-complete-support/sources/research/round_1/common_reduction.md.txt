# Permanent Gram comparison and the reduction to Gyires

This note supplies the elementary common reduction used throughout the package. It is the same mathematical identity used in the existing order-five manuscript; it is not a new result.

For a real n-by-n matrix M, define coefficients m_alpha by

\[
\prod_{i=1}^n\left(\sum_j M_{ij}x_j\right)=\sum_{|\alpha|=n}m_\alpha x^\alpha,
\qquad \alpha!=\prod_j\alpha_j!.
\]

For a second matrix N, define n_alpha in the same way. Expanding the permanent and grouping maps with common occupancy vectors gives

\[
\operatorname{per}(MN^T)=\sum_\alpha \alpha!m_\alpha n_\alpha.
\]

For any two maps from the n row labels to column labels with the same occupancy alpha, exactly alpha! row permutations carry one map to the other. This explains the multiplicity in the expansion.

Ordinary Cauchy–Schwarz therefore gives

\[
|\operatorname{per}(MN^T)|^2
\le\operatorname{per}(MM^T)\operatorname{per}(NN^T).
\]

Set M=A, N=A^T. With p=per(A), g_L=per(AA^T), g_R=per(A^TA), and q=per(A^2),

\[
2q\le g_L+g_R.
\]

If for some delta>=0 both strong margins satisfy

\[
p^2-c_ng_L\ge\delta,\qquad p^2-c_ng_R\ge\delta,
\]

then

\[
G(A)=4p^2-c_n(g_L+g_R+2q)
\ge4p^2-2c_n(g_L+g_R)\ge4\delta.
\]

In particular, a strictly positive strong margin away from J_n proves the equality clause of Gyires on that family.

Circulants are normal, so g_L=g_R and q<=g_L. Their single strong inequality therefore suffices. A nonnegative circulant with a nonzero weight has positive permanent: the corresponding cyclic permutation contributes that weight to the nth power. Scaling its common row sum to one gives a doubly stochastic matrix. At constant weights the strong and Gyires defects vanish directly; among doubly stochastic matrices this is exactly J_n.

The original conjecture's denominator is positive for doubly stochastic A. For example, van der Waerden's theorem applies to each of AA^T, A^TA and A^2, all of which are doubly stochastic. The polynomial form and ratio form are therefore equivalent on this domain.
