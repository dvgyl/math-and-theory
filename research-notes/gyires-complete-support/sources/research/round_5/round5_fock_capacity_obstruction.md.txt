# Why a Fock-capacity shortcut does not close the rank-two induction

Let p=p_A for a doubly stochastic rank-two matrix, and define

\[
\mathcal C_F(p)=\inf_{D>0}\frac{N(p(Dx))}{(\det D)^2},
\qquad g_n=\left(\frac{n-1}{n}\right)^{n-1}.
\]

One proposed route to the zero-column deletion estimate was to prove

\[
\frac{\mathcal C_F(p)}{N(p)}\ge\frac{g_n}{g_d},
\]

where d is the number of positive entries in a column containing a zero. This comparison is false, even for a matrix with n-1 identical columns.

## Exact finite certificate

Take n=50 and

\[
u=(0,1/2,\underbrace{1/96,\ldots,1/96}_{48}),
\quad A_{i1}=u_i,\quad A_{ij}=(1-u_i)/49\quad(j\ge2).
\]

Every row and column sum is one. The matrix has rank two and exactly one zero entry, so d=49. Its special-variable occupancy law is

\[
P=\operatorname{Bern}(1/2)+\operatorname{Bin}(48,1/96),
\quad B=\operatorname{Bin}(50,1/50).
\]

Define C(z)=sum_k P_k^2 z^k/B_k. Scaling only the first variable by sqrt(z) gives

\[
\frac{\mathcal C_F(p)}{N(p)}\le\frac{C(z)}{zC(1)}.
\]

At z=31/32, the standard-library Fraction computation in the accompanying script proves exactly

\[
\frac{C(31/32)}{(31/32)C(1)}
<\frac{99973}{100000}
<\frac{99979}{100000}
<\frac{g_{50}}{g_{49}}.
\]

For orientation only, the trial upper bound is approximately 0.9997278689191665, and the proposed lower bound approximately 0.9997931485695676. The proof uses the rational inequalities, not these decimals.

## Why the failure persists as the dimension grows

More generally take u=(0,a,(1-a)/(n-2),..., (1-a)/(n-2)), where 0<a<1, and put b=1-a. The special occupancy law tends to Bern(a)+Pois(b), and the binomial reference tends to Pois(1). Its limiting collision generating function is

\[
C_\infty(z)=e^{1-2b+b^2z}\left[b^2(1+az)^2+a^2z\right].
\]

Passage to this infinite sum can be justified by dominated convergence. For the finite laws, extend the coefficients by zero beyond their support. For k>=1,
\[
P_{n,k}\le \frac{b^{k-1}(b^2+ak)}{k!},\qquad
B_{n,k}^{-1}\le e^{k+2}k!\quad(0\le k\le n).
\]
The k=0 term is bounded separately. These estimates give a summable majorant for the collision series and its derivative on every compact z-interval. The second bound follows from (n)_k/n^k >= e^{-k} and (1-1/n)^n >= 1/4 > e^{-2} for n>=2.

Its logarithmic derivative at one satisfies

\[
\frac{C_\infty'(1)}{C_\infty(1)}-1
=\frac{a^4b^2}{1-a^2+a^4}>0.
\]

Thus a scale slightly below one decreases C(z)/z by a fixed amount in the limit, while g_n/g_(n-1) tends to one. The exact order-50 certificate suffices for the refutation; this limit explains the mechanism.

## Scope

This is not a counterexample to the actual deletion estimate, to the strong Gram inequality, or to original Gyires. The matrix is in the one-special-column family proved previously. The failure shows that a proof of the rank-two zero-column deletion estimate must retain more information than the global Fock capacity alone.
