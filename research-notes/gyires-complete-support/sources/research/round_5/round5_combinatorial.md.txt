# Round 5: balanced M-convex supports and precise obstructions

## Status and scope

This round does **not** prove the unrestricted Gyires conjecture, its stronger Gram inequality, or the proposed Lorentzian extension. It develops an exact geometric reformulation on a substantial Lorentzian subclass, proves two obstructions to natural interpolation arguments, and records reproducible structural checks. None of the numerical checks is an exact certificate of a general inequality. No novelty claim is made.

The new all-order scalar results are in `round5_scalar.md`, with my separate independent review in `round5_scalar_independent_audit.md`. Those are actual proofs on their stated families. They do not establish the full multivariate claim.

Throughout, let

\[
c_n=\frac{n!}{n^n},\qquad
N(p)=\sum_{|\alpha|=n}\alpha!p_\alpha^2,
\qquad Q_\alpha=\frac{c_n}{\alpha!}.
\]

For a nonnegative homogeneous degree-$n$ polynomial in $n$ variables, balanced means $\partial_i p(\mathbf1)=p(\mathbf1)$ for every $i$. The stronger target is

\[
p_{\mathbf1}^2\ge c_nN(p).
\tag{1}
\]

For a doubly stochastic matrix product polynomial, this is exactly $\operatorname{per}(A)^2\ge c_n\operatorname{per}(AA^T)$.

## 1. An exact geometric target for M-convex supports

Let $S$ be an M-convex subset of the degree-$n$ compositions, and consider its exponential-generating polynomial

\[
p_S(x)=\sum_{\alpha\in S}\frac{x^\alpha}{\alpha!}.
\]

Theorem 3.10 of Brändén–Huh implies that this polynomial is Lorentzian. Their Theorem 2.10 implies that positive variable scaling preserves Lorentzianity. Assume first that $\mathbf1$ lies in the relative interior of the convex hull of $S$, so the capacity-balancing tilt exists, modulo affine redundancies. Put

\[
F_S(z)=\sum_{\alpha\in S}Q_\alpha
e^{(\alpha-\mathbf1)\cdot z},
\qquad \nabla F_S(z_*)=0.
\tag{2}
\]

One may work in the gauge $\sum_i z_i=0$. The polynomial $p_S(e^{z_{*,1}}x_1,\ldots,e^{z_{*,n}}x_n)$ is balanced and still has central coefficient one. Direct substitution gives

\[
c_nN\bigl(p_S(e^{z_*}x)\bigr)=F_S(2z_*).
\]

Thus (1) becomes the precise finite-dimensional geometric assertion

\[
\boxed{F_S(2z_*)\le1.}
\tag{3}
\]

Boundary supports can be treated by limits on the minimal face containing $\mathbf1$. The numerical code records when its tilt approaches such a limit; it does not turn that limiting computation into a formal certificate.

### An entropy identity special to this subclass

Define the balanced information projection

\[
P_*(\alpha)=\frac{Q_\alpha\mathbf1_{\{\alpha\in S\}}
e^{(\alpha-\mathbf1)\cdot z_*}}{F_S(z_*)}.
\]

Its mean is $\mathbf1$, so

\[
D(P_*\Vert Q)=-\log F_S(z_*).
\]

Its central likelihood ratio is $r_{\mathbf1}=1/F_S(z_*)$. Consequently, unlike the failed KL bridge for general matrix products, here there is an exact identity

\[
D(P_*\Vert Q)=\log r_{\mathbf1}.
\]

Since

\[
D_2(P_*\Vert Q)=\log F_S(2z_*)-2\log F_S(z_*),
\]

(3) is equivalent to

\[
D_2(P_*\Vert Q)\le2D(P_*\Vert Q).
\tag{4}
\]

This equivalence supplies a specific remaining entropy inequality. It does not prove it.

### A possible discrete strengthening

A stronger, still unproved statement is that $F_S(2z_S)$ is nondecreasing under inclusion of M-convex supports $S\subseteq T$. The full composition support has value one. Therefore support-inclusion monotonicity would imply (3). The tests below found no counterexample, but the continuous analogues in Sections 3 and 4 are false.

## 2. A legitimate Lorentzian interpolation operation

Let $p$ be Lorentzian and let $r$ be its maximum degree in $x_i$. Multiply all coefficients with $\alpha_i=r$ by a common factor $\lambda\in[0,1]$; denote the result by $p_\lambda$.

**Claim.** Every $p_\lambda$ is Lorentzian.

For $\lambda>0$, the support is unchanged and hence remains M-convex. Consider any quadratic derivative $\partial^\beta p$, where $|\beta|=n-2$, and its Hessian. There are only three affected cases:

1. If $\beta_i=r-2$, only the $ii$ diagonal entry decreases. Subtracting a positive semidefinite diagonal matrix cannot increase the number of positive eigenvalues.
2. If $\beta_i=r-1$, the $ii$ entry is zero and only the $i$th row and column change. The new Hessian is a diagonal congruence of the old one, with the $i$th diagonal scaling factor $\lambda$.
3. If $\beta_i=r$, the entire potentially nonzero Hessian is multiplied by $\lambda$; its $i$th row and column were already zero.

Every other Hessian is unchanged or zero. Thus each quadratic derivative continues to have at most one positive eigenvalue, which is the Lorentzian criterion together with M-convex support. The case $\lambda=0$ follows by closedness of the Lorentzian cone.

Positive variable scaling then permits capacity balancing at each positive $\lambda$, whenever the center is interior. This provides a rigorously admissible interpolation family. Its balanced Fock objective, however, has neither of the two simple properties one might hope for next.

## 3. Continuous coefficient-filling monotonicity is false

Take the one-variable reference $B=\operatorname{Bin}(3,1/3)$, so

\[
(B_0,B_1,B_2,B_3)=\frac1{27}(8,12,6,1).
\]

Use likelihood coefficients $(1,1,1,\lambda)$ before balancing. This is the top-layer attenuation of the full binomial polynomial and is an ultra-log-concave, hence binary Lorentzian, family for $0\le\lambda\le1$.

The balancing tilt $t>0$ is determined by

\[
6t^2+2\lambda t^3=8.
\]

After normalizing the central likelihood to one, the squared likelihood norm is

\[
\Phi(\lambda)=\frac8{27t^2}+\frac{12}{27}
 +\frac{6t^2}{27}+\frac{\lambda^2t^4}{27}.
\]

At $\lambda=0$, implicit differentiation gives

\[
t=\frac2{\sqrt3},\qquad t'(0)=-\frac29,
\qquad \Phi(0)=\frac{26}{27},
\qquad
\boxed{\Phi'(0)=-\frac4{81\sqrt3}<0.}
\]

At $\lambda=1$, $t=1$ and $\Phi(1)=1$. Thus filling a missing coefficient can initially **decrease** the balanced norm. This rules out a continuously monotone coefficient-filling proof. It does not disprove discrete support-inclusion monotonicity or (1).

## 4. Capacity-balanced norm is not convex under this interpolation

Here is a separate exact obstruction to a convexity argument. In three variables, define

\[
p_\lambda(x,y,z)=
\frac{y^3+3y^2z+3yz^2}{6}
+\frac{x(y+z)^2}{2}
+\lambda\frac{x^2(y+z)}2,
\qquad 0\le\lambda\le1.
\tag{5}
\]

At $\lambda=1$, this is the exponential-generating polynomial on all cubic compositions except $(3,0,0)$ and $(0,0,3)$, an M-convex support. Section 2 proves Lorentzianity throughout the interval. The $xyz$ coefficient is always one.

Let $G(\lambda)=c_3N(\widehat p_\lambda)$, where $\widehat p_\lambda$ is capacity balanced while preserving central coefficient one. At zero use the limiting balanced polynomial $x(y+z)^2/2$. Then

\[
G(0)=\frac49.
\]

### Exact right derivative at zero

Write $y=1,z=t,x=u$, and set

\[
R(t)=\frac{1+3t+3t^2}{6},\quad
Q(t)=\frac{(1+t)^2}{2},\quad
L(t)=\frac{1+t}{2}.
\]

The capacity expression is

\[
\frac{p_\lambda(u,1,t)}{ut}
=\frac{R(t)}{ut}+\frac{Q(t)}t+\lambda\frac{uL(t)}t.
\]

Minimizing in $u$ gives $u^2=R/(\lambda L)$. Put $\varepsilon=\sqrt\lambda$. The remaining minimization is of

\[
\frac{Q(t)}t+2\varepsilon\frac{\sqrt{R(t)L(t)}}t.
\]

The minimum satisfies

\[
t=1+k\varepsilon+O(\varepsilon^2),\qquad
k=\frac3{14}\sqrt{\frac76},\qquad k^2=\frac3{56}.
\]

This follows from $(Q/t)''(1)=1$ and
$[2\sqrt{RL}/t]'(1)=-(3/14)\sqrt{7/6}$; the implicit function theorem applies at this strict minimum.

After dividing the scaled polynomial by $ut$ to preserve its central coefficient, its Fock norm is exactly

\[
N=1+\frac{t^2+t^{-2}}2+\lambda H(t),
\]

where

\[
H(t)=\frac{L(t)}{R(t)}
 \left(\frac1{6t^2}+\frac12+\frac{t^2}2\right)
+\frac{R(t)}{2L(t)}(t^{-2}+1),
\qquad H(1)=\frac{13}6.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{G'(0+)=\frac29\left(2k^2+\frac{13}6\right)
=\frac{191}{378}.}
\tag{6}
\]

### The endpoint chord has smaller slope

At $\lambda=1$, symmetry makes the balancing scales for $x,z$ equal. Their ratio $t$ to the $y$ scale satisfies

\[
3t^3-3t-1=0,\qquad \frac98<t<\frac87.
\]

The endpoint value is

\[
G(1)=\frac29\left(t^2+2+t^{-2}+\frac1{6t^4}\right).
\]

Using only the rational interval for $t$,

\[
t^2+2+t^{-2}+\frac1{6t^4}
<2+\frac{64}{49}+\frac{64}{81}+\frac{2048}{19683}
<\frac{359}{84}.
\]

Consequently,

\[
G(1)<\frac{359}{378}=G(0)+G'(0+).
\]

A convex function on $[0,1]$ must have right derivative at zero no greater than its endpoint chord slope. This strict reverse inequality proves that $G$ is **not convex**. Again, it is an interpolation obstruction, not a counterexample to (1). The numerical endpoint value is approximately $0.9258037872$, well below one.

## 5. Support alone does not upper-bound a product polynomial

Even proving (3) for every M-convex support would not by itself settle the matrix problem. The exponential-generating coefficients do not maximize the central-normalized Fock norm among product polynomials with the same support.

Let $C=I+P_4$, where $P_4$ is the four-cycle permutation matrix. Its product polynomial has central coefficient two and Fock norm 36. Thus

\[
\frac{N(p_C)}{(p_C)_{\mathbf1}^2}=9.
\]

On the same support, $p_S=\sum_{\alpha\in S}x^\alpha/\alpha!$ is already balanced by cyclic symmetry. The support consists of the center, twelve patterns with $\alpha!=2$, and two patterns with $\alpha!=4$. Hence

\[
N(p_S)=p_S(\mathbf1)=1+\frac{12}2+\frac24=\frac{15}2<9.
\]

Both satisfy (1), but the proposed same-support domination runs in the wrong direction. An additional coefficient-sensitive argument is necessary.

## 6. Structural checks, with exact and numerical parts separated

### Exhaustive supports in degree three

The code enumerated every subset of the ten degree-three compositions containing $(1,1,1)$ and checked the M-convex exchange axiom exactly. There are 57 labeled supports and 17 coordinate-permutation orbits. Numerical capacity balancing found no violation of (3). Some supports require balancing limits. The largest gradient residual was approximately $1.67\times10^{-12}$.

### Full-dimensional integral-polymatroid supports in degree four

The rank-function parametrization is exhaustive for M-convex supports whose convex hull is full dimensional in the degree-four affine hyperplane and has the center in its interior. For a proper subset $T$, strict interiority requires $\rho(T)>|T|$. Singleton ranks are therefore 2 through 4, pair ranks 3 or 4, and triple ranks 4.

There are 5,184 candidate assignments. Exact monotonicity and submodularity checks leave 615 rank functions, representing 63 coordinate-permutation support orbits. Each resulting support also passed an exact exchange-axiom check. All numerical values in (3) are at most one. The largest proper-support value is approximately $0.9960941728$, at the full support with one pure vertex removed. The largest balancing residual is approximately $9.63\times10^{-12}$.

All 45,456 strict inclusion pairs among the 615 labeled supports were compared, including their relative coordinate permutations. No numerical violation of the support-inclusion conjecture was found.

This is not a new proof of the $4\times4$ matrix inequality. The current linked repositories already prove the stronger matrix inequality for all $4\times4$ doubly stochastic matrices by a different, finite certificate method.

### A targeted degree-five path search

To test support inclusion beyond degree four, a deterministic beam followed valid one-unit decreases of integral submodular ranks, favoring large balanced norm, large balancing tilt, and small support. It visited 636 distinct supports and checked 1,537 rank-step inclusion comparisons through depth 33. This search was not exhaustive.

Every rank/submodularity assertion was exact. Balancing and norm values were numerical. No target or inclusion failure was found. The largest proper-support value was approximately $0.9996800006$. The largest state residual was approximately $1.51\times10^{-8}$, so these records must not be described as rigorously certified capacity minimizers.

Fifty top-layer attenuation paths from asymmetric supports were also sampled at seven interior parameters each. The largest interior value was approximately $0.6914288203$; no norm-bound violation was found. There were 252 samples above the endpoint chord. These numerical indications motivated the exact nonconvexity proof in Section 4. The largest interior balancing residual was approximately $1.33\times10^{-12}$.

## 7. The proposed duplication bridge on the saved supports

The parent investigation proposed

\[
[x_1^2\cdots x_n^2]p(x)^2\ge K_nN(p),
\qquad K_n=\frac{(2n)!}{2^n n!n^n}.
\tag{7}
\]

For an EGF support with determinant-one balancing scale, the duplication coefficient is independent of the tilt and is the exact rational number

\[
d_S=\sum_{\substack{\alpha\in S\\2\mathbf1-\alpha\in S}}
\frac1{\alpha!(2\mathbf1-\alpha)!}.
\]

Thus the tested ratio is exactly expressed as

\[
\frac{d_S}{K_nN(p_S(e^{z_*}x))}
=\frac{c_nd_S}{K_nF_S(2z_*)}.
\]

Using the already saved balancing results, (7) passed all 17 degree-three support orbits, 63 degree-four support orbits, and 636 degree-five rank states. The minimum numerical ratio was one at the full support in each size. Only $d_S$ and the constants were exact; the denominator was numerical. No additional sampling or optimization was used for this test.

Separately, `round5_scalar.md` Section 7 gives an actual all-order proof of (7) for the one-special-variable Lorentzian family. That proof uses a new strictly convex quadratic objective in the existing scalar localization argument and has been independently checked.

## 8. Files, dependencies, and reproduction

The scripts use Python, NumPy, and SciPy. The exact arithmetic checker uses only Python's standard library.

```bash
python round5_combinatorial.py --n 3
python round5_combinatorial.py --n 4
OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python round5_support_paths.py --n 5 --width 9 --depth 45
python round5_support_comparisons.py
python round5_combinatorial_exact_checks.py
```

The resulting records are:

- `round5_combinatorial_n3_supports.json` and `round5_combinatorial_n4_supports.json`: supports, multiplicities where applicable, balancing scales, residuals, and objective values.
- `round5_n4_support_inclusion.json`: the complete labeled degree-four inclusion comparison.
- `round5_support_paths_n5.json`: visited ranks, path statistics, continuous paths, residuals, and numerical interpolation failures.
- `round5_support_duplication.json`: exact complement-pair coefficients and numerical duplication-bridge comparisons.
- `round5_combinatorial_exact_checks.json`: exact rational arithmetic underlying Sections 3–5.

The arithmetic checker validates constants and finite polynomial sums. It is not a formal verification of the analytic arguments. The full derivations are supplied above so that the results do not depend on trusting optimization output.

## 9. What remains open after these steps

The cleanest remaining target from this route is (3), or the stronger discrete support-inclusion inequality, for arbitrary M-convex supports. Its entropy form is the specific Rényi comparison (4). Even a proof there would still require a valid coefficient-sensitive extension to general matrix products: Section 5 rules out the simplest fixed-support domination.

The scalar proof cannot simply be extended by continuously filling coefficients. Both continuous monotonicity and convexity fail on explicitly admissible Lorentzian families. This complements the multivariate Hessian-kernel obstruction from Round 4 and identifies what a successful general argument would have to avoid.
