\begin{abstract}
For a finite continuous-time Markov chain on a bidirectional ring, let $C_k$
denote the $k$th cumulant per unit time of the net crossing count of a fixed
edge. The conjectured dimension-independent interval
$-1/16\le C_3/C_1\le1$ is proved for arbitrary strictly positive rates and
nonzero affinity. Both constants are sharp. The proof combines the known positive
midpoint spectrum of the tilted generator with a finite-ring proof of the
thermodynamic uncertainty relation. The lower bound then reduces to a
two-variable polynomial inequality, certified by exact positive integer
coefficients on three rational intervals. The accompanying Lean 4
development verifies the actual finite-rate tilted-matrix theorem, including
existence of its local eigenvalue branch. The stochastic interpretation,
formal scope, prior work, and complete integer certificate are stated
explicitly. The conventional two-state and one-state cases are included
as supplements.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords.} Markov currents; unicyclic networks; cumulant
bounds; thermodynamic uncertainty; exact positivity certificate; Lean 4.

\section{The problem and the contribution}

Ptaszy\'nski~\cite{Ptaszynski2022}, Eq.~(21), proposes the universal interval
\begin{equation}\label{eq:target}
  -\frac1{16}\le \frac{C_3}{C_1}\le1
\end{equation}
for winding currents in unicyclic Markov networks. The nearest-neighbour
ring and the counted edge are specified in Eqs.~(72)--(74) of that paper.
Here $C_k$ is a cumulant per unit time; the ratio $C_3/C_1$ is the normalized
third cumulant used in that work, rather than the usual standardized third
central moment.

The original paper numerically optimizes over rates and studies an
irreversible family with one slow transition. That family already shows
that the proposed universal constants are optimal. A proof of the interval
for arbitrary bidirectional rates was not supplied. The result here proves
\eqref{eq:target} for every ring with $n\ge3$ states, strictly positive
forward and reverse rates, and nonzero affinity. The separate kurtosis,
joint-cumulant, and sharper fixed-size optimization claims of
\cite{Ptaszynski2022} are outside the theorem.

The determinant separation and the real-rooted midpoint shift used below
are established prior work: they appear in Appendix~C of Pietzonka, Barato
and Seifert~\cite{Pietzonka2016}. The thermodynamic uncertainty relation is
also established prior work~\cite{Gingrich2016}. The contribution is the
sharp third/first cumulant interval obtained by combining these ingredients
with a new exact scalar inequality. Finite constructions for the stationary law and correction terms derive
these ingredients from the rate assumptions in the formal development.

The proposed contribution is a proof of \eqref{eq:target}. Bibliographic
priority is not established by the formal proof. First-passage skewness bounds
\cite{Barato2015,Wampler2022} concern different normalizations and do not
directly supply this result. The novelty assessment remains subject to further literature comparison.

\section{Statement and conventions}\label{sec:statement}

Read indices modulo $n$. Write $u_i>0$ for the rate $i\to i+1$ and $v_i>0$
for the rate $i+1\to i$. Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:UV}
  U=\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}u_i,\qquad
  V=\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}v_i,\qquad
  A=\log(U/V).
\end{equation}
Let $N_t$ count forward minus reverse crossings of any fixed edge during
the time interval $[0,t]$. For an arbitrary initial probability distribution,
define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cumulants}
  C_k=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\cum_k(N_t)}{t}.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Sharp universal current bound]\label{thm:main}
Let $n\ge3$, let every $u_i,v_i$ be strictly positive, and suppose $U\ne V$.
The limits in \eqref{eq:cumulants} exist for $k=1,2,3$, $C_1\ne0$, and
\[
  \boxed{\displaystyle -\frac1{16}\le\frac{C_3}{C_1}\le1.}
\]
Both constants are sharp over strictly positive rates and arbitrary ring
sizes.
\end{theorem}

Let $W_s$ be the column generator tilted at the counted edge: multiply its
forward off-diagonal rate by $e^s$ and its reverse rate by $e^{-s}$, leaving
the diagonal escape rates unchanged. In particular,
$(W_0)_{i+1,i}=u_i$, $(W_0)_{i,i+1}=v_i$, and
$(W_0)_{ii}=-(u_i+v_{i-1})$.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-matrix form]\label{thm:matrix}
Under the rate assumptions of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, there exists a local
$C^3$ real branch $\lambda$ satisfying
\[
  \lambda(0)=0,\qquad \det(\lambda(s)\Id-W_s)=0
\]
for all $s$ sufficiently close to zero. Every such local branch satisfies
\[
  \lambda'(0)\ne0,\qquad
  -\frac1{16}\le\frac{\lambda'''(0)}{\lambda'(0)}\le1.
\]
\end{theorem}

The formal target is Theorem~\ref{thm:matrix}, together with its branch
existence assertion. The stationary distribution, correction terms,
midpoint spectral positivity, uncertainty inequality, and characteristic
coefficient formulas are derived from the rates; they are not extra
hypotheses. Section~\ref{sec:stochastic} gives the ordinary mathematical
identification $C_k=\lambda^{(k)}(0)$.

\begin{remark}[Scope]
At $U=V$ the mean winding current vanishes, so the literal quotient
$C_3/C_1$ is undefined. The theorem is about the ring specified above and
does not add dangling trees or additional cycles. Two states require two
distinguished transition channels; the one-state counted-event convention
is a difference of Poisson counts. These boundary models are treated in
Appendix~\ref{app:small}.
\end{remark}

\section{The stochastic and finite-matrix formulations}\label{sec:stochastic}

For a chain with state $X_t$, define the marked generating vector by
$F_i(t,s)=\E[e^{sN_t}\mathbf1_{\{X_t=i\}}]$. Conditioning over the next
infinitesimal jump gives
\[
  \partial_tF(t,s)=W_sF(t,s),\qquad F(0,s)=p_{\rm initial}.
\]
The holding rates remain unchanged, while a marked forward or reverse jump
contributes $e^s$ or $e^{-s}$. Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mgf}
  \E[e^{sN_t}]=\mathbf1^T e^{tW_s}p_{\rm initial}.
\end{equation}

For real $s$, $W_s$ is an irreducible Metzler matrix. Adding a sufficiently
large multiple of the identity produces an irreducible nonnegative matrix
with positive diagonal. Perron--Frobenius theory gives a simple real
eigenvalue $\lambda(s)$ strictly larger in real part than the other
eigenvalues, with positive left and right eigenvectors. At $s=0$ it is zero.
Simple-root perturbation gives an analytic continuation near zero. Let
$\Pi(s)$ be its analytic spectral projection. In a sufficiently small
neighbourhood of zero there is a decomposition
\[
 e^{tW_s}=e^{t\lambda(s)}\Pi(s)+R_t(s),\qquad
 a(s)=\mathbf1^T\Pi(s)p_{\rm initial}.
\]
At zero, $\Pi(0)=\pi\mathbf1^T$, so $a(0)=1$ for every initial probability
distribution. The complementary spectrum has a uniform gap. Analytic
spectral projection and finite-dimensional semigroup estimates therefore
bound the first three $s$-derivatives of $e^{-t\lambda(s)}R_t(s)$ by a
polynomial in $t$ times a uniformly decaying exponential. The same estimates
follow by analytic continuation to a smaller complex neighbourhood and
Cauchy bounds. Since $a(s)$ is analytic and nonzero near zero,
\eqref{eq:mgf} yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scgf}
  \log\E[e^{sN_t}]=t\lambda(s)+O(1),
\end{equation}
where the remainder and its first three $s$-derivatives are bounded locally.
This derivative-uniform estimate justifies differentiating
\eqref{eq:scgf} at zero and dividing by $t$, which proves existence of the
limits and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:identification}
  C_k=\lambda^{(k)}(0),\qquad k=1,2,3.
\end{equation}
This is the standard path-to-matrix identification used for finite-state
current cumulants. It is supplied here as a mathematical argument. The
Lean development does not construct a probability space of continuous-time
sample paths.

\section{The positive midpoint spectrum}\label{sec:spectrum}

For now orient the ring so that $U>V$. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Pdelta}
  P(z)=\det(z\Id-W_0),\qquad
  \delta=(\sqrt U-\sqrt V)^2>0.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Counted-edge separation]\label{lem:det}
For every real $s,z$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tiltdet}
  \det(z\Id-W_s)=P(z)-U(e^s-1)-V(e^{-s}-1).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Expand the determinant over permutations. A supported permutation of the
cycle consists of fixed vertices, adjacent transpositions, and possibly
one full forward or reverse cycle. The two counting factors cancel in an
adjacent transposition. Only the full cycles retain a counting factor;
their signed contributions are $-Ue^s$ and $-Ve^{-s}$. Subtracting the
untilted determinant proves the identity.
\end{proof}

Define the cyclic gradient matrix $B$ by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gram}
  (Bx)_i=\sqrt{u_i}\,x_i-\sqrt{v_i}\,x_{i+1},\qquad H=B^TB.
\end{equation}
Its diagonal is $u_i+v_{i-1}$, and each neighbouring off-diagonal is
$-\sqrt{u_iv_i}$. The determinant of $B$ is $\sqrt U-\sqrt V$, so $B$ is
invertible, $H$ is positive definite, and $\det H=\delta$. The same
permutation expansion gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spectralshift}
  \det(z\Id+H)=P(z)+\delta.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, $H$ is minus a diagonal conjugate of $W_{-A/2}$.
These midpoint facts are the known reduction of~\cite[Appendix C]{Pietzonka2016};
they do not require reversibility of $W_0$.

Let $\theta_i>0$ be the eigenvalues of $H$, and put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moments}
  T=\sum_i\frac1{\theta_i},\qquad
  x_i=\frac1{T\theta_i},\qquad
  p_2=\sum_ix_i^2,\qquad p_3=\sum_ix_i^3.
\end{equation}
The vector $x$ is a positive probability vector. Also define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c}
  c=\frac{\sqrt U+\sqrt V}{\sqrt U-\sqrt V}
   =\coth(A/4)>1.
\end{equation}
If $a_j$ denotes the coefficient of $z^j$ in $P$, expansion of
\eqref{eq:spectralshift} and the elementary symmetric identities give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coefficients}
  a_1=\delta T,\quad
  a_2=\frac{\delta T^2}{2}(1-p_2),\quad
  a_3=\frac{\delta T^3}{6}(1-3p_2+2p_3).
\end{equation}
In particular $a_1>0$, which also gives the local root branch by the implicit
function theorem. Differentiating
$P(\lambda(s))=U(e^s-1)+V(e^{-s}-1)$ at zero yields
\begin{align}
 a_1C_1&=U-V,\notag\\
 a_1C_2+2a_2C_1^2&=U+V,\label{eq:cumulantcoeff}\\
 a_1C_3+6a_2C_1C_2+6a_3C_1^3&=U-V.\notag
\end{align}
Use $U-V=\delta c$ and $U+V=\delta(c^2+1)/2$ to obtain
\begin{align}
 TC_1&=c,\notag\\
 TC_2&=c^2p_2-\frac{c^2-1}{2},\label{eq:ratios}\\
 S:=\frac{C_3}{C_1}
   &=c^2(3p_2^2-2p_3)+\frac{c^2-1}{2}(1-3p_2).\notag
\end{align}
Thus the remaining problem is an inequality for a probability vector and
the affinity parameter.

\section{A finite proof of the variance--affinity constraint}\label{sec:TUR}

The proof uses the thermodynamic uncertainty inequality
\begin{equation}\label{eq:TUR}
  \frac{C_2}{C_1}\ge\frac2A.
\end{equation}
It is an established result~\cite{Gingrich2016}. The following finite-ring
proof supplies all of its ingredients directly from the rates.

\subsection{Stationarity and correctors}
Let $\pi$ be the stationary probability vector and write
\[
  a_i=\pi_i u_i,\qquad b_i=\pi_{i+1}v_i.
\]
Stationarity means $a_i-b_i=J$, independently of $i$. An explicit positive
construction is
\begin{align}
 \rho_i&=v_i/u_i,\notag\\
 w_i&=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac1{u_{i+k}}
                 \prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\rho_{i+j},\label{eq:stationary}\\
 Z&=\sum_iw_i,\qquad \pi_i=w_i/Z,\qquad J=(1-V/U)/Z.\notag
\end{align}
The empty product is one. The geometric recurrence gives
$u_iw_i-v_iw_{i+1}=1-V/U$, proving positivity, normalization, and the
common flux identity. In particular $J>0$.

Let $d_i$ be the increment assigned to a forward crossing of edge $i$,
with $\sum_i d_i=1$. For a single counted edge, $d_i$ is its indicator.
The backward generator is
\[
  Qf_i=u_i(f_{i+1}-f_i)+v_{i-1}(f_{i-1}-f_i).
\]
Solve the centered Poisson equation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:poisson}
  Qh_i+u_id_i-v_{i-1}d_{i-1}=J,
  \qquad \sum_i\pi_i h_i=0.
\end{equation}
The forcing has stationary mean zero. The maximum principle gives
$\ker Q=\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf1\}$: at a maximum of a function in
the kernel, positivity of both neighbouring rates forces its neighbours
to have the same value, and this propagates around the ring. Thus $Q$ has
rank $n-1$. Since $\pi^TQ=0$, its image is precisely
$\{b:\pi^Tb=0\}$. The Poisson equation therefore has a solution, unique
after imposing the centering condition. One can also construct it by
solving a finite cyclic recurrence for the gradient and integrating a
zero-sum increment around the cycle. For an explicit cyclic integration,
if $\sum_i r_i=0$, then
\[
  h_i=-\frac1n\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}r_{i+j}
  \quad\text{satisfies}\quad h_{i+1}-h_i=r_i.
\]
Subtracting the $\pi$-mean centers $h$. These finite recurrences, including
their solvability for signed forcing, are proved in the accompanying Lean
construction.

Set $g_i=d_i+h_{i+1}-h_i$, so $\sum_i g_i=1$. The first characteristic
derivative is $C_1=J$. Differentiating the centered eigenvector equation
twice gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:C2raw}
 C_2=\sum_i\pi_i\left[u_id_i^2+v_{i-1}d_{i-1}^2
       +2u_id_i h_{i+1}-2v_{i-1}d_{i-1}h_{i-1}\right].
\end{equation}
The same identity can be obtained without an analytic eigenvector: solve
a second finite Poisson equation for $k$ and substitute the quadratic
approximate eigenpair
$\lambda(s)=Js+C_2s^2/2$, $r_i(s)=1+sh_i+s^2k_i/2$.
Its tilted-matrix residual vanishes to order two. Multiplication by the
adjugate transfers this vanishing to the determinant, identifying $J,C_2$
with the first two equations in \eqref{eq:cumulantcoeff}. This is the route
used by the formal proof.

\begin{lemma}[Corrected-increment variance]\label{lem:variance}
The second cumulant has the exact quadratic representation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:variance}
  C_2=\sum_i(a_i+b_i)g_i^2.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Stationarity applied to $h^2$ yields the Dirichlet identity
\[
 \sum_i(a_i+b_i)(h_{i+1}-h_i)^2
   =-2\sum_i\pi_i h_iQh_i.
\]
Insert \eqref{eq:poisson} and use $\sum_i\pi_i h_i=0$. The right side becomes
$2\sum_i(a_i h_i-b_i h_{i+1})d_i$. Expanding the square in
\eqref{eq:variance} then gives
\[
 \sum_i(a_i+b_i)d_i^2
 +2\sum_i d_i(a_i h_{i+1}-b_i h_i),
\]
which is \eqref{eq:C2raw} after shifting an index.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Cauchy--Schwarz and the logarithm}
Weighted Cauchy--Schwarz and $\sum_i g_i=1$ give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:CS}
  C_2\sum_i\frac1{a_i+b_i}\ge1.
\end{equation}
For $a>b>0$,
\[
  \log(a/b)\ge\frac{2(a-b)}{a+b}.
\]
Indeed the derivative of $\log y-2(y-1)/(y+1)$ is
$(y-1)^2/[y(y+1)^2]\ge0$ for $y\ge1$, and its value at one is zero.
Apply this with $a_i-b_i=J>0$. Telescoping the stationary factors gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:affinityCS}
 A=\sum_i\log(a_i/b_i)
   \ge2J\sum_i\frac1{a_i+b_i}.
\end{equation}
Combining \eqref{eq:CS} and \eqref{eq:affinityCS} yields
$AC_2\ge2J$, proving \eqref{eq:TUR} because $C_1=J$.

\section{Reduction of the lower bound to exact algebra}\label{sec:lower}

Put $r=1/c\in(0,1)$, $z=r^2$, and $t=\sqrt{p_2}\in(0,1]$.
Each $x_i\le t$, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentbound}
  p_3\le t^3.
\end{equation}
Since $A=2\log((1+r)/(1-r))$, equations \eqref{eq:ratios} and
\eqref{eq:TUR} imply
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentTUR}
  t^2\ge\frac{1-r^2}{2}
       +\frac{r}{\log((1+r)/(1-r))}.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Rational logarithm bound]\label{lem:log}
For $0\le r<1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:logbound}
 \log\frac{1+r}{1-r}
 \le 2r+\frac{2r^3}{3}+\frac{2r^5}{5}
            +\frac{2r^7}{7(1-r^2)}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The difference between the right and left sides is zero at zero. Its
derivative is $4r^8/[7(1-r^2)^2]\ge0$.
\end{proof}

Define
\begin{align}
 D(z)&=105-70z-14z^2-6z^3,\label{eq:D}\\
 R(t,z)&=2D(z)t^2-(1-z)(D(z)+105),\label{eq:R}\\
 F(t,z)&=48t^4-32t^3-24t^2+8+z(24t^2-7).\label{eq:F}
\end{align}
On $[0,1]$ we have $D(z)\ge15$. The right side of
\eqref{eq:logbound} equals $2rD(z)/[105(1-z)]$. Taking reciprocals in
\eqref{eq:momentTUR}, all denominators being positive, proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Rnonnegative}
  R(t,z)\ge0.
\end{equation}
The key scalar statement is the following.

\begin{lemma}[Exact scalar inequality]\label{lem:scalar}
If $0\le t,z\le1$ and $R(t,z)\ge0$, then $F(t,z)\ge0$.
\end{lemma}

Before proving the lemma, observe that it completes the lower-bound
argument. Equation~\eqref{eq:ratios} gives the exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finalLower}
  16z\left(S+\frac1{16}\right)
     =F(t,z)+32(t^3-p_3)\ge0.
\end{equation}
Since $z>0$, this is $S\ge-1/16$.

\section{An exact positivity certificate}\label{sec:certificate}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:scalar}]
At $z=1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Fone}
 F(t,1)=(2t-1)^2(12t^2+4t+1)\ge0.
\end{equation}
Suppose, for a contradiction, that $F(t,z)<0$. Write $B=24t^2-7$.
The affine dependence on $z$ and \eqref{eq:Fone} imply $B>0$. Set
\[
 q=-\frac{48t^4-32t^3-24t^2+8}{B}.
\]
Then $z<q\le1$. Also $B>0$ implies
$t^2>7/24>(27/50)^2$, hence $t\ge27/50$.
Let
\[
 E(z,q)=15+(1-z)(1-q)(20+6(z+q))>0.
\]
Direct polynomial expansion gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:monotonicR}
 D(z)R(t,q)-D(q)R(t,z)
   =(q-z)\bigl[D(z)D(q)+105E(z,q)\bigr]>0.
\end{equation}
Since $D(z),D(q)>0$ and $R(t,z)\ge0$, we conclude that $R(t,q)>0$.
A second exact identity is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Pidentity}
  B^4R(t,q)=2(t-1)P_{14}(t),
\end{equation}
where
\begin{align}\label{eq:P14}
 P_{14}(t)={}&10616832t^{14}-15261696t^{13}-20127744t^{12}\notag\\
 &+20336640t^{11}+53514240t^{10}-47832576t^9\notag\\
 &-29349376t^8+30811392t^7+5745408t^6\notag\\
 &-7539040t^5-671776t^4+645104t^3\notag\\
 &+179568t^2-17623t-17623.
\end{align}
The integer certificates in Appendix~\ref{app:certificate} prove
$P_{14}(t)\ge0$ on $[27/50,1]$. Thus the right side of
\eqref{eq:Pidentity} is nonpositive, because $t\le1$, while its left side
is positive, because $B>0$ and $R(t,q)>0$. This contradiction proves the
lemma.
\end{proof}

For completeness, the certificates cover the three closed intervals
\[
 [27/50,541/1000],\qquad [541/1000,3/5],\qquad [3/5,1].
\]
For an interval $[a/q_0,b/q_0]$ they have the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:integercertificate}
 L\,P_{14}(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{14}c_j(q_0t-a)^j(b-q_0t)^{14-j},
 \qquad L>0,\quad c_j>0.
\end{equation}
All quantities $L,c_j,a,b,q_0$ in the appendix are exact integers. Each
displayed identity is an identity of polynomials, and every basis factor
is nonnegative on its indicated interval. Consequently the certificate
is directly verifiable by expansion and positivity; it is not a numerical
sign test. The identities are literally expanded and checked by the
\Lean{ring} tactic in \Lean{ScalarCertificate.lean}.

One can also reproduce the calculation from the ordinary Bernstein basis.
Write $P_{14}(t)=\sum_{m=0}^{14}p_mt^m$. On an interval $[a,b]$ its standard
Bernstein coefficients are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Bernstein}
 \beta_k=\sum_{j=0}^k\frac{\binom{k}{j}}{\binom{14}{j}}(b-a)^j
             \sum_{m=j}^{14}p_m\binom{m}{j}a^{m-j}.
\end{equation}
They satisfy
$P_{14}(a+(b-a)y)=\sum_k\beta_k\binom{14}{k}y^k(1-y)^{14-k}$.
The exact minimum $\beta_k$ on the three intervals is, respectively,
\begin{gather*}

\section{The upper bound, reversal, and sharpness}\label{sec:upper}

Let
\[
 e_2=\frac{1-p_2}{2},\qquad e_3=\frac{1-3p_2+2p_3}{6}.
\]
These are nonnegative elementary symmetric sums of the probability vector
$x$. Equation~\eqref{eq:ratios} rewrites as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:upperS}
  S=1-3(c^2+1)e_2+12c^2e_2^2-6c^2e_3.
\end{equation}
The nonnegativity of $C_2$ gives $4c^2e_2\le c^2+1$. Multiplication by
$3e_2\ge0$ shows that the positive quadratic term in \eqref{eq:upperS} is
at most $3(c^2+1)e_2$. Thus $S\le1$.

Reversing the ring orientation replaces $C_1,C_3$ by $-C_1,-C_3$, leaving
their ratio unchanged. At the matrix level, if $\sigma$ is the successor
permutation, reversal replaces it by $\sigma^{-1}$ and sets
\[
 u'_i=v_{\sigma^{-1}i},\qquad v'_i=u_{\sigma^{-1}i}.
\]
The untilted generator is unchanged, and $U,V$ are exchanged. This supplies
the case $U<V$ and completes the proof of the two inequalities.

The extremizing family was already identified
in~\cite{Ptaszynski2022}. In an irreversible ring, take one forward rate
$1/(n-1)$ and all others equal to one. At $V=0$, the inverse-rate weights
are
\[
 x_1=\frac12,\qquad x_2=\cdots=x_n=\frac1{2(n-1)},
\]
and the third/first cumulant ratio is $3p_2^2-2p_3$. Direct substitution gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sharp}
 S=-\frac{n^2-8n+8}{16(n-1)^2}\longrightarrow-\frac1{16}.
\end{equation}
For each fixed $n$, sufficiently small positive backward rates approximate
this value. The simple zero eigenvalue and the coefficient formulas vary
continuously with the rates, with $C_1\ne0$ at the irreversible limit.
Taking $n$ large proves sharpness over strictly positive rates. A single
increasingly slow forward stage makes its normalized waiting-time weight
tend to one and hence gives $S\to1$; the same positive-rate approximation
applies. No better dimension-independent constants are possible.

\section{Assessment and limitations}

The result resolves the dimension-independent interval proposed in
\cite{Ptaszynski2022}, within its positive-rate ring model and for the
specified winding observable. Violating the interval therefore excludes
that model for this observable. It does not settle the paper's other
cumulant conjectures or the optimal bounds at each fixed ring size.

The argument does not settle the separate kurtosis, joint-cumulant, or
fixed-size optimization questions. Extending the method to other higher
cumulants remains an open direction.

\section{The small-state conventions}\label{app:small}

\subsection{Two states with two distinguished channels}
For $n=2$, the two oppositely traversed edges become parallel transition
channels. They must remain distinguished when counting. Put
\[
  U=u_0u_1,\quad V=v_0v_1,\quad
  a=u_0+u_1+v_0+v_1,
\]
with all four rates positive and $U\ne V$. The correct tilted matrix is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:twoMatrix}
 z\Id-W_s=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 z+u_0+v_1&-(u_1+v_0e^{-s})\\
 -(u_0e^s+v_1)&z+u_1+v_0
 \end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Only the marked channel is tilted; multiplying a whole combined
off-diagonal entry by a counting factor would describe a different
observable. Direct expansion gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:twoDet}
 \det(z\Id-W_s)=z^2+az-U(e^s-1)-V(e^{-s}-1).
\end{equation}
The same characteristic differentiation therefore yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:twoS}
  S=1-\frac{6(U+V)}{a^2}+\frac{12(U-V)^2}{a^4}.
\end{equation}
Let $\alpha=\sqrt U$, $\beta=\sqrt V$, and set
\[
 r=\frac{\alpha-\beta}{\alpha+\beta},\qquad
 k=\frac{4(\alpha+\beta)^2}{a^2}.
\]
Arithmetic--geometric mean gives $0<k\le1$, and $r^2\le1$. Equation
\eqref{eq:twoS} becomes
\[
 S=1-\frac{3k}{4}\bigl(1+r^2(1-k)\bigr),\qquad
 S-\frac14=\frac34(1-k)(1-kr^2).
\]
Both factors in the latter expression are nonnegative, and the former
expression is at most one. Hence the stronger two-state interval is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:twoBound}
  \frac14\le\frac{C_3}{C_1}\le1.
\end{equation}
The actual two-channel determinant, local branch existence, and this
derivative interval are the subject of \Lean{TwoStateWinding.lean}.

\subsection{One state with counted events}
In the conventional one-state counted-event model, forward and reverse
events form independent Poisson counts with positive rates $u,v$. Their
difference has cumulant generating function per unit time
\[
  \lambda(s)=u(e^s-1)+v(e^{-s}-1).
\]
Thus $\lambda'(0)=\lambda'''(0)=u-v$. If $u\ne v$, then
\[
  \frac{C_3}{C_1}=1.
\]
The explicit derivative identity is formalized in
\Lean{PoissonWinding.lean}. These small-state calculations are conventional
boundary cases and are not additional novelty claims.
