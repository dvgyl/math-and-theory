\subsection{Matrix theory}

\subsubsection{Real positive-definite counterexamples to extended q-permanent monotonicity}

For every real parameter $q>1$, some real symmetric positive-definite matrix has a negative $q$-permanent derivative at $q$. An explicit matrix of order five decreases from $q=14$ to $q=15$. The construction also gives correlation matrices with no zero entries and integer-entry examples. The dimension can depend on $q$. The real-symmetric question on $[-1,1]$ remains outside the result.

\par\smallskip

\noindent\textbf{Scope:} Counterexample and all-parameter extension.

\subsubsection{Gyires's permanent inequality for structured matrices}

Every nonnegative real circulant matrix $A$ of order five satisfies $24\operatorname{per}(AA^T)\leq625\operatorname{per}(A)^2$. Equality holds exactly for constant matrices. This proves the order-five circulant case of Gyires\textquotesingle s ratio inequality. A bound in sorted-gap coordinates has sharp coefficient $484$. The conjecture for arbitrary doubly stochastic matrices remains open.

\par\smallskip

\noindent\textbf{Scope:} Structured matrix inequalities and related polynomial bounds. The principal order-five and order-six claims, the all-order permutation-polynomial and four-block theorems, and the exceptional-vector theorem have Lean proofs.

\noindent\href{https://github.com/dvgyl/math-and-theory/blob/main/papers/mathematics/gyires/paper.pdf}{Manuscript}\quad\href{https://github.com/dvgyl/math-and-theory/blob/main/lean/docs/mathematics/gyires.md}{Formal statement and prior work}.

\subsubsection*{Broader arguments in the same preprint}

The manuscript also proves results for circulants of orders six and seven, with sharp sorted-gap margins. It treats $aI+bQ+cQ^2$ in every dimension, where $Q$ is any permutation matrix and the coefficients are nonnegative. Further results cover four-block doubly stochastic matrices with unequal block sizes and boundary entries, matrices with one exceptional column or row, and every rank-at-most-two doubly stochastic matrix through order fifteen.

The supporting sections prove structural, scalar, deletion, balancing, local polynomial, and configuration-model results. Exact counterexamples identify the limits of auxiliary assertions. The \href{https://github.com/dvgyl/math-and-theory/blob/main/papers/mathematics/gyires/research-notes/complete-support/README.md}{complete support record} gives the source map and exact reproduction commands.

Separate Lean packages prove the principal order-six and all-order permutation-polynomial theorems. The all-order rank-two problem and the unrestricted Gyires conjecture remain unproved here.

\subsubsection{A polygon enclosure fails for sums of normal matrices}

\subsubsection{An unbounded obstruction to projection AM--GM in Loewner order}

Three rank-one orthogonal projections in dimension three violate the symmetrized-product Loewner AM--GM bound. No finite real multiplier repairs it, even with a positive-definite mean. The examples do not satisfy the additional nonsingular block structure of the motivating coordinate-descent application.

\noindent\textbf{Formal scope:} Lean verifies a rational three-projection matrix obstruction and a parameterized obstruction for every real multiplier in dimension three; rank-one, positive-definite mean, rational all-multiplier choices, and the standard minimal-factor observation have complete conventional proofs.

\subsubsection{Matrix-pencil rank distance as an explicit completion problem}

Over every infinite field, two matrix pencils have strict-equivalence classes at normal-rank distance at most $x$ exactly when an explicit dilation of the first is a constant full-rank compression of the dilation of the second plus $x$ scalar blocks. With a cited block minimal bases theorem this proves Conjecture~1 of Dodig and Sto\v{s}i\'c: the bounded rank perturbation problem for matrix pencils is a completion problem. The explicit theorem is formalized in Lean~4.35.0-rc3.

\href{https://github.com/dvgyl/math-and-theory/blob/main/papers/mathematics/matrix-pencil-completion/manuscript.pdf}{Manuscript}\quad\href{https://github.com/dvgyl/math-and-theory/blob/main/papers/mathematics/matrix-pencil-completion/README.md}{Proof and reproduction}.

\subsubsection{Counterexamples to the Kronecker-square lower bound for nonnegative rank}

For every $k\ge0$ an explicit rational nonnegative matrix $C$ of order $k+11$, rank $k+10$ and nonnegative rank $k+11$ satisfies $\operatorname{rank}_+(C\otimes C)\le(k+10)^2+9$. This disproves the conjecture of Vandaele, Gillis, Glineur and Tuyttens that $\operatorname{rank}_+(M\otimes M)\ge\operatorname{rank}_+(M)\operatorname{rank}(M)$. The family is formalized in Lean~4.35.0-rc3.

\subsubsection{An entropy increase for a structurally disassortative DomiRank graph}

Its entropy nevertheless increases by more than $1/10000$ in natural-logarithm units. This refutes the source\textquotesingle s assertion for every positive-score interval.

\subsubsection{Domination sequences of powers of paths and cycles}

The domination counts of every natural power of a path or cycle form an ultra-log-concave sequence. Dividing each count by its binomial coefficient gives the normalized log-concavity inequality. The proof includes all natural graph orders and powers. It does not assert the same property for arbitrary graphs or real-rootedness of their domination polynomials.

\subsubsection{The upper saturation spectrum of Berge triangles in triple systems}

For every $n\ge8$, a Berge-triangle-free triple system on $n$ vertices with more than $\lfloor (n-1)^2/8\rfloor$ edges is a spanning subsystem of a paired product $F(n,p)$. Saturated systems above this threshold are therefore paired products, and the Berge-triangle saturation spectrum there consists exactly of the sizes $p(n-2p)$ up to $\lfloor n^2/8\rfloor$, with $n/4-O(\sqrt n)$ missing sizes. The threshold is sharp: a saturated system with exactly $\lfloor (n-1)^2/8\rfloor$ edges that is not a paired product exists for every $n\ge8$. Lean~4.35.0 proves that paired products are Berge-triangle-free and saturated, proves the construction for every $n\ge8$ and the arithmetic of the window, and derives the spectrum statements from the containment theorem, which has a conventional proof and exact checks.

\noindent\href{https://github.com/dvgyl/math-and-theory/blob/main/papers/mathematics/berge-triangle-spectrum/manuscript.pdf}{Manuscript}\quad\href{https://github.com/dvgyl/math-and-theory/blob/main/papers/mathematics/berge-triangle-spectrum/README.md}{Proof and reproduction}.

\subsubsection{Exact three-uniform Berge-path saturation numbers and a counterexample to a conjectured formula}

Two disjoint loose $8$-cycles with two isolated vertices form a Berge-$P_{10}$-saturated three-uniform hypergraph on $34$ vertices with $16$ hyperedges, one below the value conjectured by English, Graber, Kirkpatrick, Methuku and Sullivan. For all $m\ge10$ and $n\ge b=2a_m+1$ the saturation number equals the conjectured value except at the orders $b<n<2b$ with $n\equiv2\pmod{2(m-2)}$, where it is one less and the extremal hypergraph is unique. Lean~4.35.0 verifies the counterexample family and the failure of the conjecture for every $m\ge10$; the lower bound is conventional.

\noindent\href{https://github.com/dvgyl/math-and-theory/blob/main/papers/mathematics/berge-path-saturation/manuscript.pdf}{Manuscript}\quad\href{https://github.com/dvgyl/math-and-theory/blob/main/papers/mathematics/berge-path-saturation/README.md}{Proof and reproduction}.

\subsubsection{Three-uniform Berge-$C_4$ saturation: linear bounds with coefficients $5/8$ and $3/4$, the cone spectrum and exact small values}

For the three-uniform Berge-$C_4$ saturation number $s(n)$, in which any absent triple may be added, $\max(\lceil(n-1)/2\rceil,\lceil(5n-3623)/8\rceil,\lceil(5n+89)/8-\sqrt{270n+1891}/4\rceil)\le s(n)\le\lceil(3n-9)/4\rceil$ for every $n\ge9$, so the linear coefficient lies between $5/8$ and $3/4$; the previous explicit upper bound was $n-2$. Saturated cones are classified, with sizes exactly the integers from $\lceil(3n-5)/4\rceil$ to $n-2$ and $n-1$ when $n\equiv1\pmod3$, and exhaustive certificates give $s(n)$ for $n\le13$. Lean~4.35.0 verifies the upper construction for every $n\ge9$ and the values for $n\le5$; the lower bound and the cone results are conventional.

\subsubsection{Three-uniform Berge-$C_5$ saturation: a two-thirds upper bound, the cone spectrum and exact small values}

For every $n\ge47$ every integer from $\lceil(2n-8)/3\rceil$ to $n-2$ is the size of a three-uniform Berge-$C_5$-saturated hypergraph on $n$ vertices, in which any absent triple may be added, so $s(n)=\operatorname{sat}_3(n,\text{Berge-}C_5)\le\lceil(2n-8)/3\rceil$; the previous explicit upper bound had leading coefficient $4/3$. No lower bound beyond small orders is proved. Saturated cones are classified, with sizes exactly the integers from $\lceil6(n-1)/7\rceil$ to $n-2$ and $n-1$ when $4\mid n-1$ for $n\ge8$; saturated loose expansions of bipartite graphs with a degree-two side are characterised; and exhaustive certificates give $s(n)$ for $n\le11$. Lean~4.35.0 verifies every size from $\lceil2(n-1)/3\rceil$ to $n-2$ for $n\ge14$ and the values for $n\le7$; the two lowest endpoints, the cone classification and the expansion theorems are conventional.

\subsection{Inequalities and real analysis}

\subsubsection{Diagonal concavity does not imply concavity for sums of elementary symmetric polynomials}

For $M>0$, let $f_M=1+M\sigma_1+\sigma_2+M\sigma_3+\sigma_4$ in four variables, where $\sigma_j$ is the elementary symmetric polynomial of degree $j$. The fourth root $f_M^{1/4}$ is concave on the positive diagonal exactly when $M\geq1$. It is concave on the full positive orthant exactly when $M=1$. Thus every $M>1$ disproves Lachaume\textquotesingle s criterion with strictly positive coefficients.

\par\smallskip

\noindent\textbf{Scope:} Counterexample and complete parameter classification.

\par\smallskip

\noindent\href{https://github.com/dvgyl/math-and-theory/blob/main/papers/mathematics/diagonal-concavity/paper.pdf}{Manuscript}\quad\href{https://github.com/dvgyl/math-and-theory/blob/main/lean/docs/mathematics/diagonal-concavity.md}{Formal statement and prior work}.

\subsubsection{Alternating Motzkin sums}

The principal root bound and bounded-forcing companion form one sequence family.

\paragraph{Sharp root log-concavity of alternating Motzkin sums} Let $T_n=\sum_{j=0}^n(-1)^{n-j}M_j$, where $M_j$ is the Motzkin number. The sequence $T_n^{1/n}$ is strictly log-concave on its tail starting at five. The inequality reverses at center five, so no earlier tail is log-concave. This strengthens Zhao\textquotesingle s proposed tail starting at nine. The source recurrence and finite sum are linked in Lean.

\noindent\textbf{Scope:} Conjecture resolution with an optimal threshold.

\subsubsection{Counterexamples to a late-iterate ordering conjecture for generalized Hofstadter functions}

For every $k\geq4$, some input $n$ satisfies $F_k^{\circ(2k+1)}(n)>F_{k+1}^{\circ(2k+2)}(n)$. Here the superscript denotes repeated composition. \subsubsection{Order-polynomial positivity for width-two posets with a chain of at most five elements}

If a finite poset $P$ is the union of two chains $A$ and $B$ with $|A|\le5$, the chain $B$ being arbitrarily long and the comparabilities between the chains arbitrary, then $|P|!\,\Omega_P(t)$ has nonnegative coefficients, a case of Conjecture~7.1 of Ferroni, Morales and Panova; moreover $[t]\,\Omega_P(t)\ge(a-1)!\,(b-1)!\,m/(a+b)!$, where $m$ counts the elements of $B$ comparable with some element of $A$. On each of $64$ chambers the normalized linear coefficient is a polynomial of degree at most $a$ in the gaps between boundaries, certified nonnegative by exact square certificates, and the ideal--filter reduction of Ferroni, Morales and Panova lifts it to all coefficients. Lean~4.35.0 checks the certificates for $|A|\le4$; the link with the order polynomial, the size-five chambers and the reduction are conventional or checked by an independent program. Public partial results on ordinal sums of one- and two-element antichains and on single cross-link factors cover different classes.

\subsection{Finite geometry}

\subsubsection{Blocking sets of PG(2,13) invariant under a fixed-point-free Singer subgroup meet some line in five points}

Let $H$ be the subgroup of order three of a Singer cycle group of $\mathrm{PG}(2,13)$; it fixes no point. Every $H$-invariant blocking set meets some line in at least five points, and the $21$-point blocking set of Blokhuis, Brouwer and Wilbrink attains five, so five is the least possible largest line intersection of an $H$-invariant blocking set; the same holds for every fixed-point-free collineation of order three. This settles a symmetric case of the plane of order $13$ in Erd\H{o}s Problem~1159. Lean proves the theorem for the specified subgroup, including an exhaustive search evaluated in the kernel with a soundness proof.

\noindent\href{https://github.com/dvgyl/math-and-theory/blob/main/papers/mathematics/singer-orbit-blocking/manuscript.pdf}{Manuscript}\quad\href{https://github.com/dvgyl/math-and-theory/blob/main/papers/mathematics/singer-orbit-blocking/README.md}{Proof and reproduction}.

\subsection{Quadratic forms and number theory}

\subsubsection{A rank bound for represented totally positive unit squareclasses}

A totally positive definite quadratic lattice of rank $n$ over a totally real algebraic field, with integral norms but not necessarily classical, represents at most $n$ classes of totally positive units modulo squares of units. This answers Question~3.4 of Daans, Kala, Man, Widmer and Yatsyna negatively; the bound is a short consequence of standard facts on finite reflection groups. The theorem is formalized in Lean~4.19.0 for all algebraic totally real fields.

\subsection{Valuation theory}

\subsubsection{Depth-one Artin--Schreier defect towers are composita}

Over a perfect henselian subfield $K$ of $k((y^{\mathbb Q}))$ containing $k(y)$ and without finite accumulation points, a nontrivial finite Artin--Schreier tower of depth one is a compositum of Artin--Schreier extensions of $K$. This is the converse conjectured by Nart and Novacoski. Both implications are formalized in Lean, with depth one defined by Mac Lane--Vaquié chains in a Lean~4.35 project and expressed through Okutsu sequences in a Lean~4.19 project.

\noindent\href{https://github.com/dvgyl/math-and-theory/blob/main/papers/mathematics/artin-schreier-depth/manuscript.pdf}{Manuscript}\quad\href{https://github.com/dvgyl/math-and-theory/blob/main/papers/mathematics/artin-schreier-depth/README.md}{Proof and reproduction}.

\subsection{Discrete analysis, tilings, probability and entropy}

\subsubsection{The sharp Fourier bound at density one eighth}

For every indicator of mean $1/8$ on a finite uniform Boolean cube, the level-one Fourier weight is at most $3/64$. A codimension-three subcube attains this bound in every dimension at least three. The proof also gives the equivalent sharp average Hamming-distance bound. It uses exact rational polynomial certificates and a dimension-independent moment identity. Fang and Huang proved this bound earlier, together with the equality cases; no originality or priority is claimed for it.

\noindent\href{https://github.com/dvgyl/math-and-theory/blob/main/papers/mathematics/sharp-eighth/manuscript.pdf}{Manuscript}\quad\href{https://github.com/dvgyl/math-and-theory/blob/main/papers/mathematics/sharp-eighth/README.md}{Formal proof and reproduction}.

\subsubsection{A 64-point spectral tile in $\mathbb{F}_2^9$ with no periodic complement}

An explicit 64-point subset $E$ of $\mathbb{F}_2^9$ is spectral and tiles by translation, but none of its tiling complements is periodic: they are exactly the 512 translates of one aperiodic eight-point set. Hence no linear subspace complements $E$, and $\mathbb{F}_2^9$ fails the periodic tiling property. This answers negatively the subspace-complement part of a question of Ferguson and Iosevich; whether every spectral 64-point subset of $\mathbb{F}_2^9$ tiles remains open. The main theorem and the classification of complements are formalized in Lean~4.19.0.

\subsubsection{The small-coefficient upper half of the Keller--Klein conjecture}

For a Rademacher sum $X=\sum_i v_i\varepsilon_i$ with $\sum_i v_i^2=1$ and every $|v_i|\le a\le 7/100$, $\Pr(|X|\le x)\le 2\Phi(x)+2\Phi(a)-2$ for all $x\ge0$. This closes the range of the Keller--Klein conjecture left open by Ivanisvili's computer-assisted manuscript of 21 October 2026; together with his results it implies the full conjecture, conditionally on those unrefereed results. The theorem is formalized in Lean~4.19.0.

\subsubsection{A quartic entropy deficit for random rankings}

Let $P$ be a probability distribution on the complete rankings of $n$ labels. If every pairwise comparison has probability at distance at least $\varepsilon>0$ from $1/2$, then
\[
H(P)\le (1-\varepsilon^4/144)\log(n!).
\]
The formal theorem includes every finite size, including zero and one. It assumes neither independent comparisons nor transitive preferred directions. It covers both ranking lists and position-valued permutations.

\subsection{Optimization}

\subsubsection{A narrow interval for the universal multiplier step of two-block ADMM}

Two-block ADMM with relaxation only in the multiplier update converges for every constant step $0<\tau\le 377359/200000=1.886795$ on finite-dimensional convex problems whose proximal subproblems attain their minima and which have a saddle point. Fixed compact polytopes in $\mathbb R^{128}$, with zero objectives, admit a nonconstant periodic orbit for every $\tau\ge 38847831300265103262189027/20588352022933119368900773\approx1.8868840$. The universal threshold therefore lies in an interval of width below $0.00009$; its exact value is open. For scalar problems the universal range is exactly $0<\tau<2$. Lean~4.19.0 formalizes the convergence theorem and its affine-constraint versions for $0<\tau\le943/500$, the two finite-memory certificates up to $943/500$ and an earlier periodic orbit valid for $\tau\ge1.8868954$; the extension to $377359/200000$ and the present orbit rest on exact rational certificates and conventional proofs, and the scalar theorem is conventional.

\subsubsection{Escaping Nesterov flows and iterates below critical damping}

For the Nesterov differential equation $X''+(r/t)X'+\nabla F(X)=0$ with zero initial velocity, and for the Nesterov iteration with momentum $k/(k+r)$, universal boundedness over convex objectives with a $1$-Lipschitz gradient and an attained minimum holds on the Hilbert space $\ell^2$ exactly when $r\ge3$. An explicit convex planar potential has an escaping trajectory for every $0<r\le2$, and its trajectories from the same start are bounded for $r>2$. The finite-dimensional range $2<r<3$ remains open. The Hilbert-space and discrete thresholds and the planar escape for $1<r\le2$ are formalized in Lean~4.19.0.

\subsubsection{Periodic obstructions to small-extrapolation Chambolle--Pock}

For the unmodified Chambolle--Pock method, every step product $\kappa=\tau\sigma\|L\|^2>2$ admits fixed compact polytopes in $\mathbb R^4$ whose iteration is periodic and nonconvergent for all sufficiently small extrapolation $\theta\ge0$. Any universal sufficient condition $\kappa<c(\theta)$ therefore has $\limsup_{\theta\downarrow0}c(\theta)\le2$, below the quadratic bound $4/(1+2\theta)$. An exact universal three-cycle threshold and a rational seven-cycle are also proved. The constructions are formalized in Lean~4.19.0; the obstruction intervals in $\theta$ are very small.

\subsection{Boolean functions and decision trees}

\subsubsection{Logarithmic influence bounds and variance counterexamples for Boolean decision-tree covariance}

Let $T$ be a finite Boolean decision tree under uniform independent bits. No coordinate repeats on a root-to-leaf path. Let $k_i(T)$ count all query nodes labelled $i$, and let $I_i(f)$ be the probability that flipping coordinate $i$ changes the output. The weighted sum of absolute child covariances satisfies
\[
\sum_v 2^{-d(v)}\left|\operatorname{Cov}(f_{v,+},f_{v,-})\right|
\leq \sum_i I_i(f)\log_2\max\{1,k_i(T)\}.
\]
Here $d(v)$ is the node depth, and the two child functions use the same input cube. The read number counts nodes in the entire tree, not queries along one path.

For a read-$k$ tree, the bound is $I(f)\log_2 k$. The dummy-prefix construction of Wan, Wright, and Wu attains equality when $k$ is a power of two. The logarithmic potential is due to Shalev. A four-variable counterexample has covariance $5/4$ and variance $3/4$. A nine-variable counterexample has covariance $4863/4096$ and variance $3007/4096$, with every query relevant. Both refute the coefficient-one logarithmic variance proposal for a given tree.

\par\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Scope:} Covariance inequalities, an equality family, and exact finite witnesses. No Fourier-entropy theorem is formalized.

\noindent\href{https://github.com/dvgyl/math-and-theory/blob/main/papers/theoretical-computer-science/readk-covariance/docs/manuscript.pdf}{Manuscript}\quad\href{https://github.com/dvgyl/math-and-theory/blob/main/papers/theoretical-computer-science/readk-covariance/docs/related-work.md}{Prior work}\quad\href{https://github.com/dvgyl/math-and-theory/blob/main/papers/theoretical-computer-science/readk-covariance/README.md}{Formal proof and reproduction}.

\subsection{Stochastic thermodynamics}

\subsubsection{A three-state thermal counterexample to a heat-current kurtosis conjecture}

A three-state rate model with two positive-temperature baths satisfies local detailed balance. Every total off-diagonal rate is positive. The tilted generator records heat transferred to the cold bath. Its dominant eigenvalue branch is four times continuously differentiable near zero and has a larger real part than every distinct complex eigenvalue. The exact derivative ratio is
\[
\frac{C_4}{C_2}=-\frac{3470947982622836}{176627429595846691}<0,
\]
where $C_n$ is the $n$th derivative at zero. This refutes Eq.~(19) of Ptaszy\'nski, \emph{Physical Review E} \textbf{106}, 024119 (2022), in the paper's finite thermal spectral formulation. The third derivative is positive, so the witness does not refute the separate skewness conjecture.

\par\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Scope:} Finite rate matrices, local detailed balance, complex eigenvalues, and derivatives of a local dominant branch. The stochastic long-time theorem that identifies the branch with a cumulant generating function is not formalized. No minimal-state or empirical validation claim is made.
